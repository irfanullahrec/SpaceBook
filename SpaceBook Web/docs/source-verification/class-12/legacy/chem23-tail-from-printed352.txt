Green Chemistry
The green chemistry or sustainable chemistry is defined as: &quot;the invention, design and application of chemical products and processes to reduce or eliminate the use or generation of substances hazardous to humans, animals, plants, and the
environment:
The aim of the green chemistry is to reduce chemical related impact on human health and virtually eliminate contaminants of the environment through dedicated sustainable prevention programs. Green chemistry searches for alternative, environmental friendly reaction media and at the same time strive to increase reaction rate and lower reaction temperature.
Green Chemistry is based on 12 principles proposed by Anastas and Warner. These 12 principles of green chemistry are considered fundamentals to contribute to sustainable development. The principles comprise of instructions to implement new chemical products, new synthesis and new processes as given below:
1. Atom Economy: Design syntheses so that the final product contains the maximum proportion of the starting material. Waste few or no atoms of the reactant i.e. to maximize the incorporation of all materials, used in the process, into the final product.

Use Catalysts: Minimize waste by using catalytic reactions. Catalysts are effective in small amounts and can carry out a single reaction many times. Reactions at low temperature and pressure are possible due to use of new/improved catalysts.
10. Design Chemicals and Products to Degrade after use: Chemical products have to be designed in such a way that, at the end of their life span, disintegrate into nontoxic degradation products more readily.
