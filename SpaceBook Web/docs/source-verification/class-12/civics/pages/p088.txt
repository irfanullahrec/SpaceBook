The Arab world did not accept the existence of Israel, and in the Arab–Israeli war of 1948-49 the Arabs were defeated. Israel occupied the new city of Jerusalem and half the region of Palestine. Jordan occupied the old Jerusalem and the West Bank of the River Jordan, while Egypt occupied the Gaza Strip. In the year 1967 a six-day war took place between the Arabs and Israel, in which Israel occupied the West Bank of the River Jordan, the Gaza Strip and East Jerusalem. Because of this the Palestinians were scattered. About ten lakh became citizens of Israel, twenty-nine lakh are in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, and about fifty lakh took refuge in Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and other Arab countries. During this period small battles kept taking place between Palestinian mujahideen and Israelis. Indescribable cruelty was inflicted on the Palestinian people, and Western countries kept supporting Israel. On 13 September 1993, at Oslo, an agreement was made between the head of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO), Yasser Arafat, and the Israeli government. In some areas of the Gaza Strip and the West Bank a Palestinian government was established in the name of the Palestinian National Authority. But in exchange for this the PLO also recognised Israel, with which the Palestinian mujahideen became angry, and under the leadership of the organisation named Hamas they pledged an armed struggle to eliminate Israel and establish a Palestinian state. Later, talks continued between the PLO and Israel until 2010 for a permanent solution, but no satisfactory result came out, and in this way the future of the Oslo agreement is also in danger.
