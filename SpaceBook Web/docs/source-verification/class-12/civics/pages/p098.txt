resolution of international problems, conciliation too is an effective path. Through conciliation, disagreement between opposing groups or people ends and a friendly environment is established. Resolving a conflict between people, groups or states and making them friends again is called conciliation. In the year 1948 the white rulers came to power in South Africa. In South Africa racism, racial discrimination and inequality existed as a custom. After 1948 racial discrimination was given legal protection. A regular government programme was started to establish the supremacy of the whites. The government of the whites declared economic exploitation, political supremacy and social privilege lawful. Unequal education, special protection for the whites, discrimination in employment and residential separation were ensured. In the year 1950 a law was passed fixing separate residential areas for the four basic racial groups, i.e. white, black, coloured (neither black nor white) and Asian people. Access of blacks to white areas and mutual marriage of both were declared unlawful. Blacks were arrested for moving about in cities for more than 72 hours without permission. Lakhs of people were arrested, and grave violations of human rights were committed. To investigate the cruelties committed on the basis of the racial discrimination policy of the previous government, the government of Nelson Mandela established in April 1995 the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. In 1996 this seventeen-member commission started work under the leadership of Bishop Desmond Tutu. In the commission all generations were given representation. The aim of the commission was to hear the people who had been victims of oppression and racism from 1960 to 1994. The commission also thought about pardoning those people who had confessed to committing excesses on a racial basis. The aim of the commission was to redress the oppressed and to block the path of such events occurring in future, and as a kind of payment to those people who had become victims of racial discrimination. Those who adopted truthfulness and those who confessed were shown to the people directly. The commission obtained innumerable solid testimonies of unjust, unequal and racial discrimination against the previous rulers. The commission heard the stories of fifteen thousand oppressed, and in 1998 presented its report.
