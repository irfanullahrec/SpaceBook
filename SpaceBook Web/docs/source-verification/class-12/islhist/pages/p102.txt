After the death of the Fatimid caliph Salah al-Din became the ruler of Egypt and began the Abbasid khutbah. He kept working in the capacity of viceroy of Nur al-Din Zangi. When Nur al-Din Zangi passed away, he included the regions of Hijaz and Yemen too in his sultanate. The successors of Nur al-Din Zangi too accepted the patronage of Salah al-Din. Salah al-Din turned towards the Christians. He presented some conditions, which the Christians rejected. Salah al-Din besieged the city and at the same time made a firm intention of taking revenge for the general massacre of the Muslims. A few days later the people of the city laid down their arms and requested mercy. Salah al-Din's heart softened; he gave up the intention of taking bloody revenge. He gave the crusader army forty days' respite. He paid the ransom of ten thousand soldiers from his own pocket. In 1187 CE Bayt al-Maqdis came again into the possession of the Muslims and remained with them for centuries.
