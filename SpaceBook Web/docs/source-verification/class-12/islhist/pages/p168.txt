Civil war was at its height. In these circumstances the Sultan established a strong Islamic state in Andalus once again. Peace and order was established in the country. Abd al-Rahman's income from land revenue was fifty-four lakh eighty thousand dinars, and from other sources sixty lakh sixty-five thousand dinars. All this sum was devoted to welfare and betterment. The other expenses were met from the kharaj of the Christian states. The Sultan was also very fond of buildings. The mosque of Cordoba and Qasr al-Zahra' are accepted even today as the best specimens of the art of architecture. The length of the mosque of Cordoba was approximately five hundred feet. In it there were approximately one thousand four hundred and seventeen pillars. The Caliph, having the old minarets pulled down, had a new 160-foot-high minaret built. In it there were two staircases. In each staircase there were 107 steps. Abd al-Rahman had a magnificent palace built at a distance of approximately six and a half kilometres from Cordoba at the foot of Jabal al-'Arus. He named it al-Zahra' after his slave-girl. This building was called Madinat al-Zahra'. This was not a palace but appeared to be a city. It had fifteen thousand doors. Its length was four miles and breadth three miles. Abd al-Rahman was a great appreciator of the 'ulama'. In his court the 'ulama' were present at all times. The awe and majesty of his court was famous throughout Europe. Great rulers could not speak before him. His justice and fairness were famous.
