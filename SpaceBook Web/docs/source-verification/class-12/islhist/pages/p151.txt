This was the reason that the Berber chief of Africa supported Abd al-Rahman fully. With their support Abd al-Rahman succeeded in obtaining power in Andalus. Mansur, in spite of failure, spread a net of conspiracies in Spain. The representatives of the Abbasids kept inciting the Berbers and other tribes against Abd al-Rahman. Besides the Muslims the Christians too kept conspiring against Abd al-Rahman. They had established a small state in Asturias in Jabal al-Barakat. Abd al-Rahman remained entangled with the rebel Muslim chiefs; during this time the Christians strengthened their state. The boundaries of this state kept increasing. At last they invited Yusuf's son Abu al-Aswad, son-in-law Abd al-Rahman bin Habib and Sulayman, the governor of Barcelona, to invite Charlemagne, ruler of France, to attack Spain. With Sulayman's help Charlemagne made a full attack on Spain, but because of internal revolts in his own country he had to return unsuccessful. Later Charlemagne made peace with Abd al-Rahman.
