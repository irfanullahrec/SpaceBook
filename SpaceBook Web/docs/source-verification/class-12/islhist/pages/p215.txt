The capital of Islamic Spain, Cordoba, was the most developed and civilised city of Europe. It consisted of one lakh thirteen thousand houses. In it there were twenty-one suburban quarters. Seventy libraries, hundreds of shops and mosques existed. It was a city of international fame. Paved roads and highways were spread throughout the country. For lighting the roads at night excellent arrangements existed. History is witness that the roads of London were not lit even seven hundred years later. On the roads of Paris too, after rain, mud formed, and as soon as people came out of the house they got stuck in the mud. These are the facts which Christian historians themselves have stated. Islamic Spain was counted among the richest and most populous countries of the world. Travellers came here from every corner of the world. According to one historian, "in the country there were eighty municipalities of the first class and three hundred municipalities of the second class." The income of Islamic Spain was more than the income of the whole of Europe. The population of Cordoba and Seville was five lakh each, and of Granada four and a quarter lakh. The population of Malaga was three lakh, of Valencia two and a half lakh, and of Toledo two lakh. Cordoba also had political superiority in Europe. The whole of Europe was politically under the influence of Cordoba. All important decisions were made in Cordoba. According to another historian, "in the big cities, especially Cordoba, the standard of living of men and women was so high that people coming to Andalus from outside, of whatever rank they might be, felt a great sense of inferiority in meeting ordinary citizens. While speaking they had to take a hundred precautions. So much so that in the bazaars and markets, to observe the manners of shopping, acquaintance was first obtained.
