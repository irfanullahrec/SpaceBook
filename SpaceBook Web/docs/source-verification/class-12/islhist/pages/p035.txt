Baghdad became the cradle of knowledge and literature. Harun was an intelligent and wakeful ruler. He reorganised the structure of the caliphate afresh. He removed shortcomings from every department. Harun was always mindful of the hardships of the public. At night, changing his appearance, he used to wander in the streets of Baghdad so that he could find out the circumstances of the public in person. The caliphate was apparently running according to the Islamic Shari'ah, but practically the amirs and viziers did not care about it. Harun, removing these shortcomings, enforced the Islamic Shari'ah practically. Un-Islamic laws were abolished. In this era Baghdad was considered the most advanced and civilised city of the world. Seeing the magnificent palaces and attractive buildings, travellers would be left astonished. The Qasr al-Khilafah was the best example of the art of building. On both banks of the Tigris were beautiful gardens. Grand mosques too were built. Baghdad was a very big centre commercially too. Trade took place by land and sea routes. Trade caravans from India, China, Syria and Africa kept coming. The best arrangements existed for their lodging. The highways were safe. There was no name or trace of highway robbery. In Harun's era some revolts too took place, but they were brought under control. The series of conquests continued. From this the national income increased considerably. Reorganising the Bayt al-Mal, hard-working and honest people were recruited. Harun had the "Kitab al-Kharaj" written by the famous scholar of religion Imam Abu Yusuf. Harun was a great lover of knowledge and art. He greatly valued scholars. Hundreds of thousands of dirhams were spent on them. Scholars from all over the world came and settled in Baghdad.
