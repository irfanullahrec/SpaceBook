But his son Muhammad bin Ali organised the Abbasid movement well. He was a capable and intelligent person. He spread the da'is (missionaries) to every corner of the country, and got the series of allegiance begun in favour of the Ahl al-Bayt against the Banu Umayyah. However, he did not take the name of any particular imam, so that the Shi'an of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) might remain with them. In the era of Walid II the public had become fed up with the oppressions and excesses of the Banu Umayyah and wanted deliverance from them; for this very reason people joined the Abbasid movement. In this very era civil war had begun. From this too the courage of the da'is and naqibs (leaders) increased, and they began to propagate the Abbasid movement openly. During this very time Abu Muslim Khurasani joined this movement. He changed the Abbasid movement into a political force. After the death of Muhammad bin Ali, Ibrahim appointed him the chief of all the da'is and naqibs. Abu Muslim took this movement to every house and kept obtaining the cooperation of people. When the Abbasid movement became strong in Khurasan, Abu Muslim took possession of Khurasan. In this way conquests kept increasing day by day.
