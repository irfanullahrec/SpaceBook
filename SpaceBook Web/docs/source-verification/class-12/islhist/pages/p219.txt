For the salaries and stipends of scholars large properties were endowed. The regret is that the civilised people of the modern era changed this magnificent university into a church (Christian place of worship). The few remaining minarets of this Jami'ah even now tell the stories of their past. Al-Hakam II had locked up the treasures of the sciences in his libraries. His agents kept roaming the whole world in search of books. According to one report al-Hakam had four lakh books. He himself wrote the margins of the manuscripts. Through this the value of these books increased further. According to Dozy, "almost every individual of Andalus knew how to read and write." || … ختیځو علماوو ته لومړیتوب ورکول کېده؛ د عالمانو د معاشونو او وظیفو لپاره لوی لوی جایدادونه وقف وو. افسوس په دې دی چې د نوې دورې متمدنو خلکو دا لوی پوهنتون په کلیسا (عیسوي عبادتځای) بدل کړ؛ د دې جامعې پاتې څو منارونه اوس هم د خپل تېر داستانونه بیانوي. الحکم دویم د علومو خزانې په خپلو کتابتونونو کې خوندي کړې وې؛ کارکوونکي یې د کتابونو په لټه کې ټوله نړۍ ګرځېدل؛ د یوه روایت له مخې له الحکم سره څلور لکه کتابونه وو؛ د قلمي نسخو حاشیې یې پخپله لیکلې چې له امله یې د دې کتابونو ارزښت لا زیاتېده. د ډوزي په وینا «د اندلس نږدې هر کس لیک لوست زده و.»

This is a matter of the time when Europe was deprived of the light of knowledge. Knowledge was limited only to a few church priests. Like the Hindu Brahmins, the Christian priests had become the monopolists of knowledge. The common man was not being given the right to acquire knowledge. This is the greatest achievement of the Muslims that they took knowledge out of a few special halls and made it common for the public. Ending the monopoly of a few people over education, they brought its benefit to every lane, so that rich and poor, Muslim and non-Muslim, Spanish and non-Spanish — in short every human being of the world — could be watered from the spring of knowledge. For this very reason Dozy had to accept this reality that at the time when Europe, bringing about the industrial revolution, brought these universities to the height of progress, the Muslims, straying from this path, fell victim to civil wars. Against his brother he began to increase relations with others. He fell into the depths of decline and was expelled from Europe disgraced and humiliated. The Muslim of today too has strayed from his real path. He has turned away from his foundation.
