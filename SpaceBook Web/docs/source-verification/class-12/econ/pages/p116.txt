q 5 | بین الاقوامی تجارت کے فوائد اور نقصانات بیان کیجئے۔ (لاہور بورڈ 2011ء، سرگودھا بورڈ 2010ء) || State the benefits and harms of international trade. (Lahore Board 2011, Sargodha Board 2010) || د نړیوالې سوداګرۍ ګټې او زیانونه بیان کړئ. (لاهور بورډ ۲۰۱۱، سرګودها بورډ ۲۰۱۰)
solution جواب
ss Benefits: specialisation and higher world production; availability of goods not produced at home; cheaper and better goods; wider markets for surplus output; foreign exchange earnings; transfer of technology; employment; help in emergencies; friendly international relations.
ss Harms: (1) Dependence on other countries for necessities and defence goods, dangerous in war; (2) Competition from foreign goods can ruin infant domestic industries; (3) Exhaustion of natural resources through excessive exports of raw materials; (4) Import of harmful and luxury goods.
ss (5) Spread of economic depression and inflation from one country to another; (6) Balance of payments deficits; (7) Exploitation of poor countries by rich nations and multinationals; (8) Under-developed countries remain producers of primary goods; (9) Cultural influence and political pressure.

q 6 | نظریہ تقابلی مصارف کی تعریف کریں اور وضاحت بھی کریں۔ (بہاولپور بورڈ 2010ء، سرگودھا بورڈ 2011ء) || Define the theory of comparative costs and also explain it. (Bahawalpur Board 2010, Sargodha Board 2011) || د پرتلیزو لګښتونو نظریه تعریف کړئ او وضاحت یې هم وکړئ. (بهاولپور بورډ ۲۰۱۰، سرګودها بورډ ۲۰۱۱)
solution جواب
ss Definition: Ricardo's theory of comparative costs states that international trade takes place because of differences in comparative (relative) costs of producing goods in different countries; each country should produce and export the goods in which it has a comparative advantage and import those in which it has a comparative disadvantage.
ss Explanation with a table (labour days for one unit): Country A — wheat 10, cloth 20; Country B — wheat 12, cloth 15. A has an absolute advantage in wheat, B in cloth. Comparative cost of wheat in terms of cloth: A = 0.5, B = 0.8, so A specialises in wheat; cloth: A = 2, B = 1.25, so B specialises in cloth.
ss Gain from trade: if they exchange 1 wheat for (say) 1 cloth at a rate between their domestic cost ratios, A gets cloth for 10 days' labour instead of 20, and B gets wheat for 15 days instead of 12×… less than it would cost; total output of both goods rises. The theory holds even when one country is more efficient in both goods, as long as the cost ratios differ.
endsolution
q 7 | نظریہ تقابلی مصارف کا تنقیدی جائزہ پیش کیجئے۔ || Present a critical review of the theory of comparative costs. || د پرتلیزو لګښتونو د نظریې انتقادي کتنه وړاندې کړئ.
solution جواب
ss Criticism: (1) Labour theory of value — it considers only labour as the cost, while capital, land and entrepreneurship also matter (Ohlin, Haberler); (2) Unrealistic assumption of only two countries and two goods; (3) Ignores transport costs, which can cancel the cost advantage; (4) Assumes constant costs, but in reality there are increasing and decreasing returns.
ss (5) Assumes full employment and perfect competition, which do not exist; (6) Ignores demand — it does not tell the rate at which goods will be exchanged (J. S. Mill added reciprocal demand); (7) Assumes factors immobile between countries, but capital and labour do move; (8) Ignores tariffs, quotas and government policies; (9) Static theory — ignores changes in technology and growth; (10) Developing countries specialising in primary goods may lose from trade (terms of trade decline). Despite this, the theory explains the basic cause of trade.
endsolution
q 8 | گلوبلائزیشن سے کیا مراد ہے؟ اس کے فوائد بیان کیجئے۔ || What is meant by globalization? State its benefits. || له ګلوبلایزېشن څه مراد دی؟ ګټې یې بیان کړئ.
solution جواب
ss Globalization is the process of integration of national economies into one world economy through free flow of trade, capital, technology, information and people, supported by the WTO and modern communication.
ss Benefits: (1) Expansion of trade and markets; (2) Foreign direct investment and multinational companies bring capital and jobs; (3) Transfer of modern technology and management skills; (4) Consumers get cheaper, better and varied goods; (5) Competition increases efficiency and quality.
ss (6) Information technology connects the world (internet, e-commerce); (7) Specialisation and economies of scale; (8) Growth of services such as banking, tourism and outsourcing; (9) Cultural exchange and international cooperation; (10) Faster economic growth for countries that can compete.
endsolution
q 9 | کثیر القومی کمپنیوں کے فوائد اور نقصانات پر بحث کریں۔ || Discuss the benefits and harms of multinational companies. || د څو ملیتي شرکتونو پر ګټو او زیانونو بحث وکړئ.
solution جواب
ss A multinational company (MNC) is a business with its head office in one country and branches, factories or subsidiaries in many countries, e.g. Nestlé, Unilever, Coca-Cola, Toyota.
ss Benefits: (1) Bring foreign capital and investment; (2) Transfer of modern technology and management; (3) Create employment and train local workers; (4) Increase production and exports, earning foreign exchange; (5) Pay taxes to the government; (6) Provide quality goods to consumers; (7) Develop local suppliers and industries.
ss Harms: (1) Transfer large profits abroad, draining foreign exchange; (2) Destroy local small industries through competition; (3) Create monopolies and exploit consumers; (4) Use outdated or capital-intensive technology; (5) Exploit natural resources and cheap labour; (6) Influence the politics and policies of host countries; (7) Promote foreign culture and luxury consumption.
endsolution
q 10 | عالمی تجارتی تنظیم (WTO) کے اصول و قواعد اور کارکردگی بیان کیجئے۔ || State the principles, rules and performance of the World Trade Organization (WTO). || د سوداګرۍ د نړیوال سازمان (WTO) اصول، قواعد او کارکړنه بیان کړئ.
solution جواب
ss The World Trade Organization (WTO) was established on 1 January 1995 in place of GATT; its headquarters is in Geneva and it has over 160 members, including Pakistan.
ss Principles: (1) Non-discrimination — Most-Favoured-Nation treatment (equal treatment of all members) and national treatment (imported goods treated like local goods); (2) Freer trade — reduction of tariffs and removal of quotas; (3) Predictability — binding commitments and transparency; (4) Fair competition — rules against dumping and subsidies; (5) Special concessions for developing countries.
ss Rules/agreements: GATT for goods, GATS for services, TRIPS for intellectual property, TRIMs for investment, Agreement on Agriculture, Agreement on Textiles and Clothing (quotas ended 2005), anti-dumping and dispute-settlement rules.
ss Performance: it has reduced tariffs and expanded world trade, settled hundreds of trade disputes, given a forum for trade negotiations (Doha Round), and helped integrate developing countries; but it is criticised because rich countries still protect agriculture, and poor countries' industries face strong competition.
endsolution
