He regarded lentils as a Hindu innovation and eating vegetables as a plain violation of cattle’s rights. For him “karahi gosht” meant not merely eating karahi meat but eating a karahi-full; luckily “balti gosht” was not yet in fashion, or he would surely have preferred the bucket to the karahi. Picking at the bones of partridge and quail, or spitting out the seeds of grapes, oranges and watermelon, he counted among feminine fussiness. He himself was helpless before his own bulk and his “eating posture”. He loved strolling, on condition that after every forty steps he paused to rest and put something in his belly before the next forty. He had a refined nature and remarkable insight and perception that went straight to the heart of a matter. He was cultured in the true sense, having endured and dealt with man and life in every hue.
