box info | Rules of articles (continued) || حروفِ تعریف کے قاعدے (جاری) || د مقالو قاعدې (دوام)
- Rule 2: Uncountable nouns don't use 'a' or 'an'. e.g., advice is an uncountable noun. You say "Seek advice from wise people before doing something". We cannot say "Seek a or an advice in this sentence". However it can be a piece of advice.
- Rule 3: You can use 'the' to make general things specific. You can use 'the' with any type of noun - plural or singular, countable or uncountable. "Please give me a pen" - any pen. "Please give me the pen you bought yesterday". "The children grow up quickly" - children in general. "The children I know, grew up quickly" - not all children, just the ones I know. "Poetry can be beautiful" - poetry in general. "The poetry of Allama Iqbal is beautiful".
- More uses of English articles: Rivers, mountain ranges, seas, oceans and geographic areas use 'the'. For example: "The Indus", "The Himalayas", "The Gobi desert", "The Middle East".
- Specific things have 'the'. For example: "the sun", "the moon" (heavenly bodies).
- Countries: We don't use 'a' if the country is singular. "I live in Pakistan". But if the country's name has a "plural" meaning, we use 'the'. "The Islamic Republic of Pakistan".
endbox
