p Inflection is a word formation in which letters are added to the basic form of a word to change grammatical function. Inflections in English grammar include:
- Adding 's' to first form of verb in present indefinite tense: e.g. She helps the poor. Ali plays cricket.
- The teacher talks to his students, the third-person singular; past tense -d, -ed, or -t.
- Adding Plural 's'/'es': bags, books, boxes, ...
- For comparative degree add -er; and the superlative degree -est in adjectives.
- Inflections are often used to show tenses, person and number. The inflection -s at the end of cars shows that the noun is plural. The same inflection -s at the end of runs shows that the subject is in the third-person singular (he/she/it). The inflection -ed is often used to indicate the past tense, changing walk to walked and listen to listened. In this way, inflections are used to show grammatical categories such as tense, person and number, etc.
