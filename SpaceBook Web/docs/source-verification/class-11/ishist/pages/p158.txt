He always kept the thought that no one in the state should be hungry. For the poor and the destitute, daily allowances were fixed from the Bayt al-Mal without regard to religion. Disabled and needy persons were paid salaries from the Bayt al-Mal. He built guest houses in most cities, where travellers were fed free of charge at the expense of the Bayt al-Mal. In Madinah a general free kitchen was established for the Arabs. Hazrat Umar himself looked after its arrangement. Special care was taken of orphaned children. For this the arrangement of milk was made from the Bayt al-Mal; indeed, their stipend was fixed from the very day of the children’s birth. At night he himself went on patrol so that he could learn the condition of the poor. Once a woman was trying to put her hungry children to sleep. When he saw them in this state, he went back to his house, himself lifted flour and so on, and brought it to that woman and the children. His slave Aslam was with him. The slave asked to lift the flour, but he said, “On the Day of Resurrection you will not be able to lift my burden.” By this act he presented a practical example of equality. He abolished the distinction of master and servant. The money that remained after government expenses was distributed among the Muslims. In this, nearness to the Holy Prophet, priority in accepting Islam and military service for Islam were given preference. For the first time in the history of the world he introduced a regular system of pensions.
