Hajjaj controlled the situation with a political trick. Hajjaj, together with Muhallab, finished the Kharijites. The famous general of Hajjaj, Abd al-Rahman bin Ash’ath, rebelled. This revolt was not only against Hajjaj but also against Abd al-Malik. Abd al-Malik sent further army to help Hajjaj. At the place Tustar the two armies faced each other. Hajjaj was defeated and took refuge in Basrah. Ibn Ash’ath pursued him there too; Hajjaj took refuge at Zawiyah near Basrah. A hard battle took place between the two parties; in this battle Hajjaj got the upper hand. Ibn Ash’ath, reaching Kufah, began gathering the army again and took possession of the whole of Iraq. Hajjaj tried several times to defeat Ash’ath but failed. Abd al-Malik sent further reinforcements; this time Ibn Ash’ath was defeated; he took refuge with Iyad in Sistan but later committed suicide. In the time of Walid bin Abd al-Malik too the distinguished position of Hajjaj bin Yusuf remained. According to an English historian, Hajjaj bin Yusuf is famous for his cruelty, bloodthirstiness and love of rank, but he also had a few qualities. Hajjaj was a fiery orator who at once affected the gathering. He had command of the religious sciences. His recitation was especially very famous in the whole of Arabia. The honour of putting vowel marks in the Holy Quran also fell to him.
