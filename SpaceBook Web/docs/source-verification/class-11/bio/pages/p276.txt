qp 1 | Why insectivorous plants depend on insects? || حشرات خور پودے کیڑوں پر کیوں انحصار کرتے ہیں؟ || حشره خوړونکي بوټي په حشراتو ولې تکیه کوي؟
sol Model answer: Insectivorous plants (sundew, Venus flytrap, pitcher plant, bladderwort) grow in soils poor in nitrogen (acid bogs and swamps). They trap insects and digest them with enzymes to obtain nitrogen compounds and other minerals, which the roots cannot get from the soil. They make their sugar by photosynthesis, so they depend on insects only for minerals. ## Unit 10 mineral nutrition.
qp 2 | What do you mean by water potential? || پانی کی استعداد (واٹر پوٹینشل) سے آپ کیا سمجھتے ہیں؟ || د اوبو پوتانسیل څخه څه مطلب لرئ؟
sol Model answer: Water potential (psi) is the measure of the free energy of water, i.e. the tendency of water to move from one place to another; it is the sum of solute (osmotic) potential and pressure potential. Pure water has the highest water potential, which is taken as zero; solutions have negative values. Water moves from a region of higher (less negative) water potential to a lower (more negative) one. ## Unit 10 water relations.
qp 3 | Differentiate between mesophytes and xerophytes. || میزوفائٹس اور زیروفائٹس میں فرق کریں۔ || د میزوفایټونو او زیروفایټونو ترمنځ توپیر وکړئ.
sol Model answer: Mesophytes live in places with a moderate supply of water (most crop plants, rose, mango); they have a thin cuticle, stomata mostly on the lower surface, broad leaves and well-developed roots. Xerophytes live in dry places (cactus, Calotropis, date palm); they have a thick cuticle, sunken stomata, reduced or spiny leaves, fleshy stems that store water, deep or wide roots and hairs, so that water loss is reduced. ## Unit 10 plant adaptations.
qp 4 | How turgor provide support to the herbaceous plants? || ٹرگر نرم تنے والے (ہربیشس) پودوں کو کس طرح سہارا دیتا ہے؟ || ټرګر هربېشس بوټو ته څنګه ملاتړ برابروي؟
sol Model answer: In herbaceous plants, which have little wood, the cells take in water by osmosis and become turgid, pressing against their walls (turgor pressure). The cells of the stem and leaves, swollen like inflated balloons, push against each other and keep the plant firm and upright. If water is lost, cells become flaccid and the plant wilts. ## Unit 10 support.
qp 5 | Differentiate between primary and secondary growth in plants. || پودوں میں ابتدائی اور ثانوی نشوونما میں فرق کریں۔ || په بوټو کې د لومړنۍ او ثانوي ودې ترمنځ توپیر وکړئ.
sol Model answer: Primary growth is the increase in length of roots and shoots caused by the apical meristems at the tips; it forms the primary tissues (epidermis, cortex, primary xylem and phloem) and occurs in all plants. Secondary growth is the increase in thickness (girth) caused by the lateral meristems (vascular cambium and cork cambium); it forms secondary xylem (wood), secondary phloem and cork (bark), and occurs in dicots and gymnosperms but not in most monocots. ## Unit 10 plant growth.
qp 6 | Briefly describe the mechanism of formation of annual rings in plants. || پودوں میں سالانہ حلقوں کی تشکیل کے میکانزم کو مختصراً بیان کریں۔ || په بوټو کې د کلنیو کړیو د جوړېدو میکانیزم په لنډه توګه بیان کړئ.
sol Model answer: Annual rings are formed by the vascular cambium. In spring, with plenty of water, the cambium makes wide xylem vessels and thin-walled tracheids (early or spring wood, light in colour). Later in the summer and autumn, as water gets scarce, it makes narrow, thick-walled cells (late or autumn wood, dark in colour). In winter the cambium rests. The next spring, a new layer of wide cells begins, so a clear ring forms every year. Counting the rings shows the age of the tree, and their width shows the climate of each year. ## Unit 10 secondary growth.
qp 7 | List the adaptation in plants to cope with low temperatures. || کم درجہ حرارت کا مقابلہ کرنے کے لیے پودوں میں پائی جانے والی موافقتوں کی فہرست بنائیں۔ || د ټیټې تودوخې سره د مقابلې لپاره په بوټو کې د انطباقونو لیست جوړ کړئ.
sol Model answer: Adaptations of plants to cold: (1) Deciduous plants shed leaves in autumn and become dormant. (2) Buds are covered by scales and the plant is covered with hairs or thick bark for insulation. (3) The cell sap contains more solutes (sugars, proline, antifreeze proteins), which lower the freezing point. (4) Unsaturated fatty acids in membranes keep them fluid in the cold. (5) Many herbs survive as seeds, bulbs or underground stems. (6) Evergreen conifers have needle-shaped leaves with thick cuticle and drooping branches that shed snow. (7) Low, compact (cushion) growth forms. ## Unit 10 temperature.

qp 1 | Elaborate the role of macro and micronutrients plant growth and development. || پودوں کی نشوونما اور ارتقا میں میکرو اور مائیکرو نیوٹرینٹس کے کردار کی تفصیل بیان کریں۔ || د بوټي په وده او پرمختګ کې د ماکرو او مایکرو نیوټرینټونو رول تشریح کړئ.
sol Model answer: Macronutrients are needed in large amounts: nitrogen (proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll; its lack gives yellowing), phosphorus (ATP, DNA, membranes), potassium (opening of stomata, enzyme activation), calcium (cell wall, middle lamella), magnesium (centre of chlorophyll), sulphur (amino acids) as well as carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. Micronutrients are needed in very small amounts: iron (chlorophyll formation, cytochromes), manganese (photolysis of water), zinc (auxin synthesis, enzymes), copper (plastocyanin), boron (pollen tube growth), molybdenum (nitrogen metabolism), chlorine (photosynthesis, ion balance). Their lack causes deficiency symptoms such as chlorosis, necrosis and stunted growth. ## Unit 10 mineral nutrition.
qp 2 | Explain the movement of water in xylem through TACT mechanism. || زائلم میں پانی کی حرکت کی وضاحت TACT میکانزم کے ذریعے کریں۔ || په زایلم کې د اوبو حرکت د TACT میکانیزم له لارې تشریح کړئ.
sol Model answer: Water moves up in the xylem by the Transpiration-Adhesion-Cohesion-Tension (TACT) mechanism. Transpiration: water evaporates from the leaf mesophyll cells through the stomata; this lowers the water potential in the leaf and creates a tension (negative pressure, a pull). Cohesion: hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together, so the tension pulls the whole water column in the xylem like a continuous thread. Adhesion: water molecules stick to the walls of the xylem vessels, which stops the column from falling and helps capillary rise. Tension: the negative pressure transmitted down the xylem to the roots pulls water in from the soil. ## Unit 10 transport in xylem.
qp 3 | Explain the movement of sugars within plants. || پودوں کے اندر شکروں کی حرکت کی وضاحت کریں۔ || په بوټو کې د بورو حرکت تشریح کړئ.
sol Model answer: Sugars (mostly sucrose) move through the phloem by translocation, explained by the pressure-flow (mass-flow) hypothesis of Munch. At a source (leaf), sucrose is actively loaded into the sieve tubes with the help of companion cells; water enters by osmosis and raises the pressure. At a sink (root, fruit, growing parts) sucrose is unloaded and used or stored as starch, and water leaves, so pressure falls. The pressure difference pushes the phloem sap from the source to the sink. Movement can be up or down according to the need. ## Unit 10 transport in phloem.
