p It is a well-established fact that many reactions do not go to completion[scan obscured] if favourable conditions are maintained. Such reactions proceed to certain[scan obscured] and then apparently stop. This is because the products react themselves to give[scan obscured] the original reactants and a time reaches when there is no further change[scan obscured] composition of the reaction mixture occurs. The reaction when reaches the[scan obscured] where no more products are formed is said to be at equilibrium state. p At the start of a reversible reaction, the reaction proceeds towards the[scan obscured] formation of products. As soon as some product molecules are formed, the reverse[scan obscured] process begins to take place and reactant molecules are formed from the product[scan obscured] molecules. At the beginning rate of forward reaction is fast since the reactant[scan obscured] concentration is high and rate of reverse reaction is low since the concentration of[scan obscured] product is low. With the passage of time, rate of forward reaction gradually[scan obscured] becomes slow and rate of reverse reaction becomes fast. p Chemical equilibrium may be defined as the state of a reversible reaction[scan obscured] when the two opposing reactions occur at the same rate and the concentrations of[scan obscured] the reactants and products do not change with time. It is observed that under any[scan obscured] given set of conditions of temperature, pressure, and concentration the point at[scan obscured] which the reaction seems to stop or equilibrium is attained, is always the same.[scan obscured] However, the point or state of equilibrium changes by changing the conditions of[scan obscured] the reaction.
