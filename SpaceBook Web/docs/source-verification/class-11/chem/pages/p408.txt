th | Term | Definition
tr | Tetrahedral shape | The most stable shape for a compound that contains four atoms bonded to a central atom with no lone pairs; the atoms are positioned at the four corners of an imaginary tetrahedron, and the angles between the bonds are approximately 109.5°
tr | Theoretical yield | The maximum amount of product that can be produced according to a balanced equation.
tr | Thermal decomposition | The chemical breakdown of a substance under the influence of heat.
tr | Thermochemistry | The study of the energy that is involved in chemical reactions
tr | Thermodynamics | The study of energy and energy transfer
tr | Torr | A unit of pressure (1 torr = 1 mm Hg).
tr | Trigonal planar | A molecular shape in which three bonding groups surround a central atom; the three bonded atoms are all in the same plane as the central atom, at the corners of an invisible triangle
tr | Unit cell | the smallest portion of a crystal lattice that shows the three-dimensional pattern of the entire lattice
tr | Valence electrons | The electrons in the outermost energy level of an atom; these electrons are the ones involved in bonding atoms together to form compounds.
tr | Van der Waals' equation | It is an equation of sate of gases that modified the ideal gas equation to represent more faithfully the pressure and volume behavior of real gases.
tr | Vapour | The gaseous phase of [scan obscured] liquid.
tr | Vapour pressure | The pressure exerted by a vapor in equilibrium with its liquid.
tr | Viscosity | The resistance of a liquid to flow.
tr | Visible Spectrum | The spectrum which can be seen through naked eye, are called as visible spectrum and consists of seven colours, Violet (V), Indigo (I), Blue (B), Green (G), Yellow (Y), Orange (O), and Red (R)
tr | Voltaic cell | A cell that produces electric current from a spontaneous oxidation reduction reaction is carried out and electrical current is produced.
tr | VSEPR | The electron pairs (both lone pairs and shared pairs) surrounding the central atom will be arranged in space as far apart as possible to minimize the electrostatic repulsion between them.
