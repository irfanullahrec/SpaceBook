## 10.1 General Properties of Solutions || 10.1 محلولوں کی عمومی خصوصیات || 10.1 د محلولونو عمومي ځانګړنې
p A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances in which[scan obscured] the ratio of the constituent substances remains the same throughout the solution[scan obscured] Generally a solution consists of two components, the solute and solvent. The[scan obscured] substance that is present in lesser amount is the solute while the one with the[scan obscured] greater amount is called solvent. Solute may be solid, liquid or gas or combination[scan obscured] of these. The particles of solute may be at ionic or molecular level. The particle size[scan obscured] ranges from 0.01 to 1 nm, which cannot be seen by naked eye. A solution is stable if[scan obscured] it is not separated on standing. Solute and solvent cannot be separated by filtration[scan obscured] or by centrifugation. It is composed of one phase. At a given temperature and[scan obscured] pressure, the composition of solution can be varied to a maximum value up to the[scan obscured] saturation value. A solution does not allow a beam of light to scatter when passed[scan obscured] through it. p Colloids, on the other hand, are heterogeneous mixture of larger particles or[scan obscured] aggregate of particles of size ranging from 1 to 1000nm dispersed in solvent or[scan obscured] dispersion medium. They do not settle down on standing and cannot be separated[scan obscured] by filtration. They scatter light when passed through them, a phenomena, called the[scan obscured] Tyndal effect. Gum in water is an example of colloidal dispersion. Suspensions are[scan obscured] also heterogeneous mixtures with size of the particles greater than 1000nm. Particles[scan obscured] are settled down on standing and can be separated by filtration.
