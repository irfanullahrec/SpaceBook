p Putting values from equations 11 and 12 in equation 2: W = P₁Δm₁/ρ − P₂Δm₂/ρ (13). The net change in kinetic energy ΔK is ΔK = ½Δm₂v₂² − ½Δm₁v₁² (14). The net change in potential energy ΔU is ΔU = Δm₂gh₂ − Δm₁gh₁ (15). Putting values from equation 13, equation 14 and equation 15 in equation 1: P₁Δm₁/ρ − P₂Δm₂/ρ = ½Δm₂v₂² − ½Δm₁v₁² + Δm₂gh₂ − Δm₁gh₁ (16). Since for ideal fluid equal mass should flow across both ends, therefore Δm₁ = Δm₂ = Δm (17). From equation 17, equation 16 can be written as P₁Δm/ρ − P₂Δm/ρ = ½Δmv₂² − ½Δmv₁² + Δmgh₂ − Δmgh₁. Taking Δm as common: (Δm/ρ)(P₁−P₂) = Δm(½v₂² − ½v₁² + gh₂ − gh₁). Multiplying both sides by ρ, we get P₁−P₂ = ½ρv₂² − ½ρv₁² + ρgh₂ − ρgh₁. Therefore P₁ + ½ρv₁² + ρgh₁ = P₂ + ½ρv₂² + ρgh₂ (6.6), or P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant (6.7). Equations 6.6 and 6.7 are termed Bernoulli’s equations.
