p Consider n moles of an ideal gas confined in a cylinder fitted with a fixed piston (Figure 10.9). When the gas is heated, its volume remains constant. All heat supplied increases the kinetic energy of the molecules, so the gas temperature rises. If ΔQᵥ is the heat supplied to n moles of an ideal gas and ΔT is the temperature rise, the definition of molar specific heat gives ΔQᵥ = nCᵥΔT (10.8). Cᵥ is the molar specific heat of a gas at constant volume. p 10.8.2 CONSTANT-PRESSURE MOLAR SPECIFIC HEAT Cₚ. The heat needed to raise the temperature of one mole of a gas by 1 K while keeping its pressure constant is its constant-pressure molar specific heat Cₚ. Consider n moles of an ideal gas in a cylinder with a movable piston (Figure 10.10). Heating raises its temperature and expands it, pushing the piston upward against constant external pressure; the gas does work on the piston. If ΔQₚ is the heat supplied to n moles and ΔT is the temperature rise, ΔQₚ = nCₚΔT (10.9). Cₚ is the molar specific heat of a gas at constant pressure.
