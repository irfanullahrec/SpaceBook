p COAL. Coal is the most abundant fossil fuel available on Earth. It is mostly found below Earth and is a major fuel source for electricity generation. Coal formed slowly over millions of years from buried remains of ancient swamp plants. The world currently consumes about 5 billion metric tons per year. Besides electricity, coal is used for heating and cooking in less developed countries and rural areas of developed countries. At the same rate of consumption, current reserves will last more than 200 years. p OIL. Oil is abundant in many Middle Eastern countries such as Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Iran, Iraq, and the UAE. Like coal, it was made from dead plants and animals that lived millions of years ago. They were covered with thick layers of mud and sand, creating great pressure and temperature. Most known oil reserves are already being exploited, and oil is used faster than new sources are discovered. p NATURAL GAS. Natural gas is the gaseous form of fossil fuels. It is a mixture of gases including methane, ethane, propane, and butane. It burns completely and leaves no ashes. It causes almost no pollution and is one of the cleanest fossil fuels. Its use is growing rapidly. Natural gas is easy and inexpensive to transport once pipelines are in place. In developed countries it is used primarily for heating, cooking, and powering vehicles. It is also used to make ammonia fertilizer. Current estimated natural-gas reserves are about 100 million metric tons.
