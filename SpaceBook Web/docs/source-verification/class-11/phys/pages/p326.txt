p Consider plane wavefronts incident on narrow slit AB (Figure 9.15(b)). By Huygens’ principle each point on the slit wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets. At screen point O on the perpendicular bisector, waves from A and B arrive in phase because they are equidistant; there is no path difference for A/B or X/Y, so constructive interference gives a bright fringe. For every point on wavefront AQ there is a symmetric point on QB; their wavelets reach O in phase and superpose to make a bright spot. At a point P below O, the path difference between wavelets from A and B is λ. The path differences for pairs A/Q, X/Y, and Q/B reaching P are each approximately λ/2.
