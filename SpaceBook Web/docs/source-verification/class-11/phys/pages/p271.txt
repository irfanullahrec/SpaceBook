p In the sound-interference apparatus, drawing out the sliding tube makes path ADB longer than ACB. If the path difference is half a wavelength, the waves interfere destructively and no sound is heard at B. Pinching the rubber tube at C stops waves along that path, and the ear hears sound again; this shows the silence was due to destructive interference. 8.8 BEATS. We discussed superposition of two sound waves with the same frequency. If two slightly different frequencies are superposed, a stationary observer detects fluctuating loudness: loud, faint, loud, faint. This periodic variation in loudness heard when two notes of slightly different frequencies are played simultaneously is called beats. Two sources with slightly different frequencies produce a single note whose intensity rises and falls. To study beats, take two audio-frequency generators, such as 256 Hz tuning forks; add a little wax to fork B so its frequency becomes 254 Hz; place both equally distant from the ear and sound them together.
