p Anyone may find the power he or she is able to develop when running upstairs by measuring the total vertical height of a stairway and using a stopwatch to find the time taken. The unit of power is called watt. Power is said to be one watt if one joule of work is done by a body in 1 second: 1 watt = J s⁻¹. A watt is a very small unit; usually we use kilowatts: 1 kW = 1000 watts. Watt is commonly used for the power of bulbs and electric fans. The power of generating stations and grid stations is expressed in megawatts (1 MW = 10⁶ watts). We usually use the unit kilowatt-hour (1 kWh), the commercial unit of work (electrical energy). Sometimes work is expressed as watt-second. A commercial unit of electrical energy is kilowatt-hour. One kilowatt-hour is the work done in one hour by an agent whose power is one kilowatt: 1 kWh = 1000 watts × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 3.6 MJ. The power of a TV set is 120 watts and that of a pocket calculator is 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ watts.
