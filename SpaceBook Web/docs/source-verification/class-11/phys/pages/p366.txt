p Heat-engine thermal efficiency is work done by the engine in each cycle divided by heat Q₁ absorbed in each cycle: η=ΔW/Q₁ (10.12). Using ΔW=Q₁−Q₂ gives η=(Q₁−Q₂)/Q₁=1−Q₂/Q₁ (10.13). If Q₂=0, all absorbed heat would be converted to work and η=1=100%. Experiments show that a 100% efficient heat engine is impossible: a steam, gasoline or diesel engine must reject some heat Q₂ to the heat sink (LTR). One statement of the second law: no heat engine can have as its sole result the extraction of heat from a reservoir and production of an equal amount of work. Quiz: What is the function of the spark plug in a petrol engine? If a diesel engine has no spark plug, how does its fuel burn? Example 10.4—Heat engine: A reversible engine works between temperatures differing by 100°C. It absorbs 746 J from the source and rejects 546 J to the sink.
