p In an isochoric process, supplied heat becomes internal energy; pressure and temperature rise. Removing heat decreases internal energy; the system cools and pressure falls. Figure 10.5: a fixed-piston heat reservoir and a vertical line on the P–V graph (“isochor”) parallel to the pressure axis. ISOBARIC PROCESS: pressure is constant. Isobaric expansion converts heat into work; practically all heat engines use heat-to-work transformation. Consider gas in a cylinder with conducting base, nonconducting walls and a frictionless piston of area A. Initially its volume, temperature and pressure are V₁,T₁,P. Heat ΔQ enters; the gas expands and pushes the piston outward. For a sufficiently small displacement, pressure remains approximately constant; final volume and temperature are V₂,T₂. For small upward piston displacement ΔY, work by gas is ΔW=(Force)(distance)=FΔY (i). Since F=PA, ΔW=PAΔY.
