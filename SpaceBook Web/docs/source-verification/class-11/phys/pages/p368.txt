p The second law has equivalent statements emphasizing different aspects. 10.10.1 LORD KELVIN STATEMENT: It is impossible to construct a heat engine operating continuously in a cycle that takes heat from a high-temperature source and performs an equivalent amount of work without rejecting heat to a low-temperature sink. A high-temperature source and low-temperature sink are essential for converting heat into mechanical work. During a complete cycle heat cannot all become useful work: an engine converts part into work and rejects the remainder to the sink. Thus work output is less than absorbed heat and efficiency is always below 100%. 10.10.2 RUDOLF CLAUSIUS STATEMENT: It is impossible to cause heat to flow from a cold body to a hot body without expenditure of work. The first law describes heat and work as equivalent forms of energy; the second law concerns conversion of heat into work. For a cyclic process, the first law forbids getting more work from an engine than the heat supplied; the second law says work performed is less than heat absorbed. 10.11 CARNOT HEAT ENGINE. A heat engine is a closed system exchanging only heat and work with its surroundings and operating in cycles. All cyclic heat engines derive heat from a source, convert some to useful work and reject the rest to a sink. The source sentence continues on the next page.
