p The arrows indicating wavefront propagation are called rays. Rays are always perpendicular to wavefronts (Figure 9.3), so wave motion can be specified by rays or by drawing the wave itself. Illuminance varies inversely with the square of distance from a light source (Figure 9.3). As a wave travels from its source, its curvature decreases. p 9.3 HUYGENS’ PRINCIPLE. Knowing the shape and location of a wavefront at time t enables determination of the new wavefront’s shape and location at t + Δt. The principle has two parts: (1) Every point on a wavefront may be treated as a source of secondary spherical wavelets. They spread in the forward direction at the wave’s propagation speed. (2) The new wavefront at time t + Δt is the plane surface tangent to all secondary wavelets.
