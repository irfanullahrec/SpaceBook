p A diffraction grating works by interference and diffraction of light and is used to measure wavelength. Light diffracted by its slits is allowed to interfere. Figure 9.16(a) shows a five-slit grating and its operation. A parallel monochromatic beam produces waves from every slit; in certain directions, waves from adjacent slits are in phase and reinforce. The diffracted parallel rays make angle θ with the normal and an achromatic lens focuses them at screen point P. If adjacent rays 1 and 2 differ in path by one wavelength λ, they reinforce at P. Waves from any two consecutive slits then have path difference λ and produce a bright fringe. Thus constructive interference requires Δr = λ (i). From Figure 9.16(b), Δr = d sinθ (ii), where d is the separation of adjacent slits.
