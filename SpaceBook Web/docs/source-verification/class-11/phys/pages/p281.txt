p RESONANCE OF A STRETCHED STRING. When a stretched string is driven by a small periodic force at one of its quantized frequencies, resonance produces a stationary wave. Only the discrete frequencies f₁, f₂, f₃, … are possible; the resonant frequencies are quantized and separated by frequency gaps. EXAMPLE 8.6. A wave speed on a string is 24 m s⁻¹ and the string is 6.0 m long. At what driving frequencies will it resonate? Possible wavelengths λₙ = 2L/n: λ₁ = 12 m, λ₂ = 6 m, λ₃ = 4 m. Corresponding frequencies are 24/12 = 2 Hz, 24/6 = 4 Hz and 24/4 = 6 Hz. Answer: 2 Hz, 4 Hz and 6 Hz. EXAMPLE 8.7. A 4.0 m string has mass 3.0 g, one end fixed and the other passing over a pulley carrying a 20 kg mass. Find transverse-wave speed. GIVEN: L = 4.0 m; string mass = 0.003 kg; hanging mass M = 20 kg. Mass per unit length μ = 0.003/4.0 = 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ kg m⁻¹. Tension T = Mg = 20 × 9.8 = 196 N. Therefore v = √(T/μ) = 511.2 m s⁻¹. ASSIGNMENT 8.3. A 40 g string 2 m long vibrates in three loops under tension 270 N. Find wavelength and frequency. (1.33 m, 87.1 Hz.) 8.13 RESONANCE OF AIR COLUMNS AND ORGAN PIPES. Stationary waves with discrete frequencies can form in any medium, including air columns as well as stretched strings, springs and elastic membranes. Holding a sounding tuning fork over the open end of a glass tube filled with water can greatly amplify the sound under certain conditions.
