p 10.9 HEAT ENGINE. A heat engine converts heat energy into mechanical work; steam, petrol and diesel engines are examples. A heat engine has these parts. Heat source or reservoir: a very large high-temperature body at T₁ whose temperature stays practically unchanged during heat transfer; also called the high-temperature reservoir (HTR). Heat sink or cold reservoir: a very large low-temperature body at T₂ whose temperature likewise remains practically unchanged; also called the low-temperature reservoir (LTR). Working substance: normally a gas. The substance undergoes a cyclic process, absorbs heat Q₁ from the source at T₁, does maximum work W on the surroundings and rejects heat Q₂ to the sink at T₂ (Figure 10.12). At the end of a complete cycle the system returns to its original internal energy, so ΔU=0. The first law gives ΔQ=ΔU+ΔW=ΔW. Thus for one cycle ΔW=Q₁−Q₂ (10.11).
