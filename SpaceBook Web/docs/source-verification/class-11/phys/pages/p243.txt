p For an ideal oscillatory system, the total mechanical energy of the system remains constant, as discussed for a spring-mass system, circular motion and a simple pendulum. In practical life, dissipating forces act on these systems. Oscillations die out with time unless energy is continuously supplied. For example, to keep a swing continuously oscillating, give it a small push at the right time during each oscillation (Figure 7.17). Oscillations whose amplitude becomes smaller and smaller with time are called damped oscillations. To counteract damping forces, keep pumping a swing. A vehicle suspension system uses friction (air resistance, etc.) to damp its motion; damping involves work done against friction.
