p To demonstrate mechanical transverse stationary waves, consider a string of length L stretched by clamping both ends so tension is T. Pluck the string at different positions to find its characteristic frequencies. Plucking the midpoint sends transverse waves toward both ends. They reflect at the clamped ends, meet again at the middle and superpose to form a stationary wave. The whole string vibrates in one loop with nodes at its fixed ends and an antinode at the middle (Figure 8.24). The stationary-wave frequency equals the frequency f₁ of each progressive wave. The distance between successive nodes is half a wavelength, so L = λ₁/2 and λ₁ = 2L. Therefore f₁ = v/λ₁ = v/(2L) (8.20). If M is total string mass, progressive-wave speed is v = √(TL/M), where T is string tension and L its length.
