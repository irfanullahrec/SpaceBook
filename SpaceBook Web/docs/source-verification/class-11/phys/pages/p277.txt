p A stationary or standing wave forms when two plane waves of the same amplitude and frequency travel at the same speed in opposite directions and superpose. ACTIVITY. Tie one end of a rubber cord to a strong support and wiggle the other at frequency f₁. Transverse waves travel along the rope from the hand; at the fixed end they reflect with a slightly different shape. They superpose with newly generated waves, producing a stationary wave in which the cord vibrates in one loop. Its form does not move in either direction of the incident or reflected waves, hence “stationary.” Points that do not vibrate are nodes N. Midpoints between successive nodes, where amplitude is maximum, are antinodes A. Distance between successive nodes or antinodes is λ/2; adjacent node to antinode is λ/4. If frequency is increased to twice the initial frequency, f₂ = 2f₁, a stationary wave forms with two loops; this is the second harmonic.
