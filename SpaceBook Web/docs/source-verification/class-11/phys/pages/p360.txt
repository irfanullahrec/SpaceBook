p For a practical adiabatic process, use a completely insulated cylinder with movable piston; no heat enters or leaves, so ΔQ=0. Sudden expansion after lowering piston pressure cools the gas and decreases internal energy. Sudden compression heats it and increases internal energy. From first law ΔQ=ΔU+ΔW, 0=ΔU+ΔW, hence ΔW=−ΔU (equation 10.6). Thus an internal-energy increase equals work done on the system; if the system does work during adiabatic expansion, it is at the cost of internal energy. The P–V curve for ideal-gas adiabatic expansion is an “adiabat.” QUIZ: Why does ice form more slowly on ponds as more ice is formed?
