p THERMODYNAMICS. “Thermo” relates to heat and “dynamics” to motion of particles. Thermodynamics is the branch of physics dealing with laws for transformation of heat into other forms of energy—mechanical, chemical, electrical—and vice versa. It is principally based on the first and second laws. It explains operation of heat engines, refrigerators and heat pumps. System, surroundings, boundary and state variables have specific meanings in thermodynamics. p 10.1 THERMAL EQUILIBRIUM. When objects at different temperatures touch, energy transfers from the hotter to the colder object until they reach thermal equilibrium, that is, the same temperature. Heat is energy transferred spontaneously because of a temperature difference. Figure 10.1: (a) the soft drink is warmer than the ice, so they are not in equilibrium.
