p When a stretched slinky spring is pushed forward, loops near the applied force are compressed before the disturbance reaches the rest of the spring. Compressed loops exert force on loops to their right, so a compressional pulse travels along the spring. When the spring is pulled backward, loops near the applied force are rarefacted first; these exert force on loops to their right and a rarefaction travels along the spring. Pushing and pulling the spring at a constant rate sets up longitudinal waves. Other examples are sound waves and shock waves produced during an earthquake, also called seismic waves (Figure 8.10). 8.3.3 CHARACTERISTICS OF A WAVE. A wave is specified by wave speed, frequency, time period, phase, wavelength, amplitude and intensity. WAVE SPEED. The speed of a wave is the distance it travels per unit time.
