p For isobaric expansion, ΔW=PΔV (equation 10.4), where V₁ and V₂ are initial and final volumes and ΔV their increase. Using the first law ΔQ=ΔU+ΔW gives ΔQ=ΔU+PΔV (10.5). Work of expanding/contracting gas comes from heat supplied and/or its internal energy. The isobaric P–V graph is a straight line parallel to the volume axis (Figure 10.6); the figure labels gas states (P,V₁,T₁) and (P,V₂,T₂), heat Q and volume change ΔV. ISOTHERMAL PROCESS: a process in which the system changes while its temperature remains constant. For simplicity the text assumes an ideal gas. Ideal-gas internal energy depends on temperature, not volume. The shown setup has a cylinder with conducting base, nonconducting walls, movable piston and a heat reservoir at T₁, whose large heat capacity keeps the gas at T₁.
