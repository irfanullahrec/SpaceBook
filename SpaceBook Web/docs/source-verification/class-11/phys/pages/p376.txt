p Example 10.7—Change in entropy of water: Find the entropy change when 30 g (0.03 kg) water at 0°C freezes to ice at 0°C. Latent heat of fusion of ice H_f=336000 J kg⁻¹. At fusion temperature T=0°C=273 K, heat removed is ΔQ=mH_f=0.03×336000=10080 J. The source gives ΔS=ΔQ/T=10080/273=36.92 J K⁻¹ and prints answer 36.92 J K⁻¹ (magnitude; for the water freezing, ΔS is negative under the convention that entropy decreases when heat is removed). ASSIGNMENT 10.3—Melting a piece of lead: (a) Find entropy change of 3.00×10² g lead melting at 327°C; latent heat of fusion 2.45×10⁴ J kg⁻¹. (b) Use the same energy to melt part of silver already at its melting point 961°C; find entropy change. Printed answers: (a) 12.3 J/K; (b) 5.96 J/K. KEY POINTS: Thermodynamics is the branch of physics dealing with laws of transformation of heat into other forms of energy and vice versa. Internal energy is the sum of kinetic and potential energies associated with random motion of a substance’s atoms. First law: supplied heat ΔQ may partly raise internal energy ΔU; the remainder may be used as external work ΔW by the system; ΔQ=ΔU+ΔW.
