p (I-A) SOURCE MOVES TOWARD A STATIONARY LISTENER. Source S moves at speed a toward stationary listener L. The first wave emitted travels distance v in one second and reaches L. At the end of one second, the source has traveled distance a and emits the last wave. Thus f waves are compressed into distance (v − a). The apparent wavelength λ′ = (v − a)/f. The changed frequency f′ = v/λ′ = [v/(v − a)]f (8.27). If the source approaches a stationary listener, sound frequency and pitch increase. (I-B) SOURCE MOVES AWAY FROM A STATIONARY LISTENER. The f waves are now contained in distance (v + a), so apparent wavelength λ′ = (v + a)/f and apparent frequency f′ = [v/(v + a)]f (8.28). Since f′ < f, pitch decreases when the source moves away.
