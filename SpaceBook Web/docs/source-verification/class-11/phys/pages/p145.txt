p Hot water is pumped from deep underground through a well under high pressure. At the surface, pressure drops and the water turns into steam. Steam spins a turbine connected to a generator that produces electricity. Steam cools in a cooling tower and condenses back to water. The cooled water is pumped back into Earth to begin the process again. p 4. TIDAL ENERGY. Tides use gravitational pull from the Moon and Sun, which pulls water upward, while Earth’s rotational and gravitational forces pull water down, creating high and low tides. This movement of water is kinetic energy, so tidal energy is a renewable energy source. A tidal barrage is a dam that uses potential energy generated by the change in height between high and low tides (Figure 4.19). This energy turns a turbine or compresses air, generating electricity. Oxford University engineers calculated that underwater turbines across the width of a narrow sea inlet could generate a maximum 1.9 GW, averaged across the fortnightly tidal cycle.
