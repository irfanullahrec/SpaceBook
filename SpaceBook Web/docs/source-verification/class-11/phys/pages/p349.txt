p A kilocalorie (kcal) is the energy needed to raise 1.00 kg of water by 1.00°C. Besides supplying heat, work on a body can raise its temperature: rubbing hands, hammering a nail into wood, churning curd, and pumping a tyre with a hand pump. In each, work warms a body without heat supply. Heat and work can both raise temperature but are mutually distinct energy-transfer forms. Work is exchanged through overall displacement and does not require a temperature difference; heat transfer requires a temperature difference. QUIZ: A small drill boring hard board becomes hot—why? p 10.4.1 EQUIVALENCE OF HEAT AND WORK. Count Rumford, James Joule and others established equivalence. In 1798, artillery engineer Count Rumford observed heat released while boring gun metal; friction could produce heat exhaustively by mechanical work. The heat was not related to the mass of metal removed, but to work done against friction, leading Rumford to conclude heat is a form of energy. In 1845 Joule precisely measured heat produced by a certain amount of mechanical work and observed a definite relationship between work and heat.
