p Regular reflection of sound occurs when the reflecting surface treats all parts of the incident wavefront similarly; it must be flat to within a fraction of the wavelength. Sound waves above 20 kHz are used to determine shoals of fish in the ocean. A ship sends an underwater sound pulse; after reflection from the sea bottom, an underwater receiver detects it and records the time interval. Submarines are also detected by underwater sound waves from their propellers. ECHO. Reflection of an original sound from an object received 0.1 s later than direct sound is an echo. At sound speed about 340 m s⁻¹, effective distance to the reflector is (340 × 0.1)/2 = 17 m. Echoes in halls and auditoriums can be reduced by choosing suitable dimensions and avoiding continuous flat smooth walls. REVERBERATION. If the reflecting surface is less than 17 m from the source, echo follows the direct sound too closely to distinguish; this is reverberation, which confuses the sound impression. 8.10 STATIONARY WAVES. Sound from most string and wind instruments is due to stationary or standing waves. Vibrating strings of guitars and pianos set up stationary waves of definite frequencies. The source asks how stationary waves differ from progressive waves and how they are produced.
