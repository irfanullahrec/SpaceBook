p For a transverse wave pulse in an elastic stretched string or spring, v = √(T L/M) (8.1), where M is the string mass, L its length and T the tension. If μ is the string mass per unit length, v = √(T/μ) (8.2). Thus a well-stretched, thin string has a greater transverse-wave speed than a loose, thick one. The speed of a compressional or longitudinal wave depends on the medium’s elastic modulus E and density ρ: v = √(E/ρ) (8.3). Longitudinal waves travel more slowly in gases than in solids because gases are more compressible and have a smaller elastic modulus E. FREQUENCY OF WAVE. The number of waves passing a point per unit time is the wave frequency. As a wave progresses, each medium particle oscillates with the frequency and period of the source; wave frequency f equals the frequency of the simple harmonic oscillating source. Frequency = number of waves passing a point / time taken. TIME PERIOD OF WAVE. As a wave progresses, its particles vibrate with the source’s period; the wave period equals the period of the simple harmonic oscillator.
