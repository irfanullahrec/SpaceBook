p A natural process proceeds in a direction that increases the entropy of the system plus its environment. Natural processes tend toward greater disorder, relating entropy to molecular disorder. Irreversible heat flow from a hot to a cold region increases disorder: molecules initially separated by temperature become mixed at thermal equilibrium. Adding heat increases average molecular speeds and randomness of motion. Free expansion of a gas increases randomness of molecular positions. In both examples entropy increases. Only processes in which system entropy increases or remains constant are probable; in irreversible processes system entropy increases. Entropy is also a measure of disorder. Example: a box contains equal numbers of red and green balls, initially arranged on opposite sides. Shaking mixes them, changing the state from ordered to disordered and increasing entropy. Disorder increases in natural processes, so entropy measures disorder. In natural processes energy tends to pass from a more useful form to a less useful form; this is called degradation of energy. The continuing increase of entropy and disorder in the universe affects energy available for useful work. Energy continually degrades for doing useful work; ultimately the universe’s entropy should reach a maximum. Then the universe would have uniform temperature and density. Physical, chemical and biological processes would cease because perfect disorder implies no energy available to do work.
