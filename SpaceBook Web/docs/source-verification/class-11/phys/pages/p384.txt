p GLOSSARY: Null vector—Vector of zero magnitude without a specific direction. Orbital velocity—Tangential velocity that puts a satellite in orbit around Earth. Oscillatory motion—To-and-fro motion of a body about its mean position. Periodic motion—Motion repeating itself after equal time intervals. Phase—Quantity indicating the state and direction of motion of a vibrating particle. Pitch—Sound characteristic by which a shrill sound is distinguished from a grave sound. Plane wavefront—Disturbance lying in a plane surface. Polarization—Orientation of vibration along a particular direction. Position vector—Vector describing the location of a point. Potential energy—Energy possessed by a body due to position. Power—Rate of doing work. Progressive wave—Wave transferring energy away from its source. Projectile—Object moving under gravity and horizontally at the same time. Random error—Error due to fluctuations in a measured quantity. Range of a projectile—Horizontal distance from launch point to the point where the projectile returns to its launching height. Rarefaction—Region of minimum density. Rarer medium—Medium with relatively less density. Rays—Radial lines leaving a point source in all directions. Resolution power—Instrument’s ability to reveal minor details of an object under examination. Resonance—Specific response of a vibrating system to a periodic force acting at its natural period. Restoring force—Force bringing a body back to equilibrium position. Resultant vector—Sum vector of two or more vectors. Root mean square velocity—Square root of the average of squared molecular velocities. Rotational equilibrium—Printed definition: “A body having zero angular acceleration.” Scalar quantity—Physical quantity having magnitude only. Scalar product—Product of two vectors resulting in a scalar. Significant figures—Measured or calculated digits reasonably reliable for a quantity.
