p For an ideal gas, the constant-pressure work is ΔWₚ=FΔY=PAΔY=PΔV. From the ideal-gas equation, PΔV=nRΔT (iv). The first law gives ΔQₚ=ΔU+ΔWₚ (v). Substituting ΔQₚ=nCₚΔT and ΔU=nCᵥΔT gives nCₚΔT=nCᵥΔT+nRΔT, and therefore Cₚ−Cᵥ=R (10.10), where the universal gas constant R=8.315 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Point to ponder: A football is inflated in a warm room and used outdoors on a cold day. What happens to it? Why? Example 10.3—Internal energy of nitrogen gas: Find the internal-energy change of 200 g nitrogen heated from 10°C to 30°C at constant volume; Cᵥ=20.815 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Given m=200 g, Tᵢ=10°C+273 K=283 K, T_f=30°C+273 K=303 K, ΔT=20 K; required ΔU. Molar mass M=28 g mol⁻¹, n=m/M=200/28=7.143 mol. All added heat becomes internal energy: ΔU=nCᵥΔT=7.143×20.815×20=2973.6 J.
