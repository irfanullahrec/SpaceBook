p ISOTHERMAL EXPANSION. Slowly reduce pressure on the piston so gas expands and tends to cool; heat flows from the reservoir to keep system temperature equal to reservoir temperature T. The path from (P₁,V₁,T) to (P₂,V₂,T) is a continuous curve, an “isotherm” (Figure 10.7). During expansion the gas does work ΔW; since temperature is constant, ΔU=0. First law: ΔQ=ΔU+ΔW=ΔW. Thus equal heat must enter when gas expands and does external work; when it contracts, work is done on it and equal heat leaves. ADIABATIC PROCESS: no heat enters or leaves the system, so ΔQ=0. A perfectly adiabatic process is ideal; heat flow can be reduced with thick insulating material such as cork/asbestos or by making the process very fast because heat flow takes finite time. Figure 10.8 shows heat insulation and an adiabatic P–V expansion curve.
