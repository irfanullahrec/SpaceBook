The bridge receives periodic impulses from regular steps; if their time period equals the bridge’s natural time period, a dangerously large vibration may result and the bridge may collapse. These examples show a marked increase in amplitude when an external force with time period equal to the body’s natural time period is applied. This is called resonance. It also occurs when applied-force frequency is an integral multiple of the natural frequency. If f₁ is the natural frequency, resonance takes place at f₂ = 2f₁, f₃ = 3f₁, f₄ = 4f₁, …, fₙ = nf₁. EXPERIMENT 1. Figure 7.7 shows simple pendulums A, B, C, D, E, etc., attached to a stretched string/rubber cord between two hooks. Lengths A and B are equal to ℓ; lengths C and D are equal to L. Case (a): if E has the same length as A and B and is set vibrating perpendicular to the plane of the paper, after some time A and B start vibrating automatically because they have the same length and frequencies, while C and D remain at rest because their lengths differ from E. Case (b): if E is made equal in length to C and D and set vibrating, C and D start vibrating while A and B remain at rest. RADIO AND RESONANCE. Tuning a radio is an example of electrical resonance. Turning the knob changes the natural frequency of the receiver’s electric circuit to equal the transmission frequency of a radio station.
