p 10.8.3 RELATION BETWEEN MOLAR SPECIFIC HEAT AT CONSTANT PRESSURE AND AT CONSTANT VOLUME. Heating a gas at constant volume does no work on the surroundings; supplied heat becomes internal energy as molecular kinetic energy and raises temperature (Figure 10.11a). At constant pressure, heating expands the gas and it does work on the surroundings (Figure 10.11b). Since the internal-energy change is the same in both cases, Cₚ > Cᵥ. Figure 10.11 labels: (a) Fixed piston; (b) Moveable piston. At constant volume, ΔQᵥ = nCᵥΔT; volume stays fixed, so ΔWᵥ=0. From the first law ΔQᵥ=ΔU+ΔWᵥ=ΔU (i), hence ΔU=nCᵥΔT (ii).
