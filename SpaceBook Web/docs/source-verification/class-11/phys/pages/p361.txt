p 10.8 MOLAR SPECIFIC HEAT OF A GAS. Specific heat is the heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. Molar specific heat is the heat needed to raise one mole of a gas by 1 K. The heat ΔQ required for n moles and temperature change ΔT is proportional to both: ΔQ = Cₘ n ΔT (10.7), where Cₘ is molar specific heat. Cₘ = (1/n)(ΔQ/ΔT); SI unit: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. The temperature of a gas may be changed under various conditions. The volume may be held constant, pressure may be held constant, or both may vary in a definite way. Thus two specific heats are defined: molar specific heat at constant volume Cᵥ and at constant pressure Cₚ. 10.8.1 CONSTANT-VOLUME MOLAR SPECIFIC HEAT Cᵥ. p FOR YOUR INFORMATION. Thermodynamics applies to living systems. For a body, ΔU=Q−W+food energy; stored energy equals food energy when using the diagram’s accounting. Heat Q leaving the body and work W done by it reduce internal energy, while food intake replaces it (food may be regarded as work done on the body). Plants convert some radiant sunlight energy into stored chemical energy by photosynthesis.
