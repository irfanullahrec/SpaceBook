Vibratory motion is as important as translational and rotational motions. Many phenomena in nature require understanding vibrations and waves. Although large structures such as skyscrapers and bridges appear rigid, they actually vibrate. Architects and engineers take this fact into account. 7.1 OSCILLATIONS. When a particle executes repeated movement about a mean position, it is said to be in harmonic motion; if repeated at regular intervals, it is called periodic motion. Oscillation is a type of harmonic motion, typically periodic, in one or more dimensions. Examples include motion of a spring, a pendulum, or the back-and-forth motion of a swing. 7.2 TERMINOLOGY OF OSCILLATORY MOTION. Figure 7.1(a) shows a simple pendulum. When its bob is displaced to one side and released, it vibrates about its mean position. We use the following terms. (i) VIBRATORY MOTION: the to-and-fro (back-and-forth) motion of a body about the mean position. (ii) VIBRATION: the complete round trip of a body about the mean position. For a pendulum bob, motion from A to B and back from B to A is one vibration, as shown in Figure 7.1(a). (iii) TIME PERIOD: the time required for a body to complete one vibration.
