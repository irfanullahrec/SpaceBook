p Summary of paragraph 1: McWhirter observes that Americans, unlike people almost everywhere else, tend to define and judge everybody by the work they do, especially work performed for pay: Charlie is a doctor, Sam is a carpenter, Mary Ellen is a copywriter at a small ad agency. It is as if by defining how a person earns his or her rent money we validate or reject that person’s existence, and through the job title we evaluate the worth of the life attached. p Summary of paragraph 2: This seems peculiar to the author, because people are not cast in bronze by the job they hold or once held. A retired teacher may spend much volunteer time working with handicapped children or raising money for a social club, yet apparently that does not count: who is Tony? A retired teacher. A laid-off auto worker may pump gas at his cousin’s station or sell encyclopedias on weekends, but who is Larry?
