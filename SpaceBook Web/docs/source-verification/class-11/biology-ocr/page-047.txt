2.5.1 Acylglycerol

Acylglycerol are lipids which are composed of glycerol and fatty acids.
The most common acylglycerol are triglycerides containing one glycero!
molecule and three fatty acids. Glycerol is a three-carbon compound, to ac
carbon a hydroxyl group is attached. Hydroxyl groups are polar and therefore
glycerol is soluble in water. The acid portions of three fatty acids react with three
hydroxyl groups of the glycerol so that a triglycerid and three water molecules are
formed. This reaction is condensation. The triglyceride molecule can be
hydrolyzed into its components i.e. glycerol and three fatty acids.
Triglycerids are stored in animals as fats.

A fatty acid consists of a long hydrocarbon chain with a carboxyl (acid) grouP Si
COOH) at one end. Most of fatty acids in cell contain 16-18 carbon atoms per
molecule. Fatty acids may be saturated or unsaturated.
