You have learnt that vascular plants or 'tracheophytes' have specialised tissue
termed xylem and phloem, for conducting water (plus solutes) and organic nutrients
respectively. Let’s discuss these tissues in detail.

Xylem cells are elongated and connected end to end to form a tubular Water.
transport system throughout the plant, continuously replacing the large amount of
water lost by transpiration, water that is essential for both photosynthesis and 1,
maintain turgor pressure. The main kinds of xylem are tracheids and vessel elements

Tracheids are elongated cells up to 801m wide with secondary, lignified cell walls.
When mature, tracheids are subject to loss of protoplast (nucleus and cytoplasm) and
hence cell death, creating an open structure for water flow, retarded only by the thin
cellulose barrier of the porous pits through which water flows from cell to cell,
Functional tracheal conduits are surrounded by support and storage cells, including
