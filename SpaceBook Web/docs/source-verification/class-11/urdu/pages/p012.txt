The true principle is what William Dargan said at the Dublin handicrafts exhibition: “Whenever I hear the word freedom, I remember my country and the inhabitants of my city. Hard-working and steady labourers, ploughmen, miners, inventors of new things, discoverers of hidden things, users of machines of heavy lifting, practitioners of every kind of profession, craftsmen, poets, physicians, philosophers and administrators of countries have greatly helped bring man to his present state of progress. One generation has built upon the labour of another and raised it to a high level. Through these fine craftsmen, who are the builders of the edifice of civilisation, one after another without break, an order has arisen in labour, knowledge and skill that was previously in disorder. Gradually the turning of nature has made the present generation heir to this fertile and priceless property that was provided by the cleverness and labour of our forefathers; and that property has not been given to us so that we should merely guard it like a snake over a treasure, but has been given so that we should improve it and leave it to future generations in a more advanced state.
