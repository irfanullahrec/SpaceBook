Because of being in the extreme north of Europe, this country is, in the Europeans’ words, “hot”. Eating flour-based food also makes one sleepy. So from one o’clock in the afternoon till four in the evening business remains almost closed. When lunch time ends at four, the fellows work two hours more, but even in these two hours, under the name of a fifteen-minute tea break, they gossip and rest for half or three-quarters of an hour. How long can a lone man wander, where all can he wander, and the greatest difficulty! The language of the beloved is Turkish; what is the remedy for it? “Italian in Two Days” has been read again and again, indeed is always in the pocket, but is of no special use. So I have pitched my camp on a bridge which, like me, is both lonely and tumult-loving. Lonely because it is alone, one, and lifeless. Tumult-loving because many commotions keep happening around it. This is the most famous bridge of ancient Rome. In English its name is of the same English kind, that is Tiber Bank. When Christianity gained strength it was renamed the Bridge of St Angelo, because behind it there is a magnificent fort named St Angelo.
