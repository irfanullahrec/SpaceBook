Bombs burst all around the battlefield and bullets rained down. A second lieutenant of this regiment advanced calmly towards his target. A shell splinter struck his right arm; a wave of severe pain rose but his pace did not slow — he took a bandage from his bag, wound it round the wound as he walked and kept advancing. He was the only officer who, with his men, captured the left corner of Tronti peak. A fierce battle raged for three hours. Meanwhile 6 FF was ordered to fall back and take up a defensive position behind Chak Bhagwan. All the unit's vehicles had been left across the River Tawi, and there was the problem of carrying back the martyrs and wounded. The wounded second lieutenant proposed to his officer that, if permitted, he would seize an enemy vehicle, but permission was refused. Despite this he set off with two soldiers towards the enemy artillery position. Near it he saw an Indian driver sitting in a Mercedes truck. He went up and ordered him out of the truck. The stars on his shoulder were visible, but in his bloodstained uniform his identity was unclear, so the driver obeyed. The lieutenant quietly told his two men to seat the driver between them, took the steering wheel with one hand and drove towards his own position. The Indian driver cried: "Sir, where are you going?" He replied that he was going the right way. Now the driver realised the officer he had obeyed was Pakistani, not Indian; before he could protest, the young officer's men had tied him up. For this matchless courage the young man was awarded the Sitara-e-Jurat.
