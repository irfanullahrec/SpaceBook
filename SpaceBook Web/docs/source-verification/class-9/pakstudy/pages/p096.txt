The 1962 constitution (constitutional reforms): President Ayub Khan set up a constitution commission under Justice Shahabuddin, which in 1961 presented proposals based on the country's past and present. Interestingly, most were unacceptable to Ayub himself, so he formed another committee, whose constitution he enforced by order on 1 March 1962. Thus after an interim period of about three and three-quarter years the country's second constitution came into force. Provisions and features of the 1962 constitution: • The president had to be a Muslim. • Muslims would be enabled to live their individual and collective lives according to the Quran and Sunnah. • No law would be made against the Quran and Sunnah, and existing laws would be brought into line with them. • Suitable steps would be taken for compulsory teaching of the Quran and Sunnah. • Pakistan would be a welfare state providing better working hours, education, medical facilities, bread, clothing and shelter. • A society free of interest, adultery, gambling and alcohol would be established. • Better management of Zakat, Auqaf and mosques was promised. • The government was told to build better relations with Islamic countries. • Fundamental rights were not mentioned at first but were added by an amendment. • A presidential system was introduced, with the president elected for five years. • Pakistan was a federation of two provinces, East and West Pakistan. • Urdu and Bengali were national languages, with English continuing as the official language. • The independence of the judiciary was guaranteed, and a unicameral legislature, the National Assembly, was set up with 156 members divided equally between East and West Pakistan, six seats being reserved for women. • The president and members of the national and provincial assemblies would be elected by an electoral college of B.D. members. || ۵. د ۱۹۶۲ اساسي قانون (اساسي اصلاحات): ولسمشر ایوب خان د قاضي شهاب الدین په مشرۍ یو اساسي کمیسیون جوړ کړ چې په ۱۹۶۱ کې یې وړاندیزونه وړاندې کړل. په زړه پورې دا وه چې ډېری
