
### 1.1 Introduction to Biology & Major Divisions [حیاتیات کا تعارف اور بنیادی شعبہ جات]
[0] Have you ever wondered about the beauty of things in the world! They are not only beautiful but fascinating too. They are fascinating especially because of the variation in their size, shape, colour, eating habits, habitats etc. Human beings have always been interested in observing and studying living organisms. The history of such study is perhaps as old as the history of mankind.
[1] In this unit, we will study about the branch of science that explores living things i.e. biology and its relationship with other branches of science and also the levels of organization in living organisms.
[2] Biology is the science of life. The word 'Biology' is derived from two Greek words: 'Bio' meaning 'life' and 'Logos' meaning 'to study' or 'thought/reasoning'. In 1736, the Swedish scientist Carl Linnaeus for the first time used the word biology.
[3] Biology is divided into three major divisions:
[4] 1. Botany: It is the scientific study of plants, covering their physiology, structure, genetics, ecology, distribution, and economic importance.
[5] 2. Zoology: It is the scientific study of animals, including their structure, embryology, evolution, classification, habits, and distribution.
[6] 3. Microbiology: It is the study of microscopic organisms (microorganisms), including viruses, bacteria, protozoa, and microscopic fungi.

### 1.2 Specialized Branches of Biology [حیاتیات کی خصوصی شاخیں]
[0] Biology is a fast-growing field of science. Therefore, it is divided into many specialised branches for better understanding and convenience:
[1] 1. Morphology: The study of the external form, shape, and structure of organisms.
[2] 2. Anatomy: The study of the internal structures of organisms revealed by dissection.
[3] 3. Histology: The microscopic study of the composition, structure, and functions of plant and animal tissues.
[4] 4. Physiology: Deals with the normal functions of living organisms and their parts, including physical and chemical processes.
[5] 5. Embryology: Deals with the embryo and the phenomena involved in its formation and development into a new independent organism.
[6] 6. Taxonomy: The science of naming, describing, and classifying organisms into groups and subgroups based on shared characteristics.
[7] 7. Cell Biology (Cytology): The study of cell structure, function, organelles, and cell division.
[8] 8. Palaeontology: The study of fossils of ancient organisms preserved in rock strata. Plant fossil study is Paleobotany; animal fossil study is Paleozoology.
[9] 9. Immunology: Deals with the body's immune system of defence against invading disease-causing pathogens and foreign antigens.
[10] 10. Entomology: The scientific study of insects, their morphology, lifecycles, and ecological impacts.
[11] 11. Genetics: The study of genes, heredity, and the variation of inherited characteristics from parents to offspring.
[12] 12. Biotechnology: The technological exploitation of living organisms and biological systems to manufacture useful products (e.g., insulin, vaccines, biogas).
[13] 13. Environmental Biology (Ecology): The study of inter-relationships between organisms and their physical and biological environments.
[14] 14. Parasitology: Deals with parasites, their modes of transmission, host-parasite interactions, and pathogenic effects.
[15] 15. Socio-biology: Investigates the biological basis of social behaviour in animals (e.g., humans, honeybees, ants, primates) and cooperative colony organization.
[16] 16. Pharmacology: The scientific study of drugs, medication mechanisms, drug delivery, synthesis, and therapeutic actions on living systems.

### 1.3 Linkage of Biology with other Fields of Study [حیاتیات کا دیگر سائنسی علوم سے تعلق (Interdisciplinary Sciences)]
[0] Science is an interconnected body of knowledge. Living things cannot be comprehended in isolation without the laws of physics, chemistry, mathematics, and geography. Interdisciplinary fields include:
[1] 1.3.1 Biophysics: An interdisciplinary science that applies the principles and techniques of physics to understand biological phenomena and mechanisms. Examples include the physics of light absorption and energy excitation in chlorophyll during photosynthesis, fluid mechanics in human blood flow, and the lever mechanics of limbs and joints.
[2] 1.3.2 Biochemistry: Concerned with chemical compounds and chemical reactions occurring in living organisms. It involves the structural analysis of biomolecules (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids) and metabolic pathways like glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and photosynthesis.
[3] 1.3.3 Biogeography: The study of the spatial distribution of biological species and ecosystems across geological space and through ecological time. It explains why polar bears inhabit exclusively the Arctic, why kangaroos are indigenous to Australia, and why malaria vectors flourish in damp, tropical zones.
[4] 1.3.4 Biostatistics (Biometry): The application of statistical and mathematical methodologies to the design, analysis, and interpretation of biological research experiments and clinical trials.
[5] 1.3.5 Bio-economics: The integration of biological data with economic analysis. Biologists evaluate the cost-benefit ratio, yield per acre, and economic viability of agricultural crops, fish farming, dairy breeding, and disease mitigation projects.

### 1.4 Careers in Biology [حیاتیات میں مستقبل کے پیشے (Careers in Biology)]
[0] Studying biology opens up rewarding and vital professions dedicated to human welfare, health, food security, and conservation:
[1] 1.4.1 Medicine and Surgery: Medicine diagnoses and treats human diseases medically; surgery repairs, replaces, or removes damaged organs or tissues (e.g., kidney transplantation, appendectomy). Available via MBBS degree after Higher Secondary School Certificate (F.Sc Pre-Medical).
[2] 1.4.2 Fisheries: Professional management of fish hatcheries, nutritional regimens, breeding programs, and habitat quality to enhance aquatic food production. Pursued through bachelor's or master's degrees in Zoology or Fisheries Sciences.
[3] 1.4.3 Agriculture: Optimizing crop production (wheat, rice, maize, cotton) and livestock farming. Agricultural universities in Pakistan train professionals to work in research departments, seed corporations, veterinary firms, and fertilizer industries.
[4] 1.4.4 Animal Science (Animal Husbandry): Focuses on the breeding, nutrition, health, and welfare of domesticated livestock (cattle, sheep, poultry) to maximize meat, milk, and wool production.
[5] 1.4.5 Horticulture: The art and science of cultivating garden crops, ornamental flora, landscaping, and orchard fruit trees to improve nutritional and aesthetic value.
[6] 1.4.6 Forestry: Managing, conserving, and expanding natural forests and timber plantations to prevent soil erosion, regulate precipitation, and supply timber.
[7] 1.4.7 Farming: Establishment of modern poultry farms, dairy farms, fish farms, and fruit orchards utilizing scientific feed formulation, artificial insemination, and automated monitoring.

### 1.5 The Five Kingdoms of Living Organisms [جانداروں کے پانچ بنیادی عالم (Five Kingdoms)]
[0] All living things on Earth are classified into five major kingdoms based on cellular structure, body complexity, and mode of nutrition:
[1] 1.5.1 Kingdom Prokaryotae (Monera): Unicellular prokaryotic organisms lacking membrane-bound nuclei and membrane-bound organelles. Genetic material is naked DNA in the nucleoid. Examples: Bacteria, Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae).
[2] 1.5.2 Kingdom Protista (Protoctista): Unicellular or simple colonial/filamentous eukaryotes without specialized tissue differentiation. Examples: Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, Chlamydomonas, and kelps.
[3] 1.5.3 Kingdom Fungi: Eukaryotic, multicellular (except unicellular yeast) heterotrophs with chitinous cell walls. They exhibit absorptive heterotrophic nutrition by secreting extracellular enzymes. Examples: Mushrooms, Rhizopus (black bread mold), Penicillium, Yeast.
[4] 1.5.4 Kingdom Plantae: Eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic organisms that synthesize organic food via photosynthesis using chlorophyll contained in chloroplasts. Cell walls are made of cellulose. Examples: Mosses, Ferns, Pine, Mustard, Mango.
[5] 1.5.5 Kingdom Animalia: Eukaryotic, multicellular, ingestive heterotrophs lacking cell walls. Most are motile, possess sensory organs, and exhibit complex tissue/organ systems. Examples: Sponges, Earthworms, Insects, Fish, Frogs, Birds, Humans.

### 1.6 The Holy Quran and Biological Science [قرآنِ حکیم اور حیاتیاتی سائنس]
[0] The Holy Quran contains profound verses revealing biological truths, embryonic stages, water as the foundation of living matter, and the vast diversity of life centuries before modern experimental biology discovered them:
[1] 1. Origin of Life from Water:
[2] وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ حَيٍّ ۖ أَفَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
[3] 'And We made from water every living thing. Will they not then believe?' (Surah Al-Anbiyah: 30) - Protoplasm is 70% to 90% water, validating that all biochemical vitality depends upon an aqueous medium.
[4] 2. Embryological Stages of Man:
[5] ثُمَّ خَلَقْنَا النُّطْفَةَ عَلَقَةً فَخَلَقْنَا الْعَلَقَةَ مُضْغَةً فَخَلَقْنَا الْمُضْغَةَ عِظَامًا فَكَسَوْنَا الْعِظَامَ لَحْمًا ثُمَّ أَنشَأْنَاهُ خَلْقًا آخَرَ ۚ فَتَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ
[6] 'Then fashioned We the drop a clot, then fashioned We the clot a little lump, then fashioned We the little lump bones, then clothed the bones with flesh, and then produced it as another creation. So blessed be Allah the Best of Creators!' (Surah Al-Mominoon: 14) - Accurately categorizes zygote (nutfah), blastocyst/implantation (alaqah), somite-stage embryo (mudghah), osteogenesis, and myogenesis.
[7] 3. Locomotion and Classification of Animals:
[8] وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَ كُلَّ دَابَّةٍ مِّن مَّاءٍ ۖ فَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ بَطْنِهِ وَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ رِجْلَيْنِ وَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ أَرْبَعٍ ۚ يَخْلُقُ اللَّهُ مَا يَشَاءُ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
[9] 'Allah hath created every animal of water. Of them is (a kind) that goeth upon its belly and (a kind) that goeth upon two legs and (a kind) that goeth upon four. Allah createth what He will. Lo! Allah is able to do all things.' (Surah Al-Noor: 45) - References reptiles (crawlers), bipeds (humans, birds), and tetrapods (quadrupeds).
[10] 4. Botanical Germination and Sprouting:
[11] إِنَّ اللَّهَ فَالِقُ الْحَبِّ وَالنَّوَىٰ ۖ يُخْرِجُ الْحَيَّ مِنَ الْمَيِّتِ وَمُخْرِجُ الْمَيِّتِ مِنَ الْحَيِّ
[12] 'Lo! Allah (it is) who splitteth the grain of corn and the date-stone (for sprouting). He bringeth forth the living from the dead, and is the bringer-forth of the dead from the living.' (Surah Al-Ana'm: 95) - Beautiful description of seed imbibition, rupture of the testa, and seedling germination.
[13] 5. Medicinal Properties of Honey & Insect Architecture:
[14] وَأَوْحَىٰ رَبُّكَ إِلَى النَّحْلِ ... يَخْرُجُ مِن بُطُونِهَا شَرَابٌ مُّخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهُ فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِّلنَّاسِ
[15] 'And thy Lord inspired the bee ... There cometh forth from their bellies a drink diverse of hues, wherein is healing for mankind.' (Surah Al-Nahal: 68-69) - Validates honey's broad-spectrum antibacterial, antioxidant, and therapeutic properties.

### 1.7 Contributions of Eminent Muslim Scientists [نامور مسلم سائنسدانوں کے حیاتیاتی و طبی کارنامے]
[0] From the 8th to the 15th centuries AD, Muslim scholars made groundbreaking pioneer observations and wrote authoritative classical treatises that shaped global biology and medicine:
[1] 1. Jabir Bin Hayyan (721–815 AD): Born in Iran, practiced in Iraq. Founder of experimental chemistry; authored foundational biological texts 'Al-Nabatat' (Treatise on Plants) and 'Al-Hayawan' (Treatise on Animals).
[2] 2. Abdul Malik Asmai (740–828 AD): Born in Basra. Renowned zoologist who conducted detailed morphological and anatomical observations on domesticated animals. Authored 'Al-Kheil' (The Horse), 'Al-Ibil' (The Camel), 'Al-Sha' (The Sheep), 'Al-Wahoosh' (Wild Animals), and 'Khalaq-ul-Insan' (Human Anatomy and Physiology).
[3] 3. Bu Ali Sina (Avicenna, 980–1037 AD): Born near Bukhara. A monumental polymath revered in the West as the 'Prince of Physicians'. His masterwork 'Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb' (The Canon of Medicine) served as the standard medical textbook in European universities for over five centuries.
[4] 4. Abu-Usman Umar Al-Jahiz (776–868 AD): Authored the celebrated 7-volume encyclopedia 'Kitab al-Hayawan' (Book of Animals). Noted the seasonal migration of fish in the River Tigris and described the complex social communication and organization of ants.
[5] 5. Al-Farabi (870–950 AD): Celebrated philosopher and biologist who wrote 'Kitab-ul-Nabatat' (Book on Plants) and 'Kitab-ul-Hayawanat' (Book on Animals).
[6] 6. Abul Qasim Al-Zahrawi (Albucasis, 936–1004 AD): Eminent surgeon of Muslim Spain (Cordoba). Known as the father of modern surgery. Invented over 200 surgical instruments, pioneered lithotrity (bladder stone extraction), and wrote the 30-volume medical compendium 'Al-Tasrif'.
[7] 7. Ibn-ul-Haitham (Alhazen, 965–1039 AD): Outstanding physicist and ophthalmologist. In his seminal work 'Kitab-ul-Manazir' (Book of Optics), he refuted the erroneous Greek intromission theory of vision and correctly identified the retina as the photoreceptive seat of sight.
[8] 8. Ali Bin Isa (Jesu Haly, 10th Century AD): Famous ophthalmologist in Baghdad. Wrote 'Tadhkirat al-Kahhalin' (Notebook of the Oculists), describing 130 specific eye ailments and their pharmacological/surgical remedies.
[9] 9. Ibn-ul-Nafees (1213–1288 AD): Renowned physician of Damascus and Cairo. The first in medical history to discover and describe the pulmonary circulation of blood (transit of blood from right ventricle to lungs for oxygenation and then to left atrium), three centuries before William Harvey.

### 1.8 Levels of Biological Organization [حیاتیاتی تنظیم کے درجات (Levels of Biological Organization)]
[0] Living organisms show hierarchical structural complexity from subatomic particles to the global biosphere:
[1] 1. Subatomic and Atomic Level: All matter is composed of subatomic particles (protons, neutrons, electrons) forming atoms of chemical elements. Out of 92 naturally occurring elements, 16 are Bio-elements. Six major bio-elements (Oxygen 65%, Carbon 18%, Hydrogen 10%, Nitrogen 3%, Calcium 2%, Phosphorus 1%) make up 99% of total body mass. Ten trace bio-elements (Potassium, Sulfur, Chlorine, Sodium, Magnesium, Iron, Copper, Manganese, Zinc, Iodine) make up the remaining 1%.
[2] 2. Molecular Level: Atoms bond covalently or ionically into biomolecules. Micromolecules have low molecular weights (water, glucose, amino acids); Macromolecules have high molecular weights (starch, glycogen, proteins, DNA).
[3] 3. Organelle and Cell Level: Biomolecules assemble into cellular organelles (mitochondria for ATP synthesis, ribosomes for protein synthesis). An organized collection of organelles forms the Cell, the basic structural and functional unit of life.
[4] 4. Tissue Level: In multicellular organisms, groups of similar cells specialized to perform a common function form a tissue. Examples: Plant epidermal tissue, xylem, and phloem; Animal epithelial tissue, connective tissue, muscular tissue, and nervous tissue.
[5] 5. Organ and Organ System Level: Different tissues working collaboratively form an Organ (e.g., Stomach composed of secretor epithelial tissue and muscular tissue). A collection of organs performing coordinated physiological functions forms an Organ System (e.g., Digestive System comprising mouth, stomach, pancreas, liver, and intestines). Note: Plants exhibit less complex organ systems than animals.
[6] 6. Organism Level (Individual): All organ systems work in harmony to constitute an individual organism (e.g., a human running coordinates muscular, respiratory, circulatory, and nervous systems).
[7] 7. Population Level: A group of organisms of the same species living together in the same geographical area at the same time (e.g., human population of Pakistan in 2026).
[8] 8. Community Level: An assemblage of diverse populations of different species interacting in a common habitat (e.g., a forest community composed of trees, shrubs, birds, fungi, insects, and deer).
[9] 9. Biosphere Level (Ecosystem): The global zone of Earth inhabited by living organisms, encompassing atmosphere, lithosphere, and hydrosphere.

### 1.9 Multicellular Examples: Mustard Plant & Frog [کثیر خلوی جانداروں کی مثالیں: سرسوں کا پودا اور مینڈک]
[0] To understand multicellular organization in plants and animals, the textbook studies two classical model organisms:
[1] 1. Brassica campestris (Mustard Plant / سرسوں):
[2] • Sown in winter and produces flowers and seeds at the end of winter.
[3] • The plant body is divided into two distinct organ sets:
[4]   a) Vegetative organs: Roots, stems, and leaves which play no role in sexual reproduction.
[5]   b) Reproductive organs: Flowers which produce fruits and seeds through sexual reproduction.
[6] • Used as a vegetable (leaves) and extracted for mustard oil (seeds).
[7] 2. Rana tigrina (Frog / مینڈک):
[8] • Exhibits complete multicellular organ system organization.
[9] • The body consists of organ systems including digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous, excretory, and reproductive systems.
[10] • Each organ system is composed of specific organs (e.g., heart, lungs, kidneys) made of specialized tissues (epithelial, muscular, nervous, connective).
