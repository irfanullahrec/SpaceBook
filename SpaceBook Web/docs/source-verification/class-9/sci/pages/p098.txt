p The bacteria thrive in the respiratory passages. Here they produce toxins that damage the tiny hairs (cilia); these cilia help to remove particulate matter and cellular debris that are normally introduced into the airways with each breath. This results in an increased inflammation of the respiratory passages and the typical dry cough characterized by severe coughing spells that end in a "whooping" sound when the person breathes in. || ↑ || ↑
