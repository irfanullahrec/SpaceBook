q i | Describe the octet rule in terms of noble gas configurations and stability. || نوبل گیس کی ترتیب اور استحکام کے لحاظ سے آکٹیٹ کے اصول کی وضاحت کریں۔ || د نجیبه ګاز د ترتیب او ثبات له مخې د اوکټېټ قاعده تشریح کړئ.
qsolution 1. Definition of Octet Rule:
ss The octet rule (or Rule of Eight) states that atoms tend to lose, gain, or share valence electrons in order to acquire a stable electronic configuration consisting of eight electrons in their outermost valence shell, identical to the electronic configuration of the nearest noble gas (Group VIIIA).
ss 2. Noble Gas Electronic Configurations and Inherent Stability:
ss Except for helium (He, 1s2, duplet configuration of 2 electrons), all noble gases (Neon: 2,8; Argon: 2,8,8; Krypton: 2,8,18,8; Xenon; Radon) possess completely filled outermost s and p subshells containing exactly eight valence electrons (ns2 np6).
ss This completely filled octet represents a state of maximum electrostatic symmetry and minimum potential energy. Because their valence shells are full, noble gases do not readily gain, lose, or share electrons and remain monoatomic and chemically inert under normal conditions.
ss 3. How Other Atoms Achieve Stability:
ss Isolated atoms of representative elements (groups IA through VIIA) have incomplete valence shells (1 to 7 electrons). To lower their energy and achieve stability, they undergo chemical bonding via three pathways:
ss • By losing electrons (Metals): E.g., Na (2,8,1) ──> Na+ (2,8) + e- (Neon configuration).
ss • By gaining electrons (Non-metals): E.g., Cl (2,8,7) + e- ──> Cl- (2,8,8) (Argon configuration).
ss • By sharing electrons (Covalent bonding): E.g., two Cl atoms share one electron pair (Cl2) so that both attain 8 valence electrons.
ss Thus, the drive to achieve noble gas octet configuration is the fundamental driving force behind chemical bonding.
endsolution

(b) د دوو عناصرو ترمنځ د اړیکې ایوني یا کووالنټ ماهیت په ټاکلو کې الکترونګاتیویټي څنګه کارېږي؟
qsolution (a) Difference between Ionic and Covalent Bonding:
ss 1. Mechanism of Formation:
ss • Ionic Bond: Formed by the complete transfer of one or more electrons from a metallic atom (electropositive) to a non-metallic atom (electronegative).
ss Example: Formation of Sodium Chloride (NaCl). Sodium (2,8,1) transfers its 1 valence electron to Chlorine (2,8,7), producing Na+ (2,8) and Cl- (2,8,8). Electrostatic attraction between Na+ and Cl- forms the ionic crystal.
ss • Covalent Bond: Formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between two non-metallic atoms of similar or moderate electronegativity.
ss Example: Formation of Chlorine molecule (Cl2). Each Cl atom (2,8,7) shares 1 electron with the other, forming a single shared electron pair (Cl─Cl).
ss 2. Nature of Units:
ss • Ionic compounds exist as three-dimensional crystalline lattices of cations and anions (no discrete molecules).
ss • Covalent compounds exist as discrete, individual molecules.
ss 3. Physical Properties:
ss • Ionic compounds have high melting/boiling points and conduct electricity in molten/solution states.
ss • Covalent compounds typically have lower melting/boiling points and are non-electrolytes.
ss (b) Role of Electronegativity in Determining Bond Character:
ss Electronegativity (EN) is the measure of the ability of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself. The difference in electronegativity (ΔEN) between the two bonded atoms determines the nature of the bond:
ss 1. If ΔEN > 1.7: The bond is predominantly Ionic. One atom has a far stronger attraction for electrons, resulting in complete electron transfer (e.g. NaCl: 3.0 - 0.9 = 2.1 > 1.7).
ss 2. If 0.5 <= ΔEN <= 1.7: The bond is Polar Covalent. Electrons are shared unequally, producing partial positive (δ+) and partial negative (δ-) poles (e.g. HCl: 3.0 - 2.1 = 0.9; H2O: 3.5 - 2.1 = 1.4).
ss 3. If ΔEN < 0.5: The bond is Non-Polar Covalent. Electrons are shared equally between the two atoms (e.g. H2: ΔEN = 0.0; CH4: 2.5 - 2.1 = 0.4).
endsolution

q iv | Explain why most metals are malleable and ductile but ionic crystals are not. || وضاحت کریں کہ زیادہ تر دھاتیں قابلِ چادر سازی اور تار سازی کیوں ہیں، مگر آئنک قلم کیوں نہیں؟ || تشریح کړئ چې ډېری فلزونه ولې د ټکولو او غځولو وړ دي، خو ایوني بلورونه نه دي؟
qsolution 1. Structure and Deformation in Metals (Malleable and Ductile):
ss • In metallic bonding, positive metal ions (kernels) are arranged in uniform layers surrounded by a continuous, mobile 'sea' of delocalized electrons.
ss • The metallic bond is non-directional and flexible. When an external mechanical force (hammering or stretching) is applied to a metal, layers of metal ions can slide smoothly over one another into new positions without breaking the metallic bond.
ss • As the layers slide, the mobile delocalized electrons instantly adjust and continue to maintain strong electrostatic attraction with the metal ions in their new positions.
ss • Consequently, metals can be beaten into thin sheets (malleability) and drawn into long wires (ductility) without shattering.
ss 2. Structure and Deformation in Ionic Crystals (Brittle):
ss • In an ionic crystal lattice (such as NaCl), cations and anions are arranged in a rigid, highly ordered alternating pattern held by strong, directional electrostatic forces.
ss • When an external mechanical stress is applied, one layer of ions is forced to slide slightly past adjacent layers.
ss • This displacement brings ions of like charges into direct alignment with each other (e.g. positive ion next to positive ion, negative ion next to negative ion).
ss • Intense electrostatic repulsion between like charges immediately forces the crystal lattice apart, causing the crystal to shatter cleanly along its cleavage planes.
ss • Thus, ionic solids are hard and brittle, lacking malleability and ductility.
endsolution
q v | a. What is the meaning of the term polar, as applied to chemical bonding? b. Distinguish between polar covalent and non polar covalent bonds. || a. کیمیائی بند سازی میں قطبی اصطلاح کا کیا مطلب ہے؟ b. قطبی اور غیر قطبی اشتراکی بندوں میں فرق کریں۔ || a. په کیمیاوي اړیکو کې د قطبي اصطلاح مانا څه ده؟ b. د قطبي او غیرقطبي کووالنټ اړیکو ترمنځ توپیر وکړئ.
qsolution (a) Meaning of the Term 'Polar' in Chemical Bonding:
ss In chemical bonding, the term 'polar' refers to the uneven, asymmetrical distribution of electrical charge across a bond or molecule. When two bonded atoms have different electronegativities, the more electronegative atom pulls the shared pair of electrons closer to itself. This unequal sharing creates partial electrical charges (a dipole): a partial negative charge (δ-) on the more electronegative atom and an equal partial positive charge (δ+) on the less electronegative atom. A bond possessing such distinct positive and negative poles is called a polar bond.
ss (b) Distinction between Polar Covalent and Non-Polar Covalent Bonds:
ss 1. Definition:
ss • Non-Polar Covalent Bond: Formed by equal sharing of electrons between atoms of identical or nearly identical electronegativities.
ss • Polar Covalent Bond: Formed by unequal sharing of electrons between atoms of significantly different electronegativities.
ss 2. Electronegativity Difference (ΔEN):
ss • Non-Polar: ΔEN is 0.0 to 0.4 (or identical atoms).
ss • Polar: ΔEN is 0.5 to 1.7.
ss 3. Charge Distribution:
ss • Non-Polar: Electron cloud is completely symmetrical; no partial electrical poles (δ+/δ-) are created.
ss • Polar: Electron cloud is asymmetrical and shifted toward the more electronegative atom; permanent partial charges (δ+ and δ-) develop.
ss 4. Dipole Moment:
ss • Non-Polar: Dipole moment is zero (μ = 0).
ss • Polar: Possesses a measurable net dipole moment (μ > 0).
ss 5. Solubility:
ss • Non-Polar: Soluble in non-polar solvents (e.g. benzene, CCl4), insoluble in water.
ss • Polar: Soluble in polar solvents (e.g. water, alcohols).
ss 6. Examples:
ss • Non-Polar: H2 (H─H), Cl2 (Cl─Cl), O2 (O=O), N2 (N≡N), CH4.
ss • Polar: HCl (Hδ+─Clδ-), H2O, NH3, HF.
endsolution
