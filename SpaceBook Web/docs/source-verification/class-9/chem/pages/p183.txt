qsolution • True Solutions: Solute particles (ions, small molecules) are extremely tiny (0.01 - 1 nm), which is much smaller than the wavelength of visible light (~400-700 nm). They cannot scatter light rays, a
ss • Colloids: Dispersed particles have intermediate sizes (1 - 1000 nm), comparable to the wavelength of light. They scatter visible light in all directions, illuminating the beam's path (Tyndall Effect).
ss • Suspensions: Suspended particles are too large (>1000 nm); instead of scattering light homogeneously, they reflect, absorb, or completely block the light beam, rendering the suspension opaque.

q i | Define solution. Explain types of solution on the basis of states of matter. || محلول کی تعریف کریں۔ مادّے کی حالتوں کی بنیاد پر محلول کی اقسام واضح کریں۔ || محلول تعریف کړئ. د مادې د حالتونو پر بنسټ د محلول ډولونه تشریح کړئ.
qsolution 1. Definition of Solution:
ss A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances whose composition can be varied within certain limits. It consists of a solute (component present in smaller amount) dissolved in a solvent (component present in larger amount).
ss 2. Nine Types of Solutions Based on Physical States:
ss A. Gaseous Solutions (Solvent is Gas):
ss 1. Gas in Gas: Oxygen and other gases dissolved in Nitrogen (e.g. Air).
ss 2. Liquid in Gas: Liquid water droplets dispersed in air (e.g. Fog, mist, humidity).
ss 3. Solid in Gas: Solid carbon smoke particles dispersed in air (e.g. Smoke, auto exhaust).
ss B. Liquid Solutions (Solvent is Liquid):
ss 4. Gas in Liquid: Carbon dioxide gas dissolved in water (e.g. Carbonated soft drinks, soda water).
ss 5. Liquid in Liquid: Alcohol dissolved in water (e.g. Ethanol in water, vinegar).
ss 6. Solid in Liquid: Solid salt or sugar dissolved in water (e.g. Sea water, brine, syrup).
ss C. Solid Solutions (Solvent is Solid):
ss 7. Gas in Solid: Hydrogen gas adsorbed in solid palladium metal (H2/Pd).
ss 8. Liquid in Solid: Liquid mercury dissolved in solid silver or tin (e.g. Dental amalgams for tooth fillings).
ss 9. Solid in Solid: Zinc dissolved in copper (e.g. Brass alloy), Tin in copper (Bronze alloy), or 92.5% silver with 7.5% copper (Sterling silver).
endsolution
q ii | (a) Discuss the solubility of a substance. (b) Explain the factors that are responsible for the solubility of a substance. || (a) مادّے کی حل پذیری بیان کریں۔ (b) مادّے کی حل پذیری کے ذمہ دار عوامل واضح کریں۔ || (a) د مادې حل کېدنه وڅېړئ. (b) د مادې د حل کېدنې مسئول لاملونه تشریح کړئ.
qsolution (a) Discussion of Solubility:
ss Solubility is defined as the maximum mass of solute in grams that can dissolve in 100 grams of a given solvent to establish a saturated solution in dynamic equilibrium at a specified temperature.
ss Solubility = [Mass of solute (g) / Mass of solvent (g)] × 100.
ss (b) Factors Responsible for Solubility:
ss 1. Nature of Solute and Solvent ('Like Dissolves Like'):
ss • Polar and ionic solutes dissolve readily in polar solvents (e.g. NaCl, sugar in water) because strong electrostatic ion-dipole or dipole-dipole attractions overcome solute lattice energies.
ss • Non-polar solutes dissolve in non-polar solvents (e.g. grease in petrol/CCl4).
ss • Non-polar substances do not dissolve in polar water due to lack of compatible attractive forces.
ss 2. Effect of Temperature:
ss • Endothermic dissolution (solute absorbs heat upon dissolving, ΔH > 0): Solubility increases with temperature (e.g. KNO3, CaCl2, Pb(NO3)2).
ss • Exothermic dissolution (solute releases heat upon dissolving, ΔH < 0): Solubility decreases with temperature (e.g. Ce2(SO4)3, Li2CO3, CaO).
ss • Nearly constant solubility: NaCl and KBr.
ss • Discontinuous curve: Na2SO4·10H2O transforms to anhydrous Na2SO4 at 32.4°C, where solubility reaches its peak.
ss • Gases in liquids: Solubility of all gases decreases with increasing temperature.
ss 3. Effect of Pressure:
ss • Solids and liquids: Pressure has negligible effect because liquids and solids are practically incompressible.
ss • Gases in liquids: Solubility of gases is directly proportional to the applied pressure (Henry's Law), as seen in pressurized soda bottles.
endsolution
q iii | (a) What is the difference between a concentrated and a dilute solution? Give examples of each. (b) Differentiate between unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated solutions. || (a) مرتکز اور رقیق محلول میں کیا فرق ہے؟ ہر ایک کی مثالیں دیں۔ (b) غیر سیرشدہ، سیرشدہ اور فوق سیرشدہ محلول میں فرق کریں۔ || (a) د غلیظ او رقیق محلول توپیر څه دی؟ د هر یوه بېلګې ورکړئ. (b) د غیرمشبوع، مشبوع او فوق مشبوع محلولونو ترمنځ توپیر وکړئ.
qsolution (a) Difference between Concentrated and Dilute Solutions:
ss • Dilute Solution: A solution that contains a relatively small amount of dissolved solute compared to the volume of solvent. Example: 5 g of sugar in 100 cm^3 of water, or 0.05 M HCl.
ss • Concentrated Solution: A solution that contains a relatively large amount of dissolved solute in the same volume of solvent. Example: 40 g of sugar in 100 cm^3 of water, or 12.0 M concentrated HCl.
ss (b) Differentiation between Unsaturated, Saturated, and Supersaturated Solutions:
ss 1. Unsaturated Solution:
ss • Contains less solute than the maximum amount capable of dissolving at that temperature.
ss • Capacity: Can dissolve additional solute upon stirring.
ss • Seed crystal test: A dropped crystal dissolves completely.
ss 2. Saturated Solution:
ss • Contains the exact maximum amount of dissolved solute in dynamic equilibrium with undissolved solute at a given temperature.
ss • Capacity: Cannot dissolve any further solute at that temperature.
ss • Seed crystal test: A dropped crystal remains undissolved and unchanged at the bottom.
ss 3. Supersaturated Solution:
ss • An unstable (meta-stable) solution that contains more dissolved solute than a saturated solution at that temperature (prepared by heating a saturated solution, adding excess solute, and cooling slowly).
ss • Capacity: Excess solute will precipitate out immediately upon disturbance.
ss • Seed crystal test: A dropped seed crystal induces rapid crystallization of all excess dissolved solute.
endsolution
q iv | Describe one way to prove that a mixture of sugar and water is a solution and that a mixture of sand and water is not a solution. || ایک طریقہ بیان کریں جس سے ثابت ہو کہ چینی اور پانی کا آمیزہ محلول ہے، مگر ریت اور پانی کا آمیزہ محلول نہیں۔ || یوه لاره بیان کړئ چې ثابته کړي د بورې او اوبو مخلوط محلول دی، خو د شګې او اوبو مخلوط محلول نه دی.
qsolution To prove experimentally that sugar + water is a true solution and sand + water is a suspension (not a solution), perform the following two laboratory tests:
ss 1. Filtration Test:
ss • Sugar + Water: Pass the mixture through standard filter paper in a funnel. The entire mixture passes through cleanly into the receiving flask as a clear filtrate; no sugar residue remains on the filter paper because sugar molecules are molecularly dissolved (<1 nm).
ss • Sand + Water: Pass the mixture through filter paper. The sand particles (>1000 nm) are completely trapped on the filter paper as residue, while clear water passes through as filtrate.
ss 2. Settling / Stability Test:
ss • Sugar + Water: Leave the mixture undisturbed on a laboratory bench for several days. It remains perfectly transparent and homogeneous throughout; no solute settles to the bottom.
ss • Sand + Water: Leave the mixture undisturbed. Within seconds to minutes, all dense sand particles settle to the bottom by sedimentation, forming two distinct phases.
ss Conclusion: These tests prove that sugar in water is a homogeneous true solution, whereas sand in water is a heterogeneous suspension.
endsolution
q v | Explain the following concentration units. a. Percentage composition b. Molarity || ارتکاز کی درج ذیل اکائیاں واضح کریں۔ a. فیصدی ترکیب b. مولیریٹی || د غلظت لاندې واحدونه تشریح کړئ. a. سلنه‌ییز ترکیب b. مولاریت
qsolution a. Percentage Composition:
ss Percentage composition expresses the number of parts of solute present in 100 parts of solution. It is calculated in four distinct relationships:
ss 1. Percentage Mass by Mass (% m/m): Grams of solute per 100 g of solution.
ss Formula: % m/m = [Mass of solute (g) / Mass of solution (g)] × 100
ss 2. Percentage Mass by Volume (% m/v): Grams of solute per 100 cm^3 of solution.
ss Formula: % m/v = [Mass of solute (g) / Volume of solution (cm^3)] × 100
ss 3. Percentage Volume by Mass (% v/m): Volume in cm^3 of solute per 100 g of solution.
ss Formula: % v/m = [Volume of solute (cm^3) / Mass of solution (g)] × 100
ss 4. Percentage Volume by Volume (% v/v): Volume in cm^3 of solute per 100 cm^3 of solution.
ss Formula: % v/v = [Volume of solute (cm^3) / Volume of solution (cm^3)] × 100
ss b. Molarity (M):
ss Molarity is the standard IUPAC quantitative concentration unit defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved in one cubic decimeter (1 dm^3 or 1 liter) of total solution.
ss • Mathematical Formula:
ss Molarity (M) = Moles of solute / Volume of solution in dm^3
ss Molarity (M) = Mass of solute (g) / [Molar mass of solute (g/mol) × Volume of solution (dm^3)]
ss • Units: mol/dm^3, mol/L, or M (Molar).
ss • Standard Solution Preparation: To prepare 1.0 dm^3 of a 1.0 M NaOH standard solution, accurately weigh 40.0 g of pure NaOH (1 mole), dissolve it in ~500 mL distilled water in a beaker, transfer quantitatively into a 1000 mL volumetric flask, and dilute with distilled water exactly to the 1.0 dm^3 calibration line.
endsolution
