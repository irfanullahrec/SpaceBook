q v | What is the relationship between the atmospheric pressure and boiling point of a liquid? || فضائی دباؤ اور مائع کے نقطۂ ابال میں کیا تعلق ہے؟ || د اتموسفېري فشار او د مایع د اېشېدو د ټکي ترمنځ څه اړیکه ده؟
qsolution The boiling point of a liquid is directly proportional to the external atmospheric pressure. A liquid boils when its internal vapour pressure becomes equal to the external atmospheric pressure:
ss • When atmospheric pressure increases (e.g. inside a pressure cooker), the liquid must reach a higher temperature for its vapour pressure to match external pressure; hence boiling point increases.
ss • When atmospheric pressure decreases (e.g. at high altitudes or in vacuum distillation), vapour pressure equals external pressure at a lower temperature; hence boiling point decreases.
endsolution

q i | Define Boyle's law and verify it experimentally. || بوائل کا قانون بیان اور تجرباتی طور پر تصدیق کریں۔ || د بویل قانون تعریف او په تجربه یې تایید کړئ.
qsolution 1. Statement of Boyle's Law:
ss Robert Boyle (1662) formulated this fundamental gas law:
ss 'The volume of a given mass of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to the applied pressure, provided the temperature remains constant.'
ss 2. Mathematical Expression:
ss V ∝ 1/P (at constant T and n)
ss V = Kb × (1/P) => P × V = Kb (constant)
ss For a gas changing from state 1 (P1, V1) to state 2 (P2, V2):
ss P1V1 = P2V2 = Kb
ss 3. Experimental Verification:
ss • Apparatus: A gas cylinder fitted with a frictionless, airtight movable piston and weights, maintaining constant temperature (Fig. 5.4).
ss • Procedure:
ss a. Enclose a fixed mass of gas at an initial pressure P1 (e.g. 1 atm) and measure volume V1 (e.g. 4.0 dm^3). Product P1 × V1 = 1 × 4 = 4.0 atm·dm^3.
ss b. Double the pressure to P2 = 2 atm by placing additional weights on the piston. The volume reduces to V2 = 2.0 dm^3. Product P2 × V2 = 2 × 2 = 4.0 atm·dm^3.
ss c. Increase pressure to P3 = 4 atm; volume decreases to V3 = 1.0 dm^3. Product P3 × V3 = 4 × 1 = 4.0 atm·dm^3.
ss • Conclusion: In each observation, the product P × V remains constant (Kb = 4.0), which proves and experimentally verifies Boyle's Law.
endsolution
q ii | Differentiate between a. Evaporation and Boiling point. b. Effusion and Diffusion of gases. c. Condensation and Evaporation. || فرق بیان کریں: a. تبخیر اور نقطۂ ابال۔ b. گیسوں کا اخراج اور انتشار۔ c. تکثیف اور تبخیر۔ || توپیر بیان کړئ: a. تبخیر او د اېشېدو ټکی. b. د ګازونو افیوژن او خپرېدل. c. تکاثف او تبخیر.
qsolution a. Difference between Evaporation and Boiling Point:
ss • Occurrence: Evaporation occurs only at the liquid surface; Boiling occurs throughout the entire bulk liquid.
ss • Temperature: Evaporation occurs continuously at all temperatures; Boiling occurs only at a specific fixed boiling point temperature.
ss • Bubbles: No bubble formation occurs during evaporation; Vigorous vapor bubble formation occurs during boiling.
ss • Energy requirement: Evaporation is spontaneous without external heating; Boiling requires continuous external heat supply.
ss b. Difference between Effusion and Diffusion of Gases:
ss • Mechanism: Diffusion is the spontaneous mixing of gas molecules through random motion and collisions across all directions; Effusion is the escape of gas molecules one by one without molecular collisions through a tiny pinhole of molecular dimensions.
ss • Opening/Barrier: Diffusion requires no pinhole/orifice (occurs across open medium); Effusion requires a tiny microscopic hole/aperture.
ss • Examples: Spreading of perfume scent in a room (Diffusion); Escape of gas from a punctured bicycle tyre or sealed onion bag (Effusion).
ss c. Difference between Condensation and Evaporation:
ss • Definition: Evaporation is the phase transition from liquid to gas; Condensation is the phase transition from gas (vapour) to liquid.
ss • Thermal Effect: Evaporation absorbs heat energy and causes cooling; Condensation releases latent heat of condensation and causes warming.
ss • Kinetic Energy: Evaporation involves high-KE molecules escaping the liquid; Condensation involves low-KE vapor molecules losing energy and returning to liquid state.
endsolution
q iii | Define the term allotropy with examples. Explain the three allotropic forms of carbon in detail. || تعددِ اشکال مثالوں سمیت بیان کریں۔ کاربن کی تین الوٹروپی صورتوں کی تفصیل دیں۔ || الوټروپي له بېلګو سره تعریف کړئ. د کاربن درې الوټروپي بڼې په تفصیل تشریح کړئ.
qsolution 1. Definition of Allotropy:
ss Allotropy is the phenomenon in which an element exists in more than one physical form having different physical properties but identical chemical properties. These different forms are called allotropes.
ss 2. Three Crystalline Allotropic Forms of Carbon:
ss (a) Diamond:
ss • Structure: Each carbon atom is sp3 hybridized and covalently bonded to four other carbon atoms in a rigid three-dimensional tetrahedral cubic lattice.
ss • Properties: The hardest known natural substance, very high melting point (>3500°C), high refractive index (sparkles brilliantly), and non-conductor of electricity (all 4 valence electrons are tightly held in covalent bonds, no free electrons).
ss • Uses: Cutting glass, drilling rock, and exquisite jewellery.
ss (b) Graphite:
ss • Structure: Each carbon atom is sp2 hybridized and bonded to three other carbon atoms in flat hexagonal sheets (planar layers). The layers are held together by weak Van der Waals dispersion forces with a spacing of 3.35 Å.
ss • Properties: Soft, slippery to touch, blackish-grey solid; excellent conductor of electricity parallel to sheets due to 1 delocalized mobile pi-electron per carbon atom.
ss • Uses: Dry lubricant in heavy machinery, lead pencil cores, and inert electrodes in electrolysis.
ss (c) Buckyball (Fullerene):
ss • Structure: Discovered in 1985 (e.g. C60 Buckminsterfullerene). Consists of 20 to 100 carbon atoms linked in alternating 12 pentagonal and 20 hexagonal rings to form a hollow soccer-ball cage structure.
ss • Properties: High tensile strength, thermal stability, electron acceptor properties.
ss • Uses: Nanotechnology, high-efficiency semiconductors, superconductors when doped, drug delivery carriers, and advanced lubricants.
endsolution
q iv | What are solids? Differentiate between amorphous and crystalline solids. || ٹھوس کیا ہیں؟ بے شکل اور قلمی ٹھوسوں میں فرق بیان کریں۔ || جامد څه دي؟ د بې‌شکله او بلوري جامدو توپیر بیان کړئ.
qsolution 1. Definition of Solids:
ss Solids represent the state of matter in which constituent particles (atoms, ions, or molecules) are tightly packed in fixed structural arrangements by strong cohesive forces. Solids possess definite shape, definite volume, high rigidity, and incompressibility, with particles exhibiting only vibrational motion.
ss 2. Comprehensive Distinction:
ss • Particle Arrangement:
ss - Crystalline: Regular, highly ordered three-dimensional repeating geometric pattern (long-range order).
ss - Amorphous: Irregular, disordered arrangement lacking long-range repeating order.
ss • Melting Point:
ss - Crystalline: Sharp, precise melting point (e.g. ice melts at 0°C, NaCl melts at 801°C).
ss - Amorphous: No sharp melting point; softens and melts gradually over a broad temperature range.
ss • Cleavage & Geometry:
ss - Crystalline: Cleaves smoothly along specific crystalline planes with definite interfacial angles.
ss - Amorphous: Fractures irregularly with curved, rough surfaces (conchoidal fracture).
ss • Heat of Fusion:
ss - Crystalline: Possesses a definite and characteristic latent heat of fusion.
ss - Amorphous: Lacks a definite latent heat of fusion.
ss • Physical Nature:
ss - Crystalline: Considered 'True solids'.
ss - Amorphous: Considered 'Pseudo-solids' or supercooled liquids.
ss • Examples:
ss - Crystalline: NaCl, Diamond, Graphite, Copper sulphate (CuSO4), Ice, Naphthalene.
ss - Amorphous: Glass, Rubber, Candle wax, Plastic, Butter, Cotton candy.
endsolution
q v | Define Charles's law and verify it graphically and diagrammatically. || چارلس کا قانون بیان اور گراف و خاکے سے تصدیق کریں۔ || د چارلس قانون تعریف او په ګراف او انځور یې تایید کړئ.
qsolution 1. Statement of Charles's Law:
ss Jacques Charles (1787) stated:
ss 'The volume of a given mass of an ideal gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature (in Kelvin), provided the pressure remains constant.'
ss 2. Mathematical Expression:
ss V ∝ T (at constant P and n)
ss V = Kc × T => V / T = Kc (constant)
ss For a gas changing from state 1 (V1, T1) to state 2 (V2, T2):
ss V1 / T1 = V2 / T2 = Kc
ss (Note: T must always be expressed in Kelvin: K = °C + 273).
ss 3. Graphical Verification:
ss • If the volume 'V' of a gas is plotted against absolute temperature 'T' (in Kelvin) on a Cartesian graph at constant pressure, a straight line passing through the origin (0 K) is obtained (Fig. 5.8).
ss • This linear relationship (isobar) confirms that volume expands in exact direct proportion to absolute temperature.
ss 4. Diagrammatic Verification:
ss • Consider a fixed mass of gas in a cylinder fitted with a movable piston under constant atmospheric pressure P (Fig. 5.9):
ss - At initial temperature T1 = 300 K, gas occupies volume V1 = 2 dm^3 (V1/T1 = 2/300 = 0.0066 dm^3/K).
ss - When heated to T2 = 600 K (doubled), the thermal kinetic energy of gas molecules increases; they strike the piston with greater force, pushing it upward until volume expands to V2 = 4 dm^3 (doubled).
ss - Ratio V2/T2 = 4/600 = 0.0066 dm^3/K = constant Kc.
ss • This diagrammatic demonstration verifies that volume expands proportionally with temperature at constant pressure.
endsolution
