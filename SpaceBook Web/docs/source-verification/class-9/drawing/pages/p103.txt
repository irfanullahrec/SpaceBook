### Khat-e-Nastaleeq || خطِ نستعلیق || نستعلیق خط
p Nastaleeq is one of the main script styles used in writing the Perso-Arabic script, and traditionally the predominant style in Persian calligraphy. It was developed in Iran in the 14th and 15th centuries. Nastaleeq has extensively been practiced in Iran, Pakistan and Afghanistan as a form of art. Nastaleeq serves as the preferred style for writing Persian, Kashmiri, and Urdu, and it is often used alongside Naskh for Pashto. Nastaleeq is amongst the most popular calligraphy styles for the Arabic alphabet. It has short verticals with no serifs, and long horizontal strokes. The inventor was Mir 'Ali of Tabriz, the most famous calligrapher of the Timurid period (1402-1502). A cursive script, Nasta'liq was a combination of the Naskh and Ta'liq styles. At the end of 19th century, Persian style was the most popular style of Nasta'liq in sub-continent but within next 30 years, there were three new styles evolved from it. First of them is Nasta'liq Lakhnawi (Developed in Lakhnau, India), second is Dehelwi (Developed in Delhi, India) and third is Nastaleeq Lahori (Developed in Lahore, Pakistan).
