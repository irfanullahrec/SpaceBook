box teach | Do you remember? Regular and irregular verbs
p A verb following the normal pattern of inflection is called a regular verb, e.g. reach – reached – reached.
p A verb that does not follow the normal pattern of inflection is called an irregular verb, e.g. go – went – gone.
endbox
box teach | Do you remember? Transitive and intransitive verbs
p A transitive verb is one that is used with an object: a noun, phrase, or pronoun, e.g. I admire your courage.
p An intransitive verb does not have an object, e.g. The baby was crying.
endbox

qp 2 | Analyze the grammar concepts in Unit 3: Rules for Articles (a, an, the, zero article) and Modal Auxiliary Verbs with detailed rules and examples.

The System of Articles: • Indefinite Article 'A': Used before singular countable nouns starting with a consonant sound (e.g. *a car, a tractor, a university*). • Indefinite Article 'An': Used before singular countable nouns starting with a vowel sound (e.g. *an aeroplane, an hour, an old building*). • Definite Article 'The': Used for specific items, unique entities, or previously mentioned nouns (e.g. *the Quaid, the USB drive I lent you, the blue T-shirt*). • Zero Article (X): Omitted before proper nouns of cities/countries (e.g. *live in Bannu, visit Karachi*), abstract virtues, and general plurals.  2. Modal Auxiliary Verbs and Their Semantic Functions: Modal verbs modify the mood and meaning of main verbs: • Ability: 'Can / Could' → *They can speak English well.* (Equivalent to: *are able to*). • Possibility: 'May / Might' → *It may rain tomorrow.* (Equivalent to: *there is a possibility*). • Obligation / Necessity: 'Must / Have to' → *Must I clean the kitchen?* (Equivalent to: *is it required*). • Prohibition: 'Must not' → *We must not swim when the red flag is flying.* (Equivalent to: *are not allowed to*). • Advice / Recommendation: 'Should / Ought to' → *You should respect your elders.*  Understanding these syntactic rules enables students to construct precise, grammatically flawless sentences in spoken and written English.
qp 3 | Explain the classifications of Regular vs. Irregular Verbs and Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs with comprehensive rules and illustrative sentences.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: 1. Verb Inflection: Regular vs. Irregular Verbs. 2. Morphological Patterns of Regular Verbs (-ed / -d additions). 3. Vowel Mutation and Irregular Verb Classes. 4. Transitive Verbs: Direct and Indirect Objects. 5. Intransitive Verbs: Ergative, Motion, and State Verbs. 6. Summary Table and Practical Examples. · Answer: Verbs are the operational core of English sentences, classified by inflectional morphology and syntactic valency:  1. Regular vs. Irregular Verbs: • Regular Verbs: Form their past tense and past participle by uniformly adding '-ed' or '-d' to the base form:   - *play → played → played*   - *listen → listened → listened*   - *help → helped → helped*   - *clean → cleaned → cleaned* • Irregular Verbs: Do not follow the '-ed' rule; they change internal vowels or remain invariant:   - *go → went → gone*   - *write → wrote → written*   - *eat → ate → eaten*   - *cut → cut → cut*  2. Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs: • Transitive Verbs: Express an action that transfers directly to a recipient noun or pronoun (direct object):   - Formula: Subject + Verb + Direct Object   - Example: *We showed her the photo album.* ('photo album' is the direct object).   - Example: *The doctor advised me.* ('me' is the direct object). • Intransitive Verbs: Express an action or state that does not pass onto an object; they make complete sense on their own or with adverbial modifiers:   - Formula: Subject + Verb (+ Adverbial / Prepositional Phrase)   - Example: *She was crying all day long.* (No object receiving crying).   - Example: *It was raining at that time.* (No object).   - Example: *She laughed at the joke.* ('at the joke' is a prepositional phrase, not a direct object).  Mastering these distinctions is crucial for accurate sentence construction, active-passive voice transformations, and reading analysis.
