### Present Perfect Continuous Tense
p This tense expresses an action that started in past and still continues in present. It is used to state an ongoing action that has started at a point in the past. A time-reference is also used in the sentence to show that when the action started in past or for how long the action continued.
p The specific words 'since' and 'for' are used in sentence to show the time of action. The word 'since' is used if the exact starting time of action is known or intended to be reflected in the sentence (e.g. since Monday, since 3 P.M). The word 'for' is used to express the duration of time for which the action was continued (e.g. for three hours, for six months).
p Structure of Sentence: Has/have + been + present participle
q 5 | Examples
qp 1 | He has been teaching in this school for three years.
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous (has been + teaching). · The action began in the past and is still going on; "for three years" says how long.
qp 2 | They have been using their car for five years.
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous (have been + using). · The car was bought five years ago and is still in use; "for five years" is the length of time.
qp 3 | She has not been studying her books for five days.
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous, negative (has not been + studying). · Up to now she has not studied for five days; "for five days" is the length of time.
qp 4 | Kids have not been playing games for five hours.
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous, negative (have not been + playing). · Up to now the kids have not played for five hours; "for five hours" is the length of time.
qp 5 | Have you been going to your college since 2012?
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous, question (Have + you + been + going). · "since 2012" gives the starting point of an action that still goes on.
qp 6 | Has she been writing the report since 7th August 2011?
sol Solution ## Tense: present perfect continuous, question (Has + she + been + writing). · "since 7th August 2011" gives the starting point of an action that still goes on.
### Past Perfect Continuous Tense
p Past Perfect Progressive tense is used to describe an ongoing action that started in past and continued for some time in past. It expresses continued nature of an action that started and stopped at some point in the past. The sentence includes a 'time-reference' to show that when the action started in past or for how long the action was continued in past.
p Structure of Sentence: had + been + present participle
