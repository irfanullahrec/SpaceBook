q 5 | Abou Ben Adhem ‘spoke more low’ means that Abou Ben Adhem spoke: (a) boldly. (b) humbly. (c) slowly. (d) firmly.
sol (b) humbly. ## "Spoke more low" means he spoke in a softer, humble voice because he was modest before the angel.
## Vocabulary
q A | Which lines of the poem indicate imagery?
sol Model answer ## "An angel writing in a book of gold" and "Making it rich and like a lily in bloom" (the room full of moonlight) appeal to our sense of sight; "And lo! Ben Adhem's name led all the rest" is also a vivid picture. Other lines with images are possible.
q B | Pick out rhyming words from the poem.
sol Model answer ## Examples from the poem: increase – peace; bloom – room; gold – bold; said – led; low – so; blest – rest. Check against the poem in the lesson; other pairs may be found.
q C | Identify different figures of speech from the poem.
sol Model answer ## Simile: "like a lily in bloom". Personification: the angel writes and answers as a person; also imagery of the moonlit room. Other figures may be found.
## Writing
q A | Write a short poem on a theme similar to the one in ‘Abou Ben Adhem’.
sol Model answer ## He who serves the poor and weak / is dearest to the Lord; / for love of man is love of God, / the highest, truest word. Other poems on the theme of loving mankind are possible.
q B | Write the summary of the poem ‘Abou Ben Adhem’.
sol Model answer ## Abou Ben Adhem wakes in the night and sees an angel writing the names of those who love God. His name is not there, so he asks to be written as one who loves his fellow men. The next night the angel shows the names of those whom God has blessed, and Ben Adhem's name leads all the rest. Other correct summaries are possible.
q C | Write an essay of about 100 words on ‘Haqooq-ul-Ebad’.
sol Model answer ## Haqooq-ul-Ebad means the rights of people. Islam teaches that we must fulfil the rights of parents, relatives, neighbours, the poor and all human beings, because Allah forgives faults against Him more easily than wrongs done to people. We should be honest, keep promises, help the needy and never harm anyone. A society that respects these rights lives in peace. Other essays are possible.
q D | Paraphrase the first and third stanzas of the poem ‘Abou Ben Adhem’.
sol Model answer ## First stanza: Abou Ben Adhem, may his tribe increase, woke one night from a deep dream of peace and saw in his room, in the moonlight, an angel writing in a golden book. Third stanza: the angel wrote and vanished; the next night he came again with a great awakening light and showed the names of those whom the love of God had blessed, and lo, Ben Adhem's name led all the rest. Other correct paraphrases are possible.
