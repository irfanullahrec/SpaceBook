box teach | Figures of Speech: Simile, Metaphor, Personification, Hyperbole, Imagery
p A Simile compares two unlike things using 'as' or 'like' ('lonely as a cloud'). Personification gives human attributes to non-human entities ('daffodils dancing'). Hyperbole uses deliberate exaggeration ('Ten thousand saw I at a glance'). Imagery uses sensory language appealing to sight, sound, or touch ('golden daffodils', 'sparkling waves').
- Simile: Continuous as the stars that shine.
- Personification: Tossing their heads in sprightly dance.
- Hyperbole: Ten thousand saw I at a glance.
- Imagery: Fluttering and dancing in the breeze.

### Critical Appreciation: The Romantic Ideal of Nature in Wordsworth's 'The Daffodils'
p William Wordsworth's 'The Daffodils' is a quintessential masterpiece of the English Romantic movement. Romantic poetry prioritizes emotion over cold logic, individualism, and a spiritual veneration of nature. In this poem, Wordsworth illustrates his famous definition of poetry as 'emotion recollected in tranquillity.' The progression from solitude to ecstatic communion with the daffodils and finally to therapeutic mental recollection captures the transformative power of the natural world. Through vivid visual imagery, kinetic personification, and melodic rhythm, Wordsworth reminds humanity that communion with nature is the ultimate antidote to industrial anxiety and urban alienation.
