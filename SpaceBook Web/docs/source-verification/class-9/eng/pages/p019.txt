As highlighted in Unit 1, tolerance is defined as the virtue that enables a person to forebear negative remarks and adverse actions with calm steadfastness. Entering the city with ten thousand troops, the army practiced total restraint—no looting, no bloodshed, and no personal retribution. Even arch-enemies such as Abu Sufyan were pardoned unconditionally.  Through these historic milestones, Hazrat Muhammad (PBUH) demonstrated that true strength lies not in retaliation, but in boundless compassion, earning his divinely ordained title 'Rahmatul-lil-Alameen'.

qp 3 | Explain the grammar concepts covered in Unit 1: classifications of nouns and nouns changing meaning in plural forms, with clear examples.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: 1. Overview of Noun Classifications. 2. Collective, Countable, Uncountable, Abstract, and Material Nouns with examples. 3. Detailed Analysis of Nouns changing meaning in Plural form. 4. Pedagogical and Practical Relevance in Translation. · Answer: Unit 1 comprehensively covers both fundamental and nuanced grammatical classifications of English nouns:  1. Primary Noun Classifications: • Collective Nouns: Refer to a collection of persons or things taken as a single whole (e.g. 'Muslim army', 'Banu-Hashim family'). • Countable Nouns: Individual items that can be counted and made plural (e.g. 'valley / valleys', 'debt / debts', 'companion / companions'). • Uncountable Nouns: Substances, concepts, or masses that cannot be counted as discrete units (e.g. 'water', 'food', 'tolerance', 'advice'). • Abstract Nouns: Intangible qualities, states, or virtues (e.g. 'patience', 'forgiveness', 'mercy', 'justice'). • Material Nouns: Concrete substances or raw matter from which things are made (e.g. 'leather', 'dates', 'cloth').  2. Nouns Changing Meaning in Plural Forms: A vital SLO in Unit 1 is recognizing that certain English nouns change their semantic definition when transformed into plural form: • Arm (a human limb) → Arms (weapons, military armaments). • Custom (a traditional practice) → Customs (duties levied on imported goods). • Force (physical power / strength) → Forces (organized military troops). • Good (moral virtue / benefit) → Goods (commercial products and freight). • Wood (substance from trees) → Woods (a forest or tract of trees).  Mastering these nuances ensures accuracy when translating complex literary and technical passages between English and Urdu.

qp 5 | Examine the grammatical rules governing Nouns that change meaning in their plural form as taught in Unit 1.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Introduction to Semantic Shift in English Plural Morphology II. Analysis of Core Pairs: Arm/Arms, Good/Goods, Force/Forces III. Analysis of Compass/Compasses, Respect/Respects, Custom/Customs IV. Pedagogical Importance for Grammatical Precision and Board Examinations · Answer: In English morphology, certain nouns undergo radical semantic shifts when converted from singular to plural forms.  1. Arm vs. Arms: - Arm (singular): Human upper limb extending from shoulder to wrist. - Arms (plural): Weapons, munitions, or military hardware.  2. Good vs. Goods: - Good (singular/abstract): Moral virtue, benefit, or righteousness. - Goods (plural): Commercial merchandise, freight, or portable property.  3. Force vs. Forces: - Force (singular): Physical strength, power, or momentum. - Forces (plural): Military, naval, or air army contingents.  4. Compass vs. Compasses: - Compass (singular): Navigational instrument indicating magnetic direction. - Compasses (plural): Two-legged drafting tool used to draw circles.  5. Respect vs. Respects: - Respect (singular): Esteem, admiration, or polite consideration. - Respects (plural): Formal polite greetings or condolences ('Pay one's respects').
