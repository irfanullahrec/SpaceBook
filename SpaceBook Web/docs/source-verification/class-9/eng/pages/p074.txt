p It was agreed that Hazrat Usman would receive all the income from the well for one day and the Jew for the next. Hazrat Usman returned to the Masjid and offered his share in the well to Hazrat Muhammad (رسول اللہ خاتم النبیین ﷺ). It was proclaimed in Medina that everyone could carry as much water, free of cost, as he/she liked, on the day appointed for Hazrat Usman. The Muslims as well as the Jews made full use of Hazrat Usman's generosity and carried enough water to last for the next day as well.
p The Jew learnt a bitter lesson. Whereas he had hoped to make more money, he made none at all. He went to Hazrat Usman and offered to sell the other half of the well also. Hazrat Usman could have refused to buy it, for the Muslims had no longer any difficulty, but such was his generosity that he paid the Jew whatever he asked for his share.
p The well was dedicated as an endowment to the people of Madina. The well exists in Medina to this day and is known as Baire Usman or Usman's well.
p Teacher's guideline: Encourage the students to narrate any other incident from the lives of any of the four Caliphs highlighting their humanity. The text is an excellent example of love for humanity and generosity. Discuss with the students that generosity is not only giving money but your time and energy too.
