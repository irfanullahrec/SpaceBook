q 17 | SLO-based MCQs
qp 1 | The poem 'Abou Ben Adhem' is composed in rhyming pairs known as: (a) a. Blank verse (b) b. Free verse (c) c. Heroic couplets (AABBCCDDEE...) (d) d. Terza rima
sol c. Heroic couplets (AABBCCDDEE...) ## The 18 lines are arranged in 9 rhyming couplets of iambic pentameter.
qp 2 | The book in which the angel was writing was made of: (a) a. Silver (b) b. Gold (c) c. Parchment (d) d. Bronze
sol b. Gold ## Line 5: 'An angel writing in a book of gold'.

qp 6 | Convert into Indirect Speech: He said, "I have won." (a) a. He said that he has won. (b) b. He exclaimed with joy that he had won. (c) c. He says he won. (d) d. He told he is winning.
sol b. He exclaimed with joy that he had won. ## Exclamations of victory use 'exclaimed with joy' and past perfect 'had won'.
qp 7 | Convert into Indirect Speech: The teacher said, "The earth moves around the sun." (a) a. The teacher said that the earth moved around the sun. (b) b. qp 8 | Convert into Passive Voice: 'The teacher praised the boy.' (a) a. The boy is praised by the teacher. (b) b. The boy was praised by the teacher. (c) c. The boy had been praised by the teacher. (d) d. The teacher was praising the boy.
sol b. The boy was praised by the teacher. ## Past Simple active ('praised') becomes 'was praised' in passive voice.

q 18 | SLO-based short questions
qp 1 | Q1. What is the central spiritual lesson of 'Abou Ben Adhem'?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The central spiritual lesson is that genuine devotion to Almighty Allah is best expressed through active love, compassion, and service to His creation (Haqooq-ul-Ebad). Those who love and serve humanity are rewarded with the highest station in God's divine favor.
qp 2 | Q2. Explain the significance of the angel's 'book of gold'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The 'book of gold' symbolizes the precious, eternal, and incorruptible heavenly ledger where righteous deeds and divine blessings are recorded. Gold signifies purity, everlasting value, and heavenly honor.
qp 3 | Q3. How did Abou Ben Adhem react when told his name was not among those who loved the Lord?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Abou did not despair, become angry, or lose faith. Instead, he maintained his cheerful composure ('cheerly still') and humbly requested the angel with lowered voice to record his name as one who loved his fellow human beings.
qp 4 | Q4. Describe the poetic form, meter, and rhyme scheme of 'Abou Ben Adhem'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The poem is composed in 18 lines arranged into 9 heroic couplets (AABBCCDDEEFFGGHHII). It follows predominantly iambic pentameter (ten syllables per line with alternating unstressed and stressed beats), lending it a noble, solemn cadence.
qp 5 | Q5. State the rules for converting Direct Interrogative sentences into Indirect Speech.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 1. Change the reporting verb (said/told) to 'asked' or 'inquired'. 2. If the question begins with Wh-words (what, where, why), retain the wh-word as the connector; if it begins with an auxiliary verb (is, do, have), use 'if' or 'whether'. 3. Convert the interrogative word order into assertive sentence order (subject before verb). 4. Shift tenses and pronouns according to sequence of tenses and remove the question mark.
qp 6 | Q6. Change into Indirect Speech: 1. The teacher says, "If you work hard, you will pass." 2. She said, "I am going to college."
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 1. The teacher says that if they work hard, they will pass. (No tense shift because the reporting verb 'says' is in the present tense). 2. She said that she was going to college.
qp 7 | Q7. Change into Passive Voice: 1. She gave the beggar a five rupee coin. 2. Alia sang a song.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 1. The beggar was given a five-rupee coin by her. (or: A five-rupee coin was given to the beggar by her.) 2. A song was sung by Alia.
qp 8 | Q8. Explain the Islamic concept of 'Haqooq-ul-Ebad' and its priority in human ethics.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Haqooq-ul-Ebad represents the fundamental rights owed to fellow human beings (parents, orphans, neighbors, travelers, the needy). In Islamic ethics, while Allah may forgive deficiencies in ritual duties, sins against fellow human beings cannot be absolved without the forgiveness of the aggrieved person.
qp 9 | Q9. What is the meaning and context of Surah Al-Hujurat Ayat 10 quoted at the textbook conclusion?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The Ayat translates: 'The believers are but brothers, so make settlement between your brothers. And fear Allah that you may receive mercy' (49:10). It emphasizes universal Islamic brotherhood, reconciliation, empathy, and social peace across the community.
qp 10 | Q10. What are the biographical credentials of co-author Prof. Hamidullah Khan?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Prof. Hamidullah Khan has taught English at Intermediate, Graduate, and Postgraduate levels, served as Senior Subject Specialist (English) at KP Textbook Board Peshawar, co-authored textbooks from Class 1 to 12, and contributed to National and Provincial Textbook Review Committees.
q 19 | SLO-based long questions
qp 1 | Comprehensive Critical Appreciation: Discuss the Themes, Heroic Form, Spiritual Symbolism, and Enduring Moral Appeal of Leigh Hunt's 'Abou Ben Adhem'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Introduction: Leigh Hunt and the Sufi Lore of Ibrahim Ibn Adham II. The Conflict Between Ritual Piety and Altruistic Humanism III. Poetic Structure: Masterful Use of Heroic Couplets and Archaic Register IV. Layered Symbolism: Moonlight, Lily, Book of Gold, and Wakening Light V. The Climax: Divine Validation of Humanitarian Love · Answer: Leigh Hunt's 'Abou Ben Adhem' is one of the most sublime and universally revered lyric poems in the English language.  1. Narrative Arc and Theological Insight: The poem dramatizes an encounter between Abou Ben Adhem and an angel. When Abou discovers his name is missing from the list of those who love God, he does not despair. Instead, his humble plea to be recorded as 'one that loves his fellow men' leads to the triumphant climax on the second night, where his name leads all the rest.  2. The Triumph of Humanism (Khidmat-e-Khalq): Hunt demonstrates that God needs no human favors, but His vulnerable creation does. Therefore, the only tangible proof of divine love is selfless service to humanity.  3. Metrics and Poetic Craftsmanship: Composed in eighteen lines of crisp heroic couplets (AABBCCDDEEFFGGHHII) in iambic pentameter, the poem uses archaic verbs ('writest', 'blest') and pronouns ('thou', 'thee') to evoke solemn biblical and Qur'anic gravity.  Conclusion: The poem stands as an eternal beacon reminding mankind that love of God and love of humanity are one and the same.
qp 2 | Examine the Rules of Direct and Indirect Speech (Statements, Imperatives, Interrogatives, and Exclamations) with Solved Textbook Illustrations.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Fundamentals of Narration: Reporting Verb, Conjunctions, and Tense Shifts II. Rules for Assertive Sentences and Universal Truths III. Rules for Interrogative Sentences (Wh-words vs. Auxiliary Inversion) IV. Rules for Imperative (Commands/Requests) and Exclamatory Sentences V. Analysis of Unit 15 Exercises (Pages 149–151) · Answer: Narration transformation requires precise adherence to syntactic and deictic shift rules.  1. Assertive Statements: - Reporting verb in Past tense causes backshifting: Present Simple -> Past Simple ('gives' -> 'gave'), Present Perfect -> Past Perfect ('have won' -> 'had won'). - Universal Truths remain unchanged: 'The earth moves around the sun.'  2. Interrogative Sentences: - Converted using 'asked' or 'inquired'. - Auxiliary questions take 'if/whether': 'He asked me if I had brought the pens.' - Wh-questions retain the wh-word: 'He asked where I was going.'  3. Imperatives (Requests/Commands): - Use infinitive 'to + base verb': 'She requested him to wait there till she returned.'  4. Exclamatory Sentences: - Use 'exclaimed with joy/sorrow' and convert exclamations to statements: 'Alice exclaimed that she was very clever.'
qp 3 | Comprehensive Essay on 'Haqooq-ul-Ebad: The Bedrock of Islamic Morality and Social Harmony'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Introduction: The Dual Pillars of Islamic Ethics (Haqooq-Allah and Haqooq-ul-Ebad) II. The Inviolability of Human Rights in the Holy Quran and Hadith III. Spheres of Responsibility: Parents, Kin, Orphans, Neighbors, and Strangers IV. The Moral Lesson of 'Abou Ben Adhem' in Relation to Khidmat-e-Khalq V. Conclusion: Practical Implementation in Modern Society · Answer: Islam is a complete code of life rooted in justice, compassion, and the preservation of human dignity through 'Haqooq-ul-Ebad'.  1. Theological Primacy: In the Farewell Sermon (Khutbatul Wada), the Holy Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) declared the life, honor, and property of every human being to be sacred and inviolable. Allah will pardon lapses in ritual obligations, but injustice against fellow humans must be reconciled directly with the victim.  2. Key Dimensions: - Filial Reverence: Utmost respect and support for elderly parents. - Care for the Vulnerable: Protecting orphans, widows, and destitute laborers. - Universal Neighborliness: The Prophet (PBUH) emphasized kindness to neighbors regardless of religious affiliation.  3. Humanitarian Synthesis: Abou Ben Adhem epitomizes this Islamic ideal: true spiritual greatness is achieved not in isolated contemplation, but through active relief of human distress.
qp 4 | Analyze the Mechanics of Active and Passive Voice Transformations Across Various Tenses as Presented in Unit 15 Revision.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Definition and Communicative Functions of Passive Voice II. The General Formula for Passive Construction (Object + Form of 'Be' + Past Participle V3 + By + Subject) III. Tense Variations (Past Simple, Present Continuous, Present Perfect) IV. Verbs with Double Objects (Direct vs. Indirect Object) V. Detailed Solutions for Textbook Revision Exercises 7 and 8 · Answer: Voice transformation shifts focus from the doer of the action (active) to the recipient of the action (passive).  1. Transformation Rules: - The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence. - Use the appropriate auxiliary form of 'be' matching the tense and number of the new subject. - The main verb is always converted to its Past Participle (V3) form. - The original subject is placed after the preposition 'by' (or omitted if generic).  2. Tense Models from Unit 15: - Past Simple: 'The teacher praised the boy' -> 'The boy was praised by the teacher.' - Present Continuous: 'They are painting the walls' -> 'The walls are being painted by them.' - Present Perfect: 'The mechanic has fixed the car' <-> 'The car has been fixed by the mechanic.' - Double Object: 'She gave the beggar a five rupee coin' -> 'The beggar was given a five-rupee coin by her.'
