box activity | Poem Summary
p Summary of the Poem 'The Old Woman'
p Content: In 'The Old Woman', Joseph Campbell pays a tender, lyrical tribute to the beauty, dignity, and serenity of old age. The poet asserts that true beauty does not vanish with youth; rather, it transforms into a higher spiritual grace.  In the first stanza, Campbell compares an elderly woman's wrinkled face to a pure white candle glowing in a holy shrine, symbolizing purity, sanctity, and reverence. In the second stanza, her state is likened to the spent radiance of the winter sun. Having completed her life's grueling toil ('travail done') and raised her family, her presence radiates soothing, mild warmth. In the final stanza, her children have grown up and departed ('her brood gone from her'). Free from domestic turmoil, her mind rests in deep contemplation, as serene and unruffled as the still pool of water beneath a ruined watermill.  The poem beautifully captures the peace, fulfillment, and divine tranquility that crown a life of selfless devotion.

box activity | Essay Respecting Elders
p Essay: Appreciating and Respecting Elders (100–150 Words)
p Content: Elders are the foundation and guiding light of every family and society. Throughout their active lives, our parents and grandparents make countless sacrifices—enduring hardships, providing quality education, and offering unconditional love to secure our future.  In their old age, elderly people often become physically frail and emotionally vulnerable as their children become busy with their own lives. It is our foremost moral, cultural, and religious duty to treat them with utmost reverence, patience, and affection. Listening to their stories, valuing their wisdom, attending to their healthcare needs, and spending quality time with them brings immense solace to their hearts.  Respecting elders is not merely a social courtesy; it is a blessing that enriches our moral character and ensures harmony across generations.
endbox
