qp 1 | A. Write down the Summary of the Story 'The Fantastic Shoemaker'
sol 'The Fantastic Shoemaker' by Waheedullah is a poignant story ## 'The Fantastic Shoemaker' by Waheedullah is a poignant story of craftsmanship, professional pride, and moral integrity set in Peshawar's Qissa Khwani Bazaar. The narrator recalls his lifelong association with Zarin Gul, a master artisan who crafted flawless Peshawari chappals on order.  Zarin Gul viewed his trade as an art, utilizing only authentic materials and ensuring unmatched durability. When commercial footwear corporations dominated the market through aggressive advertising, Zarin Gul refused to compromise his principles or become a factory worker. His business suffered severely, and upon returning from abroad, the narrator found his old friend impoverished yet undefeated in spirit.  A week after placing a large order to support him, the author discovered that Zarin Gul had died of slow starvation, having spent all his resources on rent and premium leather. The story stands as a powerful tribute to honest labor and a profound critique of heartless consumerism.

All of a sudden, a violent crash of thunder rattled the glass windowpanes, jerking him awake into pitch darkness. The electricity was out, and torrential rain hammered the roof like galloping horses.  Through the howling wind, Saad heard a faint, persistent whimpering sound coming from the back garden. Gathering his courage, he clicked on his flashlight and stepped out onto the veranda. Huddled beneath the drenched bougainvillea bush was a shivering stray puppy, trapped under a fallen tree branch.  Without hesitating, Saad carefully lifted the heavy branch, wrapped the terrified creature in his warm woollen towel, and brought it inside. He dried its fur and offered it warm milk. As the little pup looked up with grateful, trusting eyes and wagged its tail, Saad realized that bravery and compassion can bring radiant light to the darkest of storms.

box teach | Active and Passive Voice Transformation
p In Active Voice, the subject performs the action ('Zarin Gul made the fine boots'). In Passive Voice, the subject receives the action ('The fine boots were made by Zarin Gul'). To convert, swap the subject and object, use the appropriate form of 'be' + past participle (V3), and add 'by' + agent.
- Active: The company produced poor quality shoes.
- Passive: Poor quality shoes were produced by the company.
- Active: They have finished the job.
- Passive: The job has been finished by them.
endbox

### Story Review: The Conflict between Quality Craftsmanship and Commercial Advertising in 'The Fantastic Shoemaker'
p John Galsworthy's narrative, adapted as 'The Fantastic Shoemaker', presents a haunting critique of modern industrial capitalism. Zarin Gul represents the pre-industrial artisan whose identity is inseparable from his art. For him, a pair of boots is not a disposable commodity but a masterpiece of integrity. However, modern advertising prioritizes superficial appearance and rapid turnover over durable quality. Galsworthy illustrates how unregulated market forces ruthlessly crush small, honest artisans in favor of profit-driven corporations. Zarin Gul's death is not merely personal tragedy; it symbolizes the death of human artistry in an era of mechanical mass consumption.
