q 10 | SLO-based short questions
qp 1 | Q1. How does Joseph Campbell portray the concept of beauty in old age?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Joseph Campbell redefines beauty, showing that while youth possesses superficial physical charm, old age possesses a superior spiritual beauty. He portrays an aged face as possessing sacred dignity, comparable to a white candle burning in a holy temple—pure, solemn, and illuminated by a lifetime of virtue and selfless maternal devotion.
qp 2 | Q2. Explain the significance of the simile 'As a white candle in a holy place'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: This simile holds two profound meanings: 1. Purity and Sanctity: The 'white candle' symbolizes innocence, piety, and moral righteousness, while the 'holy place' imbues her existence with sacred reverence. 2. Selfless Sacrifice: Just as a candle consumes its own wax to radiate light in darkness, a mother exhausts her youthful strength to nurture and illuminate her family's future.
qp 3 | Q3. What is meant by 'spent radiance of the winter sun' in Stanza 2?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 'Spent radiance of the winter sun' refers to the mild, soothing, and non-scorching glow of the sun in winter. Unlike the fierce, burning heat of the summer sun (symbolizing youthful passion and vigor), the winter sun's warmth is gentle and mature, perfectly mirroring the calm, comforting presence of an elderly woman whose earthly struggles ('travail') are complete.
qp 4 | Q4. Analyze the imagery and symbolism of 'waters under a ruined mill'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: In Stanza 3, the 'ruined mill' represents the completion and cessation of life's relentless physical labor (the grinding wheel of domestic duty has stopped). The 'still waters' beneath it symbolize a mind that is undisturbed by worldly anxieties—deep, tranquil, reflective, and enjoying peaceful contentment.
qp 5 | Q5. What does the phrase 'Her brood gone from her' suggest about maternal sacrifice and loneliness?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 'Her brood gone from her' illustrates the natural departure of grown children who have left the family nest to pursue their own livelihoods. While it hints at the quiet solitude of an aging parent, Campbell emphasizes that the mother feels serene fulfillment rather than bitterness, having accomplished her life's sacred mission.
qp 6 | Q6. Describe the structure, meter, and rhyme scheme of the poem.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The poem consists of: - Stanzas: 3 quatrains (four-line stanzas), making 12 lines in total. - Rhyme Scheme: ABCB in each stanza (Stanza 1: candle/place/beauty/face; Stanza 2: radiance/sun/woman/done; Stanza 3: her/still/waters/mill). - Tone: Reverent, gentle, contemplative, and solemn.
qp 7 | Q7. Why is old age considered a period of wisdom and tranquility according to the poet?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Old age is a period of wisdom because the turbulence, competitive ambitions, and harsh struggles of youth have ended ('travail done'). Having experienced all aspects of human joy and sorrow, an elderly individual attains a meditative state of calm, possessing unmatched emotional equilibrium and clarity.
qp 8 | Q8. Paraphrase Stanza 2 of 'The Old Woman' in simple prose.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Paraphrase of Stanza 2: 'Just as the setting winter sun radiates a gentle, mild warmth after its intense heat has diminished, in the same way, an aged woman appears graceful and serene now that all her life's exhausting struggles, painful hardships, and responsibilities have been successfully completed.'
qp 9 | Q9. How does the poem foster moral values and empathy among young readers?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The poem inspires students to look beyond the frail physical appearance of elderly grandparents and recognize the enormous sacrifices behind every wrinkle. It teaches youth to honor their elders, provide compassionate companionship, and cherish the profound wisdom they offer to society.
qp 10 | Q10. What brief biographical details are given about poet Joseph Campbell in the textbook?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Joseph Campbell was born in 1906 in White Plains, New York. He attained great renown as a versatile writer, poet, and world-class athlete (one of the fastest half-mile runners in the world). His first published work was 'Where the Two Came to Their Father', and his poem 'The Old Woman' is celebrated for its sublime depiction of maternal old age.
q 11 | SLO-based long questions
qp 1 | Comprehensive Critical Appreciation: Discuss the major themes, figurative language, and philosophical depth of Joseph Campbell's 'The Old Woman'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Introduction: Redefining Aesthetics of Age in Modern Poetry II. Triad of Master Similes (Candle, Winter Sun, Still Waters) III. Maternal Sacrifice and the Completion of Earthly Labor ('Travail Done') IV. Structural Craftsmanship and Rhyme Scheme V. Pedagogical and Ethical Message for Society · Answer: Joseph Campbell's 'The Old Woman' is a masterpiece of lyrical economy, compressing profound spiritual insight into twelve concise lines.  1. Spiritual Aesthetics vs. Physical Youth: Society often equates beauty exclusively with youthful vitality and flawless skin. Campbell dismantles this shallow perception by elevating the aged, wrinkled face of an old woman to a sacred icon. Her beauty is spiritual, born of righteous sacrifice and moral steadfastness.  2. The Triad of Master Similes: - The Holy Candle: Stanza 1 establishes an aura of sacredness. The white candle evokes purity, solemn prayer, and divine illumination. - The Winter Sun: Stanza 2 balances visual and thermal elements. The 'spent radiance' of a winter sun does not burn; it comforts with gentle, golden warmth, mirroring an elder's mellow temperament. - The Still Waters of the Ruined Mill: Stanza 3 provides psychological resolution. The ruined mill symbolizes the cessation of active physical machinery, leaving behind undisturbed pools of deep contemplation.  3. Maternal Fulfillment: Though her children ('brood') have flown, the mother experiences not despair, but sublime peace ('travail done'). Her life's work is complete.  Conclusion: Campbell delivers a timeless moral sermon: elderly mothers are living sanctuaries of love whose lifelong devotion commands society's deepest veneration.
qp 2 | Examine the three extended similes used in 'The Old Woman' and explain how each enhances the central idea.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Definition and Function of Simile in Lyric Poetry II. Simile 1: 'As a white candle in a holy place' III. Simile 2: 'As the spent radiance of the winter sun' IV. Simile 3: 'As the waters under a ruined mill' V. Synthesis: How the Three Similes Create a Progressive Portrait of Old Age · Answer: In 'The Old Woman', Joseph Campbell constructs each of the three stanzas around a single, powerful simile that progressively unfolds the physical, emotional, and psychological reality of aging.  1. Simile 1 (Spiritual Dimension): 'As a white candle In a holy place, So is the beauty Of an aged face.' Here, the vehicle (white candle in a shrine) transfers attributes of sacred purity, quiet burning, and reverence to the tenor (the aged face). It elevates old age from decay to divine sanctity.  2. Simile 2 (Physical & Historical Dimension): 'As the spent radiance Of the winter sun, So is a woman With her travail done,' The vehicle (winter sun's spent light) conveys reduced physical energy coupled with comforting, warm benevolence. It acknowledges that her physical vigor has been spent in lifelong domestic labor ('travail').  3. Simile 3 (Psychological & Emotional Dimension): 'And her thoughts as still As the waters Under a ruined mill.' The vehicle (motionless water under a disused mill) captures total emotional tranquility. The mill wheel of daily worries has stopped, allowing the water of the mind to settle into crystal clarity.  Synthesis: Together, the three similes move from the woman's sacred appearance, to her past labor, to her present peaceful mindset, creating a complete and magnificent portrait.
qp 3 | Write an extensive analytical essay on 'The Socio-Religious Duty of Respecting and Caring for Elderly Parents and Grandparents'.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Introduction: The Universal Obligation of Filial Piety II. The Islamic Perspective: Quranic Injunctions on Parents (Surah Al-Isra 17:23-24) III. Physical, Psychological, and Emotional Vulnerabilities in Geriatric Life IV. Modern Challenges: The Threat of Nuclear Family Isolation V. Practical Measures to Ensure Dignified Elder Care · Answer: Respect and compassionate care for elderly parents is both the cornerstone of civilized human culture and a supreme religious duty in Islam.  1. The Religious Mandate: The Holy Quran explicitly commands reverence for aging parents: 'And lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy and say: My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up when I was small' (Surah Al-Isra 17:24). The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) declared that serving elderly parents is one of the most beloved deeds to Allah, leading straight to Paradise.  2. Vulnerability and Maternal Sacrifice: Elderly parents spent their youthful vigor, earnings, and sleep to shelter, educate, and protect their children. When age weakens their limbs, diminishes their eyesight, and leaves them dependent, abandoning them is an unforgivable moral failure.  3. Overcoming Modern Isolation: Urbanization and fast-paced modern lifestyles frequently isolate elderly family members. We must combat this by actively involving grandparents in family discussions, providing timely healthcare, and surrounding them with patience and love rather than treating them as burdens.  Conclusion: A society that honors its elders inherits divine blessings, moral strength, and enduring cultural heritage.

sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Stanza 1: Paraphrase, Diction Analysis ('white candle', 'holy place', 'aged face') II. Stanza 2: Paraphrase, Diction Analysis ('spent radiance', 'winter sun', 'travail done') III. Stanza 3: Paraphrase, Diction Analysis ('brood', 'thoughts as still', 'ruined mill') IV. Evaluation of Tone and Metric Flow · Answer: Stanza 1: - Paraphrase: In the same manner that a pure white candle illuminates a sacred temple with quiet reverence, the wrinkled face of an old woman radiates divine grace and spiritual beauty. - Diction & Tone: The words 'white', 'candle', and 'holy' establish an atmosphere of solemn purity and sacred reverence. The tone is reverential and serene.  Stanza 2: - Paraphrase: Just as the gentle, mellow light of the winter sun spreads mild warmth across the Earth after its fierce heat is exhausted, so does an elderly woman rest in tranquil peace after all the exhausting struggles and pains of her life are finished. - Diction & Tone: 'Spent radiance' contrasts with summer fury; 'travail' evokes biblical depth of hard domestic labor. qp 5 | Compare and contrast the theme of nature and human emotion in 'The Daffodils' (Unit 4) with 'The Old Woman' (Unit 12).
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Overview of Both Poems and Authors (Wordsworth vs. Campbell) II. Romantic Joy and Active Nature in 'The Daffodils' III. Quiet Contemplation and Symbolist Similes in 'The Old Woman' IV. The Portrayal of Memory, Solitude, and Inward Peace V. Conclusion: Common Thread of Sublime Spiritual Elevation · Answer: William Wordsworth's 'The Daffodils' and Joseph Campbell's 'The Old Woman' represent two distinct yet profoundly complementary masterpieces of English lyric poetry.  1. Nature as Dynamic Joy vs. Nature as Contemplative Symbol: - In 'The Daffodils', nature is dynamic, vibrant, and overflowing with kinetic energy: 'fluttering and dancing in the breeze', 'tossing their heads in sprightly dance'. Wordsworth derives ecstatic joy from the living landscape. - In 'The Old Woman', nature provides calm, symbolic metaphors: the 'winter sun' and 'waters under a ruined mill'. Here, nature mirrors the quiet, still maturity of human old age rather than wild youthful exuberance.  2. Solitude and Inward Peace: - Wordsworth experiences blissful solitude ('that inward eye which is the bliss of solitude') when remembering the golden daffodils from his couch. - Campbell's old woman experiences blissful solitude after her 'brood is gone', her thoughts becoming as tranquil as motionless millpond waters.  3. Conclusion: Both poems celebrate the supremacy of the human spirit when harmonized with nature—Wordsworth through youthful communion with flora, and Campbell through the sacred sunset of human life.
