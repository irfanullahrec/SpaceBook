q 11 | Anaphoric Cataphoric References
qp 1 | Anaphoric Reference (Looking Backward)
sol Solution ## Definition: When a pronoun or word refers back to an antecedent mentioned earlier in the text. · Example: "I went out with Jameela on Sunday. She looked confused." · Analysis: 'She' is anaphoric because it refers back to 'Jameela'.
qp 2 | Cataphoric Reference (Looking Forward)
sol Solution ## Definition: When a pronoun or word refers forward to an antecedent that appears later in the text. · Example: "When he arrived, Ashraf noticed that the door was open." · Analysis: 'He' is cataphoric because it points forward to 'Ashraf'.
qp 3 | Analysis of Lesson's Last Paragraph
sol Solution ## Definition: Identification of references in the conclusion: · Example: "He declares youth a symbol of hope..." · Analysis: 'He' is an anaphoric reference pointing back to Allama Iqbal mentioned in preceding sentences.

box teach | Pronouns: Relative, Indefinite, and Demonstrative
p Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. Relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) connect relative clauses to antecedents ('The youth who strives shall conquer'). Indefinite pronouns (someone, everyone, none, each, both, all) refer to non-specific entities ('Everyone possesses divine potential'). Demonstrative pronouns (this, that, these, those) point to specific entities.
- Relative: The leader who guided the youth was visionary.
- Indefinite: None can deny the impact of youth.
- Demonstrative: This is the philosophy of Khudi.
endbox
box teach | Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement
p A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number (singular/plural) and gender (masculine/feminine/neuter). Singular indefinite pronouns like 'everyone', 'each', 'everybody' grammatically take singular pronouns ('Every student must submit his/her assignment').
- Each of the boys demonstrated his potential.
- The eagles spread their wings towards the sky.
endbox
### Essay: Iqbal's Vision of Shaheen and its Relevance to Contemporary Pakistani Youth
p Allama Iqbal's metaphor of the Shaheen (eagle) remains the most powerful poetic vision for youth empowerment. In an era dominated by digital distractions, superficial trends, and economic anxieties, Iqbal's message serves as an invigorating clarion call. The Shaheen embodies high ambition, self-respect, relentless perseverance, and moral courage. For today's Pakistani youth, adopting the spirit of the Shaheen means striving for academic excellence, excelling in global science and technology, and rejecting despair and corruption. By anchoring our lives in self-reliance (Khudi) and social responsibility, we can fulfill Iqbal's dream of transforming Pakistan into an enlightened, prosperous, and self-sufficient nation.
