### Parentheses
p Parentheses add emphasis to the words contained in the parentheses. One way parentheses are used in writing is to give information that might interrupt the normal flow of the sentence. Sometimes this information adds to reader’s understanding of the rest of the sentence. Parenthesis (round brackets) is used to:
- enclose numbers or letters in enumerations in the text
- express an amount in numbers previously expressed in words
- mark off explanatory or supplementary material
p Example: Hameed Ullah Khan (the architect) built castles and bridges. The information in the parentheses, called a parenthetical element, may also be information that is unrelated to the sentence or unnecessary.
q 4 | Re-write the following sentences placing parenthesis in the correct place.
qp 1 | My mother likes although I don't know why old folk music.
sol Solution ## Corrected: My mother likes (although I don't know why) old folk music. · Rule: Encloses parenthetical supplementary clause interrupting the sentence.
qp 2 | All tiger species especially the South China tiger are endangered.
sol Solution ## Corrected: All tiger species (especially the South China tiger) are endangered. · Rule: Encloses specific explanatory example.
qp 3 | I'll get back to you tomorrow Friday.
sol Solution ## Corrected: I'll get back to you tomorrow (Friday). · Rule: Marks off clarifying day of the week.
qp 4 | Which country countries do you want to visit?
sol Solution ## Corrected: Which country (countries) do you want to visit? · Rule: Indicates alternative singular/plural grammatical form.
qp 5 | You put fifty-four 54 books on that shelf.
sol Solution ## Corrected: You put fifty-four (54) books on that shelf. · Rule: Expresses an amount in numerals previously written in words.
### Ellipsis
p An ellipsis is a series of three dots (...) which shows that some text has been left out. The ellipsis is used to shorten a passage. The omitted text does not change the meaning of the original text and is unnecessary to its meaning. It may also be used to show a pause, a hesitation, or an incomplete thought, especially in quotations. Note there is a space before and after the ellipsis. If the ellipsis is at the end of a quotation followed by a speaker reference, there is no comma.
p Example: 1. The guests ... were impressed by the library. 2. “I’m confused ...” said Gulmeena.
