q 2 | Comprehension
qp 1 | Why did Hazrat Muhammad (رسول اللہ خاتم النبیین ﷺ) abolish the tribal distinction in Medina?
sol Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ) abolished the tribal distinction in Medi ## Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ) abolished the tribal distinction in Medina to eradicate centuries-old inter-tribal rivalries and feuds, unify the diverse Muslim believers under one universal brotherly community named 'Ansar' (Helpers), and establish lasting social harmony, mutual co-operation, and political solidarity necessary for founding a just Islamic state.
qp 2 | What did UN adopt unanimously?
sol On 10th December 1948, the General Assembly of the United Na ## On 10th December 1948, the General Assembly of the United Nations unanimously adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR). This historic document proclaimed the fundamental civil, political, economic, social, religious, and cultural rights of all human beings across the globe.
qp 3 | How do we know that Islam is the protector of Human Rights?
sol We know that Islam is the greatest protector of Human Rights ## We know that Islam is the greatest protector of Human Rights because over 1400 years ago, the Medina Charter codified and protected universal civic equality, religious freedom, minority rights, justice, and the defense of the oppressed. The fact that the modern UN Declaration of Human Rights incorporates the very principles established in the Medina Charter proves Islam's pioneering leadership in human rights.
qp 4 | Define tolerance and give examples from the text.
sol Tolerance is the virtue of maintaining a fair, forbearing, a ## Tolerance is the virtue of maintaining a fair, forbearing, and objective attitude toward individuals whose beliefs, lifestyle, or customs differ from one's own. In the text, Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ) demonstrated profound tolerance by ensuring that non-Muslims (the Jewish communities and idol worshippers) enjoyed complete religious freedom to profess their faiths, perform their sacred rites, and live peacefully without coercion.
qp 5 | We must always honour our words. Why?
sol We must always honour our words because keeping covenants an ## We must always honour our words because keeping covenants and promises forms the moral foundation of mutual trust, social harmony, legal integrity, and peaceful co-existence. As demonstrated in the Medina Charter, honoring agreements prevents chaos, protects collective security, and builds a trustworthy and just society.
qp 6 | Write an account of an act of tolerance you have shown/seen.
sol Model Student Account: In our school sports gala, during a h ## Model Student Account: In our school sports gala, during a highly competitive inter-class football final, an opposing team's player accidentally made a harsh tackle that injured our captain.
