box teach | Similes and Sensory Imagery in Poetry
p A Simile is an explicit comparison between two different entities using 'as' or 'so... as'. Campbell uses three consecutive structural similes: 1. 'As a white candle in a holy place, so is the beauty of an aged face.' 2. 'As the spent radiance of the winter's sun, so is a woman with her travail done.' 3. 'Her thoughts as still as the waters under a ruined mill.'
- Simile of Purity: White candle in a holy place.
- Simile of Faded Warmth: Spent radiance of winter sun.
- Simile of Stillness: Waters under a ruined mill.
endbox
box teach | Syntactic Inversion in Poetic Stanzas
p Poets often invert normal grammatical subject-verb-object word order for poetic cadence and emphasis ('Her brood gone from her' instead of 'Her brood has gone from her').
- 'So is the beauty of an aged face' instead of 'The beauty of an aged face is so.'
endbox
### Paraphrase of 'The Old Woman' by Joseph Campbell
p Stanza 1: Just as a pure white candle burning inside a sacred sanctuary spreads a calm and spiritual glow, so too is the holy and peaceful beauty seen upon the face of an elderly woman.
p Stanza 2: Just as the gentle, fading sunlight of a winter afternoon provides a soft and comforting warmth after the blazing heat of summer, so too is an elderly woman whose lifelong hard labor, maternal struggles, and family duties are peacefully completed.
p Stanza 3: Her grown-up children have now departed to build their own independent lives, leaving her alone in quiet reflection. Her thoughts and memories are now as deep, motionless, and completely undisturbed as the calm, clear pool of water resting beneath an old, abandoned watermill.
