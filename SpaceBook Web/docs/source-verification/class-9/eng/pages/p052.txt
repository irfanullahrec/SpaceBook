@sec 5-s4 | 4. UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights & Islamic Precedence || اقوامِ متحدہ کا انسانی حقوق کا عالمی منشور اور اسلام کی اولیت || د ملګرو ملتونو د بشري حقونو نړیواله اعلامیه او د اسلام مخکښ والی
p In 1948, the General Assembly of the United Nations unanimously adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. The declaration proclaims the religious, personal, civic, political, economic, social and cultural rights of human beings. The incorporation of the clauses of Medina Charter in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights by the UN shows that Islam is the greatest protector of Human Rights.
p Teacher's guideline:
p Make students recall the use of colon. Ask them to find examples of the use of colon. Revise with the students that parenthesis are used to mark off explanatory or supplementary material e.g. Haram (abominable). Ask students to find more examples of the use of parenthesis, to enclose numbers or letters in enumeration from newspaper or a magazine.
p The Universal declaration of Human Rights:
p This Charter was approved on 10th December, 1948 and made public. There are thirty points in all. A few are being mentioned here.
p • We are all free and equal. We are all born free. We all have our own thoughts and ideas. We should all be treated in the same way.
p • Don't discriminate. These rights belong to everybody, whatever our differences are.
p • We all have the right to live, and to live in freedom and safety.
p • Nobody has any right to hurt us or to torture us.
p • We are all protected by the law. The law is the same for everybody. It must treat us all fairly.
p • We all have the right to take part in the government of our country.
p • We all have a right to lead a good life. Mothers and children, people who are old, unemployed or disabled, and all other people have the right to be cared for.
p • Education is a right, primary education should be free.
p • We have a duty to other people and we should protect their rights and freedoms.
