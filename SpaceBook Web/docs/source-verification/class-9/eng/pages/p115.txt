qp 2 | LQ2. Detailed Exposition of Clauses: Main, Subordinate, Noun, Adjective, and Adverbial Clauses with illustrative examples.
sol A clause is a syntactic unit containing a subject and a fini ## A clause is a syntactic unit containing a subject and a finite predicate verb.  1. Main (Independent) Clause: Can stand alone as a complete thought. Example: 'He runs with great speed.'  2. Subordinate (Dependent) Clause: Cannot stand alone and depends on a main clause for full meaning.  Subordinate clauses are classified into three types: - Noun Clause: Functions as subject, object, or complement.   Example: 'The angler said that he hadn't caught any fish.' ('that he hadn't caught...' is object of 'said').  - Adjective (Relative) Clause: Modifies a noun or pronoun, introduced by relative pronouns (who, which, that, whose).   Example: 'The boys who misbehaved in the class were suspended.' ('who misbehaved' describes 'boys').  - Adverb Clause: Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb, indicating time, place, condition, concession, or cause.   Example: 'Though he didn't receive formal schooling, he is clever.' ('Though he didn't receive...' indicates concession).  Mastery of clause types empowers students to construct complex, sophisticated, and grammatically precise prose.

qp 5 | LQ5. Comprehensive Analysis of Adjective Formation, Degrees, and Adjective Ordering Rules from the Revision Exercises.
sol Adjectives enrich descriptive language by qualifying nouns: ## Adjectives enrich descriptive language by qualifying nouns:  1. Morphological Formation: - Suffixes like -ful (harm ➔ harmful, grace ➔ graceful), -al (music ➔ musical), -y (juice ➔ juicy, dust ➔ dusty), -ish (fool ➔ foolish, child ➔ childish), and -able (comfort ➔ comfortable).  2. Degrees of Comparison: - Positive: Base quality (tall, wise, good, bad). - Comparative: Compares two entities (taller, wiser, better, worse, more harmful). - Superlative: Identifies the extreme among three or more (tallest, wisest, best, worst, most harmful).  3. Royal Order of Adjectives: When multiple adjectives modify a single noun, English syntax follows the order: [Opinion] ➔ [Size] ➔ [Age] ➔ [Shape] ➔ [Colour] ➔ [Origin] ➔ [Material] ➔ [Purpose] + Noun. Example: 'He bought a handsome (opinion) new (age) English (origin) car.'  Applying these principles ensures stylistic harmony and grammatical precision in written compositions.
