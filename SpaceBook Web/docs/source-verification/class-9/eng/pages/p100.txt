p Prepositional Phrase: A phrase that begins with a preposition (i.e., in, at, for, behind, until, after, of, during) and modifies a word in the sentence. A prepositional phrase answers one of many questions. 'Where? When? In what way?' Here are a few examples:
p • I like spaghetti for dinner.
p • He reads many books in the library.
## English Sentence Structure
p The following statements are true about sentences in English:
p • A new sentence begins with a capital letter.
p He obtained his degree.
p • A sentence ends with punctuation (a period, a question mark, or an exclamation point).
p He obtained his degree.
p • A sentence contains a subject that is only given once.
p Saleem obtained his degree.
p • A sentence contains a verb or a verb phrase.
p He obtained his degree.
p • A sentence follows Subject + Verb + Object word order.
p He (subject) obtained (verb) his degree (object).
p • A sentence must have a complete idea that stands alone. This is also called an independent clause.
p He obtained his degree.
## Simple Sentences
p A sentence consisting of only one clause, with a single subject and predicate. Here are a few examples:
p • She read.
p • She completed her literature review.
p • He organized his sources by theme.
p • They studied traffic rules for many hours.
