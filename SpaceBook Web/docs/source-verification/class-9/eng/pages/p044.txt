qp 1 | Is there any personification in this poem? If yes, where?
sol Yes, personification is used prominently throughout the poem ## Yes, personification is used prominently throughout the poem. In Stanza 1, the daffodils are described as a 'crowd' and a 'host' that are 'fluttering and dancing in the breeze' like human dancers. In Stanza 2, the flowers are personified as 'tossing their heads in sprightly dance'. In Stanza 3, both the lake waves and the daffodils 'danced in glee' and form a 'jocund company'. Finally, in Stanza 4, the poet's own heart is personified as it 'dances with the daffodils'.

qp 4 | Why does the poet compare himself to a cloud?
sol The poet compares himself to a high, solitary cloud to empha ## The poet compares himself to a high, solitary cloud to emphasize his initial feelings of loneliness, detachment, and aimless wandering. Just as a cloud drifts freely and purposelessly across the open sky, Wordsworth was walking alone without any specific goal until the vibrant sight of the daffodils suddenly reconnected him with the joy of nature.
qp 5 | Write the main idea of the poem.
sol The main idea of 'The Daffodils' is the profound healing, tr ## The main idea of 'The Daffodils' is the profound healing, transformative, and lasting power of nature's beauty. William Wordsworth illustrates that natural beauty is not only a source of immediate visual delight but also a permanent spiritual treasure. Whenever a person experiences solitude, grief, or pensive moods, the recalled memory of nature's splendor revives the spirit and fills the heart with pure happiness.
