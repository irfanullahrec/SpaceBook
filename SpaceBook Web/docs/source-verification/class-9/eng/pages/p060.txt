q 18 | Exercise 10: Modal Verbs Matching Meaning Which of the three sentences containing a modal has the same meaning as the original one?
qp 1 | She is not able to read Chinese. (a) She cannot read Chinese. ✅ (b) She may not read Chinese. (c) She need not read Chinese.
sol She cannot read Chinese. ## 'Cannot' denotes lack of ability.
qp 2 | Do you think we are allowed to park here? (a) Do you think we may park here? ✅ (b) Do you think we must park here? (c) Do you think we need park here?
sol Do you think we may park here? ## 'May' asks for permission (are allowed to).
qp 3 | You don't have to do this exercise. (a) You must not do this exercise. (b) You need not do this exercise. ✅ (c) You should not do this exercise.
sol You need not do this exercise. ## 'Need not' / 'don't have to' indicates absence of necessity or obligation.
qp 4 | We have to stop when the traffic lights are red. (a) We can stop when the traffic lights are red. (b) We may stop when the traffic lights are red. (c) We must stop when the traffic lights are red. ✅
sol We must stop when the traffic lights are red. ## 'Must' / 'have to' indicates absolute obligation and legal compulsion.
qp 5 | Am I allowed to ask a question? (a) May I ask a question? ✅ (b) Must I ask a question? (c) Should I ask a question?
sol May I ask a question? ## 'May I' is the standard modal expression for politely asking permission.
