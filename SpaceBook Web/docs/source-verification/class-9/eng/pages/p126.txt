box teach | Gerunds vs. Present Participles (-ing forms)
p Both end in '-ing', but perform different syntactic functions: - A Gerund functions as a NOUN (Subject, Object, or Object of Preposition): '*Bleeding* can be stopped by direct pressure.' / 'He enjoys *swimming*.' - A Present Participle functions as an ADJECTIVE (modifying a noun) or forms continuous tenses with 'be': 'He saw a *bleeding* soldier.' / 'The doctor is *cleaning* the wound.'
- Gerund (Subject): Smoking is prohibited.
- Present Participle (Adjective): He tripped on a running hose.
- Gerund (Object of prep): Wash hands before dressing wounds.
endbox
box teach | Imperative Sentences for Emergency Instructions
p Imperative sentences express commands, safety instructions, or warnings, using the base form of the verb with an implied subject 'You'.
- Apply direct pressure immediately.
- Do not remove embedded objects.
- Dial 1122 without delay.
endbox
### Instruction Manual: Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for Laboratory Accidents in High Schools
p 1. Chemical Spills on Skin: Immediately flush the affected area with running cold water for at least 15 minutes. Remove contaminated clothing. Do not apply neutralizing chemicals without professional guidance.
p 2. Minor Thermal Burns: Cool the burn under cold, gentle running water for 10–20 minutes. Never apply ice, butter, or toothpaste. Cover loosely with sterile cling film or non-stick gauze.
p 3. Broken Glass Injuries: Do not remove large glass shards embedded deeply in wounds; stabilize them with a doughnut bandage and apply surrounding pressure. Immediately transport the student to the hospital emergency ward.
p 4. Toxic Gas Inhalation: Evacuate the laboratory immediately to fresh outdoor air. Loosen tight collars and maintain an open airway. Contact emergency Rescue 1122.

q 9 | SLO-based short questions
qp 1 | Q1. Define First Aid and state its three fundamental objectives.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: First Aid is the immediate care and assistance administered to an injured or suddenly ill person before professional medical personnel arrive.  Its three primary objectives ('The 3 Ps') are: 1. Preserve Life: Prevent premature death through basic airway maintenance, CPR, or stopping hemorrhage. 2. Prevent Deterioration: Keep the victim's medical condition from worsening (e.g., stabilizing fractures, cooling burns). 3. Promote Recovery: Facilitate early healing by dressing wounds, reassuring the casualty, and easing pain.
qp 2 | Q2. List eight indispensable items found in a standardized household First Aid Kit.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: A standard household First Aid Kit must include: 1. Adhesive plasters (assorted sizes) 2. Sterile gauze dressings (small, medium, large) 3. Sterile eye pads 4. Triangular bandages (for slings) 5. Crepe rolled elastic bandages 6. Safety pins and blunt-ended scissors 7. Tweezers and disposable sterile gloves 8. Antiseptic wipes and basic analgesic/antipyretic medications.
qp 3 | Q3. Explain the step-by-step first aid procedure for a minor thermal burn.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: For minor thermal burns: 1. Cool Immediately: Hold the burned area under gently flowing cool (not iced) tap water for at least 20 minutes to dissipate heat. 2. Remove Constrictions: Gently remove rings, watches, or loose clothing before swelling begins. 3. Avoid Home Remedies: Never apply butter, oils, or ice, as they trap heat and introduce infection. 4. Cover Wound: Apply a sterile, non-adhesive dressing or clean plastic wrap loosely over the burn.
qp 4 | Q4. Describe the Heimlich Maneuver (abdominal thrust) and when it should be administered.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: The Heimlich Maneuver is applied exclusively when an adult's airway is completely obstructed (the person cannot breathe, speak, or cough).  Procedure: 1. Stand behind the victim and wrap your arms around their waist. 2. Make a fist with one hand and place the thumb side against the abdomen, just above the navel and well below the ribcage. 3. Grasp the fist with your other hand and deliver rapid, upward and inward thrusts until the lodged object is expelled by upward air pressure.
qp 5 | Q5. How should severe external bleeding be managed effectively?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: To control severe external hemorrhage: 1. Apply Direct Pressure: Press a sterile gauze pad or clean cloth firmly over the wound using your hand. 2. Maintain Continuous Pressure: Do not remove the cloth even if blood soaks through; add another pad on top. 3. Elevate the Limb: Raise the bleeding limb above heart level to lower hydrostatic blood pressure. 4. Summon Emergency Services: Call emergency rescue (1122) immediately.
qp 6 | Q6. Why is a half-sitting position recommended for a conscious patient experiencing a heart attack?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: A half-sitting posture (with head and shoulders propped up and knees bent) is recommended because: - It significantly reduces the gravitational return of venous blood to the heart, lessening cardiac workload. - It lowers abdominal pressure on the diaphragm, facilitating easier lung expansion and oxygen exchange during acute breathlessness.
qp 7 | Q7. What common mistakes should be avoided when treating a nosebleed (epistaxis)?
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Mistakes to avoid during nosebleeds include: 1. Tilting the Head Backward: This causes blood to run down the throat, leading to choking, coughing, or vomiting. 2. Packing Cotton Deep in Nostrils: Rough packing can tear delicate nasal mucosa. 3. Releasing Pressure Prematurely: Pinching must be held uninterrupted for at least 10 minutes without checking every minute.
qp 8 | Q8. Explain the difference in grammatical function between a colon (:) and a semicolon (;).
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 1. Semicolon (;): Connects two independent clauses that are closely related in thought without using a coordinating conjunction (e.g., 'He knocked several times; no one came.'). 2. Colon (:): Introduces a list, an explanation, an elaboration, or a direct quotation that expands upon the preceding clause (e.g., 'He had one goal: to win.').
qp 9 | Q9. Differentiate between 'reward' and 'award' with illustrative sentences.
sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: 1. Reward: Something given in recognition of hard work, effort, or returning a lost item (informal or personal). - Example: The tourist gave the taxi driver a cash reward for returning his lost passport. 2. Award: A formal prize, medal, or certificate conferred by an official committee or institution for notable achievement. - Example: The author received the Nobel Award for Literature.

qp 2 | Examine the technical rules of Punctuation (Commas, Semicolons, Colons, Capitalization) with examples from Unit 11.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Capitalization Conventions for Proper Nouns and Sentence Openers II. Comma Usage in Complex, Compound, and Non-Defining Relative Clauses III. The Semicolon as an Independent Clause Coordinator IV. The Colon for Antithesis and Series Introductions V. Analysis of Solved Textbook Paragraphs and Sentences · Answer: Punctuation marks are grammatical traffic signals that establish clarity, rhythm, and semantic precision in written English.  1. Rules of Capitalization: - The first word of every sentence must begin with a capital letter. - All proper nouns (names of persons, geographical rivers, cities, countries, monuments) require capitalization (e.g., 'River Ravi', 'Lahore', 'Eiffel Tower', 'Sindh'). - The personal pronoun 'I' is always capitalized regardless of its position.  2. The Colon (:): - Introduces lists and series: 'Here is what you need to get: bananas, bread, milk and biscuits.' - Separates contrasting independent clauses with sharp antithesis.

qp 5 | Evaluate the semantic distinctions between commonly confused English word pairs: look/search, buy/purchase, hide/conceal, learn/educate, reward/award.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Semantic Nuances and Register in English Lexicography II. Detailed Contrast of 'Look' vs. 'Search' III. Detailed Contrast of 'Buy' vs. 'Purchase' IV. Detailed Contrast of 'Hide' vs. 'Conceal' V. Detailed Contrast of 'Learn' vs. 'Educate' and 'Reward' vs. 'Award' · Answer: Mastery of English vocabulary requires appreciating subtle register, intentionality, and formality distinctions between related terms.  1. Look vs. Search: - 'Look' involves passive or casual visual observation ('Look at the sky'). - 'Search' implies active, systematic, and vigorous investigation to find something lost or hidden ('Police searched the forest').  2. Buy vs. Purchase: - 'Buy' belongs to everyday informal speech for routine items ('Buy some fruit'). - 'Purchase' represents formal, commercial, or high-value acquisitions ('The firm purchased new machinery').  3. Hide vs. Conceal: - 'Hide' is common usage for physical obscurity ('Hide behind the curtain'). - 'Conceal' carries a formal connotation of deliberate suppression of evidence, feelings, or secrets ('He concealed his guilt').  4. Learn vs. Educate: - 'Learn' is the internal cognitive acquisition of skill by the student ('He learned swimming'). - 'Educate' refers to the structured process of imparting knowledge by institutions/teachers ('Schools educate future leaders').  5. Reward vs. Award: - 'Reward' is an informal token of gratitude or incentive for helpful behavior ('Reward for returning a purse'). - 'Award' is a formal institutional honor decided by an official judging committee ('Nobel Award').
