box teach | Degrees of Adjectives: Positive, Comparative, Superlative
p Adjectives have three degrees of comparison: Positive (base quality: 'fine cloak'), Comparative (comparing two: 'finer cloak', 'more intelligent'), and Superlative (comparing three or more: 'finest cloak', 'most intelligent'). Regular one-syllable adjectives add '-er' and '-est', while multi-syllable adjectives use 'more' and 'most'.
- Positive: wise, funny, fast
- Comparative: wiser, funnier, faster
- Superlative: wisest, funniest, fastest
endbox
box teach | Direct and Indirect Speech (Reporting Statements & Questions)
p Direct speech quotes exact spoken words ('He said, "I cannot read this"'). Indirect speech reports the essence without quotes ('He said that he could not read that'). Pronouns, tenses, and time/place words shift appropriately.
- Direct: Nasiruddin said, 'Ask my horse.'
- Indirect: Nasiruddin replied to ask his horse.

qp 3 | Comprehensive Grammar Guide: Adjectives, Types, Degrees of Comparison, Cumulative Order, and Suffix Formation.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Definition and Classification of Adjectives (Quality, Quantity, Size, Shape, Color, Origin, Material) II. Degrees of Comparison (Rules for Regular, Multi-syllable, and Irregular Adjectives) III. Standard Cumulative Order of Adjectives in English Syntax IV. Morphological Derivation: Forming Adjectives from Nouns & Verbs Using Suffixes V. Adjective Phrases vs. Single Adjectives: Syntactic Conversions · Answer: Adjectives are descriptive words that modify, quantify, or specify nouns and pronouns.  1. Classification of Adjectives: • Quality: *safe, responsible, honest* • Size/Height: *tall, big, huge* • Shape: *round, spherical, oval* • Color: *blue, black, crimson* • Origin: *German, Egyptian, Pakistani* • Material: *flannel, wooden, plastic, gold*  2. Degrees of Comparison: • Positive: Base form modifying one entity (*rich, strong, fast*). • Comparative: Compares two entities (*richer, stronger, faster, more intelligent*). • Superlative: Compares one against a whole group (*the richest, the strongest, the most intelligent*). • Irregular forms: *good -> better -> best*, *bad -> worse -> worst*.  3. Standard Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP): Opinion/Condition -> Size -> Age -> Shape -> Color -> Origin -> Material -> Purpose + Noun. • Example: *a dirty (opinion) old (age) flannel (material) shirt*. • Example: *a small (size) black (color) Egyptian (origin) vase*.  4. Adjective Phrases: A phrase functioning as an adjective can replace a single adjective (*a golden crown -> a crown made of gold*).
