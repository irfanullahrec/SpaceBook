Paraphrasing is re-writing the poet's words or ideas in your own clear prose without altering the fundamental meaning. A stanza takes the shape of natural prose sentences. It is about the same length as the original, because the purpose is to re-phrase without omitting any important thoughts.  Essential Rules for Paraphrasing a Poem: 1. qp 4 | B. Guidelines for Summarizing a Poem & Comprehensive Summary of 'The Daffodils'
sol Rules for Summarizing a Poem: 1. Read the poem thoroughly to ## Rules for Summarizing a Poem: 1. Read the poem thoroughly to grasp the central theme and development. 2. Note down only the salient, indispensable points and discard minor descriptions. 3. Arrange the main points in logical, chronological order. 4. A summary should be concise, objective, written in simple prose, and roughly one-third the length of the original.  Solved Comprehensive Summary of 'The Daffodils': 'The Daffodils' by William Wordsworth is a celebrated Romantic poem that glorifies the healing, everlasting charm of nature. While wandering lonely as a cloud across hills and vales, the poet unexpectedly encounters a vast host of golden daffodils blooming along the edge of a lake beneath the trees. The flowers stretch in an endless line like twinkling stars in the Milky Way, dancing vigorously in the spring breeze. Although the sparkling lake waves dance beside them, the vibrant daffodils outshine the waters in pure joy. The poet gazes in deep fascination, receiving an invaluable spiritual treasure. Later, whenever the poet lies on his couch in a thoughtful, vacant, or sad mood, the memory of the daffodils flashes upon his 'inward eye'—the supreme blessing of solitude. His heart fills with overwhelming delight and dances joyfully alongside the daffodils.

qp 3 | Q3. Explain the simile 'Continuous as the stars that shine and twinkle on the milky way'.

sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: Wordsworth personifies the daffodils by endowing them with human emotions and actions: 1. sol Solution ## Marks: 4 Marks · Answer: In Stanza 3, Wordsworth notes that while the sparkling water waves of the lake also danced in the wind, the golden daffodils outdid the sparkling waves in sheer glee and vibrant ecstasy, creating an overwhelmingly festive ('jocund') company.
