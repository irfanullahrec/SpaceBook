Conclusion: The Medina Charter served as the world's first comprehensive human rights charter, preceding modern international conventions by more than thirteen centuries.

qp 3 | C. Passage Linguistic & Grammatical Comprehension (Page 47)
sol Solution ## Topic Sentence: Analysis of the introductory paragraph of the Medina Charter text. · Content: Given Passage: "When Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ) migrated to Medina, this city was inhabited by different sections of people. They were the faithful followers of Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ) who had abandoned the ties of kith and kin and followed him (ﷺ). They were known as Muhajireen or 'Refugees'. The new converts of Medina who had helped the Rasool (ﷺ) through thick and thin were known as Ansar or 'Helpers'."  Solved Questions: I. Reference in the first sentence: • In the clause 'this city', the demonstrative noun phrase refers backward to 'Medina'. This is an ANAPHORIC reference (referring back to an already mentioned entity). • Similarly, in the second sentence, the pronoun 'They' refers back to 'faithful followers', and 'him' refers back to 'Hazrat Muhammad (ﷺ)' (both anaphoric references).  II. Transitive and Intransitive Verbs: • Transitive Verbs (require a direct object): 'abandoned' (Object: the ties of kith and kin), 'followed' (Object: him), 'helped' (Object: the Rasool). • Intransitive Verbs (do not require an object): 'migrated' (Intransitive verb of motion), 'was inhabited' (Passive form of intransitive state).  III. Main Idea of the Passage: • The main idea is to delineate the multi-ethnic demographic composition of Medina following the Prophet's migration, emphasizing the distinct identities, unwavering sacrifices, and brotherly roles of both the Muhajireen (Emigrants) and the Ansar (Helpers).  IV. Tense of the Passage: • The dominant tense is Past Simple (Indefinite) Tense (e.g., migrated, was inhabited, followed, were known) combined with Past Perfect Tense (e.g., had abandoned, had helped) to indicate actions completed before other past events.

box teach | Adverbs: Time, Place, Manner, Degree, and Frequency
p Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Adverbs of manner describe *how* (e.g., 'judged fairly'), time describes *when* ('promulgated in 622 CE'), place describes *where* ('gathered locally'), and degree describes *intensity* ('extremely significant').
- Manner: The Prophet ruled justly.
- Time: The charter was signed immediately upon migration.
- Degree: It was completely revolutionary.
endbox
box teach | Adverb Clauses of Reason and Condition
p Adverb clauses of reason start with 'because', 'since', or 'as' ('Since disputes arose, an arbiter was appointed'). Adverb clauses of condition start with 'if', 'unless', or 'provided that' ('If an enemy attacks, all shall defend').
- Because society was fractured, a written constitution was drafted.
- If Madina were attacked, the Jews and Muslims would fight together.
endbox

qp 3 | Comprehensive Grammar Mastery: The Mechanics and Usage of Present and Past Perfect Continuous Tenses.
sol Solution ## Marks: 8 Marks · Outline: I. Definition and Core Concept of Perfect Continuous Aspects II. Present Perfect Continuous: Form, Rules, and 'Since vs. For' III. Past Perfect Continuous: Form, Interrupted Past Actions, and Duration IV. Comparative Conjugation Matrix (Affirmative, Negative, Interrogative) V. Practical Error Analysis and Board Exam Tips · Answer: The Perfect Continuous (Progressive) tenses are essential for expressing duration and unbroken continuity across time.  1. Present Perfect Continuous Tense: • Purpose: Denotes an action that commenced in the past and is still actively continuing in the present moment. • Formula: Subject + has/have been + Present Participle (verb + ing) + [since/for + time]. • 'Since' is used for a point in time (since Monday, since 2010, since 6 o'clock). • 'For' is used for a period or duration (for two hours, for five years, for three days). • Examples: 'She has been studying since morning.' / 'They have been playing for two hours.'  2. Past Perfect Continuous Tense: • Purpose: Describes an ongoing action that began in the past and continued up until another specific event occurred in the past. • Formula: Subject + had been + Present Participle (verb + ing) + [since/for + time]. • Examples: 'He had been cycling for two hours before he felt exhausted.' / 'They had been waiting for 90 minutes when the train arrived.'  3. Structural Transformations: • Affirmative: 'He has been teaching.' / 'He had been teaching.' • Negative: 'He has not been teaching.' / 'He had not been teaching.' • Interrogative: 'Has he been teaching?' / 'Had he been teaching?'  Mastery of these tenses enables students to convey precise temporal relationships in academic English.
