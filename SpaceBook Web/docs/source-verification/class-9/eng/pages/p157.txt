box activity | Poem Summary
p Summary of 'Abou Ben Adhem'
p Content: In this inspiring narrative poem, Leigh Hunt illustrates the sublime spiritual truth that loving humanity is the most direct pathway to earning God's love. One night, Abou Ben Adhem, a pious and peaceful man, awakens to see an angel bathed in silvery moonlight, writing in a golden book. Abou respectfully asks what the angel is writing. The angel gently replies that it is listing the names of those who love God. Upon learning that his own name is not included, Abou remains undismayed and humbly requests to be recorded as one who loves his fellow human beings. The next night, the angel returns surrounded by a glorious, awakening light and shows the names of those blessed by God's divine love—and Abou Ben Adhem's name leads all the rest. The poem beautifully concludes that selfless service to humanity is the supreme act of worship.
endbox
box activity | Essay Haqooq Ul Ebad
p Essay: Haqooq-ul-Ebad — Rights of Fellow Human Beings (100–150 Words)
p Content: In Islamic jurisprudence and universal ethics, human duties are broadly categorized into two spheres: 'Haqooq-Allah' (the Rights of Allah, such as prayer and fasting) and 'Haqooq-ul-Ebad' (the Rights of Fellow Human Beings). While Almighty Allah may forgive shortcomings in His personal worship out of His infinite mercy, He has ordained that violations against fellow human beings will not be forgiven until the wronged individual pardons them.  Haqooq-ul-Ebad encompasses treating parents with utmost kindness, supporting orphans, aiding neighbors regardless of faith, providing charity to the poor, maintaining honesty in business, and safeguarding others from harm. Serving suffering humanity is the greatest manifestation of faith. A society grounded in Haqooq-ul-Ebad fosters universal brotherhood, social justice, and lasting peace.
endbox
