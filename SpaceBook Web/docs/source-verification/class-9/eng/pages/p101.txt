q 9 | Sentence Classification
qp 1 | This is a simple sentence.
sol Solution ## Classification: Simple Sentence · Clause Breakdown: One independent clause: Subject ('This') + Verb ('is') + Subject Complement ('a simple sentence').
qp 2 | I like playing basketball, and my brother likes playing tennis.
sol Solution ## Classification: Compound Sentence · Clause Breakdown: Two independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunction 'and': [I like playing basketball] + and + [my brother likes playing tennis].
qp 3 | I'll help you if you help me.
sol Solution ## Classification: Complex Sentence · Clause Breakdown: One independent clause [I'll help you] + one subordinate conditional clause [if you help me].
qp 4 | Her name is Sachiko and she comes from Japan.
sol Solution ## Classification: Compound Sentence · Clause Breakdown: Two independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunction 'and': [Her name is Sachiko] + and + [she comes from Japan].
qp 5 | My mother cooked dinner while I was doing my homework.
sol Solution ## Classification: Complex Sentence · Clause Breakdown: One independent clause [My mother cooked dinner] + one subordinate time clause [while I was doing my homework].
qp 6 | While the dog chased the cat, it barked loudly.
sol Solution ## Classification: Complex Sentence · Clause Breakdown: Subordinate clause [While the dog chased the cat] + independent clause [it barked loudly].
qp 7 | Although he worked hard, he did not do well in the examination.
sol Solution ## Classification: Complex Sentence · Clause Breakdown: Subordinate concessive clause [Although he worked hard] + independent clause [he did not do well in the examination].
qp 8 | The boys did not go to the zoo because they went to the park.
sol Solution ## Classification: Complex Sentence · Clause Breakdown: Independent clause [The boys did not go to the zoo] + subordinate causal clause [because they went to the park].

qp 1 | LQ1. Analyze the character of Zarin Gul as a tragic hero who stood for uncompromising artistic integrity.
sol In 'The Fantastic Shoemaker', Waheedullah constructs Zarin G ## In 'The Fantastic Shoemaker', Waheedullah constructs Zarin Gul as a classic tragic hero—a noble individual whose unwavering commitment to perfection ultimately leads to his economic ruin and death in an industrialized world.  1. Nobility of Craft: Zarin Gul regards shoemaking not as a commercial trade, but as an art. His workshop resembles a quiet, sacred sanctuary. He never produces speculative stock and never uses inferior leather.  2. Uncompromising Conscience: When faced with a customer complaint, he immediately offers a complete refund, viewing defective work as a personal failure. He refuses advance payment, adhering strictly to a code of dignity.  3. Resistance to Corporate Exploitation: Unlike other craftsmen who capitulate to big firms, Zarin Gul refuses to become an unthinking cog in a corporate machine. He declares: 'I am an artist not machine'.  4. Tragic Climax: His tragic flaw—or rather his greatest virtue—is his refusal to adapt to a deceptive, advertisement-driven market. He starves himself to buy genuine leather and pay rent, dying a martyr to authentic craftsmanship.
qp 2 | LQ2. Discuss the central conflict between Traditional Craftsmanship and Modern Corporate Industrialization in the lesson.
sol The core thematic conflict of the story revolves around the ## The core thematic conflict of the story revolves around the clash between indigenous, bespoke craftsmanship and modern multinational industrial capitalism.  1. Quality vs. Mass Production: Zarin Gul stitches durability, anatomical comfort, and individual care into every pair of shoes. In contrast, multinational firms produce flimsy, mass-manufactured footwear that pinches the wearer's feet.  2. Merit vs. Advertisement: Big companies dominate the market not through superior product quality, but through aggressive marketing, glossy showrooms, and psychological manipulation. qp 3 | LQ3. Detailed Exposition of Sentence Structures: Simple, Compound, and Complex sentences with definitions, coordinating conjunctions, and examples.
sol English sentences are classified based on clause structure i ## English sentences are classified based on clause structure into three primary categories:  1. Simple Sentence: - Definition: Contains exactly one independent clause with a single subject and predicate. - Examples:   a) 'She completed her literature review.'   b) 'Zarin Gul crafted durable Peshawari chappals.'  2. Compound Sentence: - Definition: Contains two or more independent clauses joined by a comma and coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So) or a semicolon. - Examples:   a) 'I like playing basketball, and my brother likes playing tennis.'   b) 'He organized his sources by theme; then, he updated his reference list.'  3. Complex Sentence: - Definition: Contains one independent clause and at least one dependent (subordinate) clause introduced by subordinating conjunctions (because, although, if, while, when, since, etc.). - Examples:   a) 'Although he worked hard, he did not do well in the examination.'   b) 'The boys did not go to the zoo because they went to the park.'  Mastering these structures enables students to vary syntactic rhythm and construct cohesive analytical essays.
