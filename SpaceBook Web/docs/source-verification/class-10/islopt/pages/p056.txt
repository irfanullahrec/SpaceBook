The Prophet permitted the writing of hadith, and its foundation was laid in his time: he had a short treatise on the rules for dhimmis written for Hazrat Ali, rules on charity for Amr ibn Hazm, governor of Yemen, and the Conquest of Makkah sermon for Abu Shah of Yemen; he had the terms of Hudaybiyah written and given to Suhayl ibn Amr, and wrote letters of invitation to kings. Thus a large stock of hadith had been gathered in the Prophet's own time. B: The Sahihayn are Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Sahih Muslim (Imam Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj al-Qushayri al-Naysaburi, 206–261 AH) has about 4,000 hadiths selected from three hundred thousand. The two imams are called the "Shaykhayn", and a hadith found in both is called "muttafaq alayh". C: Al-Kafi, by Muhammad ibn Ya'qub al-Kulayni, is the most reliable Shi'i book, in three parts: Usul al-Kafi (principles of the faith; eight books in two volumes), Furu' al-Kafi (laws; 11,156 hadiths in eight volumes) and Rawdat al-Kafi (various religious aspects and writings of the Imams).
