Model answer ## For an object of height $h_o$ at distance $d_o$ from a concave mirror, the image has height $h_i$ at distance $d_i$. Two similar triangles (the ray striking the pole P and the ray through the centre of curvature C) give $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$ and, from the triangles at the focus F, $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{f}{d_o - f}$. Equating, $\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = \dfrac{f}{d_o - f}$, which gives $d_i(d_o - f) = f d_o$ and so $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i}$, the mirror equation. Magnification: $m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$.
@@ p107:6
Model answer ## Refraction is the change of direction of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes. The index of refraction is $n = c/v$, the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium. Laws (Snell): (1) the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal lie in the same plane; (2) $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$, i.e. the ratio $\sin\theta_1/\sin\theta_2$ is constant for the two media. A ray entering a denser medium bends towards the normal; entering a rarer medium it bends away.
@@ p107:7
Model answer ## Total internal reflection (TIR) is the complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when it meets a boundary with a rarer medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle. The critical angle is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90°: $\sin\theta_c = n_2/n_1$ ($n_1 > n_2$). Conditions: (1) light must travel from a denser to a rarer medium; (2) the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

Model answer ## For a thin lens, an object of height $h_o$ at distance $d_o$ forms an image of height $h_i$ at distance $d_i$. Using similar triangles formed by the ray through the optical centre and the ray through the focus, $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$ and $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i - f}{f}$. Equating, $\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = \dfrac{d_i - f}{f}$, so $f d_i = d_o d_i - f d_o$; dividing by $d_o d_i f$ gives $\dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, the thin-lens equation (with the sign convention, the magnification is $m = -d_i/d_o$).
