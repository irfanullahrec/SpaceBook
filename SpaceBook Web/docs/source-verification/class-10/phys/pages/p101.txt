example 12.8 | BIOLOGY LABORATORY
p In biology class, a student with a near-point distance of N = 25 cm uses a microscope to view an amoeba. If the objective has a focal length of 1.0 cm, the eyepiece has a focal length of 2.5 cm, and the amoeba is 1.1 cm from the objective, what is the magnification produced by the microscope?
### GIVEN
p Focal length of eye piece 'fₑ' = 2.5 cm. Focal length of objective 'fₒ' = 1.0 cm. Near-point distance of N = 25 cm. Object distance 'dₒ' = 1.1 cm
### REQUIRED
p Total magnification 'm' = ?
### SOLUTION
p The image distance dᵢ for the equation 12.13 is not given, therefore we will first find the image distance by using the thin-lens formula
m 1/fₒ = 1/dᵢ + 1/dₒ  or  1/dᵢ = 1/fₒ − 1/dₒ  taking LCM  1/dᵢ = (dₒ − fₒ)/(fₒ dₒ)
m or  dᵢ = fₒ dₒ / (dₒ − fₒ)   ①
p Putting values
m dᵢ = (1.1 cm × 1.0 cm) / (1.0 cm − 1.1 cm)  and  dᵢ = 1.1 cm² / −0.1 cm
m dᵢ = −11 cm
p The magnification formula for compound microscope is
m m = −(dᵢ / fₒ) × (N / fₑ)
p Putting values
m m = −(−11 cm / 1.0 cm) × (25 cm / 2.5 cm)
p Therefore m = −110   [Answer]
p Thus, the amoeba appears 110 times larger and is inverted. If the amoeba is to be viewed with a relaxed eye, the image formed by the objective should be at the focal point of the eyepiece, which will then form an image at infinity. Therefore, the length of the tube containing the objective and eyepiece is L = 11 cm + 2.5 cm = 13.5 cm in this case.
endexample
