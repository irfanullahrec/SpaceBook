p Figure 12.32 (a) shows that the angle subtended by the object without the magnifier is given by
m tanθ = Perpendicular / base = hₒ / N   ②
p Assuming that the eye is next to the magnifying glass, the angular size as seen by the eye as in figure 12.32 (b) is
m tanθ′ = Perpendicular / base = hₒ / dₒ   ③
p where f is the focal length of the lens. We assume that the object is placed at the focal length of the lens, so the image is at minus infinity. For small-angles tanθ ≈ θ and tanθ′ ≈ θ′. Thus, by putting values from equation 2 and equation 3 in equation 1, the angular magnification of a magnifier can be written as
m mθ = (hₒ / dₒ) / (hₒ / N)  or  mθ = N / dₒ   ④
p According to the thin-lens equation, dₒ is related to the image distance dᵢ and the focal length f of the lens by
m 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ  or  1/dₒ = 1/f − 1/dᵢ   ⑤
p putting values from equation 5 in equation 4, we get
m mθ = [1/f − 1/dᵢ] N   (12.12)
p Two special cases of this result are of interest, depending on whether the image is located as close to the eye as possible or as far away as possible.
p To be seen clearly, the closest the image can be relative to the eye is at the near point, or dᵢ = −N. The minus sign indicates that the image lies to the left of the lens and is virtual. In this event, Equation 12.12 becomes
m mθ = [1/f − 1/(−N)] N  or  mθ = [1/f + 1/N] N
