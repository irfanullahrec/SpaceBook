exercise 11
q 1 | MULTIPLE CHOICE QUESTIONS
qp 1 | The intensity of a sound wave increases by 1000 W/m². What is this increase equal to in decibels? (A) 10 (B) 20 (C) 30 (D) 40
sol C. 30 dB ## A rise of 1000 times in intensity changes the level by $\Delta\beta = 10\log_{10}(1000) = 10\times 3 = 30$ dB.
qp 2 | An echo occurs when a sound wave is ... (A) absorbed (B) transmitted (C) refracted (D) reflected
sol D. reflected ## An echo is the repetition of a sound heard after the sound wave is reflected from a hard, distant surface.
qp 3 | Compared with a sound of 60 decibels, a sound of 80 decibels has an intensity ______ times greater. (A) 10 (B) 100 (C) 1000 (D) 10000
sol B. 100 ## The levels differ by $80-60 = 20$ dB, so $10\log_{10}(I_2/I_1)=20$ and $I_2/I_1 = 10^{2} = 100$.
qp 4 | The loudness of a sound is most closely related to its ______. (A) frequency (B) period (C) wavelength (D) amplitude
sol D. amplitude ## Loudness is the perception of intensity, and intensity is proportional to the square of the amplitude of the wave.
qp 5 | Various instruments sound different even when they play the same note due to difference in (A) pitch (B) loudness (C) quality (D) intensity
sol C. quality ## Quality (timbre) depends on the mixture of harmonics a source produces, so two instruments playing the same note (same pitch and loudness) still sound different.
qp 6 | Humans can hear sound that is (A) less than 20 Hz (B) between 20 Hz and 20 kHz (C) greater than 20 kHz (D) None
sol B. between 20 Hz and 20 kHz ## The audible range of the human ear is about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz; below it is infrasound and above it is ultrasound.
qp 7 | The speed of sound on a warm day when the outdoor temperature is 38°C is (A) 331 m/s (B) 345 m/s (C) 354 m/s (D) 362 m/s
sol C. 354 m/s ## $v = 331 + 0.6\,T = 331 + 0.6(38) = 353.8 \approx 354$ m/s.
qp 8 | Minimum echo distance is reduced in (A) summer (B) winter (C) spring (D) space
sol B. winter ## The minimum echo distance is $d = v t/2$ with $t = 0.1$ s, so it is smaller when the speed of sound is smaller. Sound travels slower in cold air, so the minimum distance is reduced in winter.
qp 9 | Which of the following cannot transmit sound? (A) solid (B) liquid (C) gas (D) vacuum
sol D. vacuum ## Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a material medium (solid, liquid or gas); a vacuum has no particles to vibrate.
q 2 | CONCEPTUAL QUESTIONS. Give a brief response to the following questions
q 1 | Why sound produced by a simple pendulum is not heard?
sol Model answer ## A simple pendulum oscillates about once per second (frequency of about 1 Hz). This is far below 20 Hz, the lower limit of human hearing (infrasound), so the sound it produces cannot be heard.
q 2 | If a ringing bicycle bell is held tightly by hand, it stops producing sound. Why?
sol Model answer ## A sounding bell must vibrate freely. When it is held tightly the hand damps (absorbs) its vibrations, so the bell cannot vibrate and no sound is produced.
q 3 | Why is the intensity of an echo less than that of the original sound?
sol Model answer ## Part of the sound energy is absorbed by the reflecting surface, and the reflected wave spreads over a larger area as it travels back (intensity falls with the square of the distance), so the echo is weaker than the original sound.
q 4 | In which medium air or water, an echo is heard sooner? Why?
sol Model answer ## In water. Sound travels about 1500 m/s in water but only about 340 m/s in air, so the reflected sound returns sooner and the echo is heard earlier.
q 5 | Why sound cannot be heard on the moon?
sol Model answer ## The moon has no atmosphere. Sound needs a material medium to travel, and in the vacuum there is nothing to carry the vibrations, so sound cannot be heard.
