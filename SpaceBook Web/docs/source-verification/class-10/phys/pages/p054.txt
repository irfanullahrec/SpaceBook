q 6 | If a person places his ear on rails of railroad for determination of coming train. Why is this done, and how does it work?
sol Model answer ## Sound travels much faster and with less loss in steel (about 5000 m/s) than in air (about 340 m/s). The rails carry the vibrations of the approaching train long before the sound arrives through air, so the person hears the train earlier and can judge that it is coming.
q 7 | When you watch a thunderstorm, you see the lightning first, and you hear the thunder afterward. Why is the thunder delayed?
sol Model answer ## Light travels at $3\times10^{8}$ m/s, so the flash reaches us almost instantly, while sound travels only about 340 m/s. The thunder is delayed by the time $t = d/v$ the sound needs to cover the distance to the storm.
q 8 | If the speed of sound is dependent on frequency, would music from marching band be enjoyed?
sol Model answer ## No. If the speed of sound depended on frequency, notes of different frequency would reach the listener at different times and the music would become distorted. Because in air all frequencies travel at nearly the same speed, the band's music arrives together and is enjoyed.
q 9 | Why does your voice sound fuller in the shower?
sol Model answer ## The hard, smooth walls of a shower reflect sound with little absorption. The reflected sound reaches the ear almost at once and merges with the original sound (reverberation), which makes the voice sound louder and fuller.
q 10 | Why is it so quiet after a snowfall?
sol Model answer ## Fresh snow is soft and porous with a lot of trapped air. It absorbs sound instead of reflecting it, so there is almost no reflection or reverberation and the surroundings become very quiet.
q 3 | COMPREHENSIVE QUESTIONS. Give an extended response to the following questions
q 1 | What is sound? How it is produced, transmitted and received?
sol Model answer ## Sound is a form of energy produced by a vibrating body. Production: a vibrating source (vocal cords, string, tuning fork) disturbs the surrounding particles. Transmission: the disturbance travels as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions through a material medium (solid, liquid or gas); it cannot travel in a vacuum. Reception: the wave reaches the ear, makes the eardrum vibrate, and the vibrations are passed on to the inner ear and then as signals to the brain.
q 2 | What is audible frequency range?
sol Model answer ## The audible range is the range of frequencies the human ear can hear, about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz). Sounds below 20 Hz are infrasound and above 20 kHz are ultrasound; both are inaudible to humans.
q 3 | What is the speed of sound?
sol Model answer ## The speed of sound is the distance a sound wave travels in one second, $v = f\lambda$. In air at 0 °C it is about 331 m/s and increases by about 0.6 m/s for each °C: $v = 331 + 0.6\,T$. It is greater in liquids (about 1500 m/s in water) and greatest in solids (about 5000 m/s in steel).
q 4 | Describe the terms loudness, pitch and quality. Explain each by giving an example.
sol Model answer ## Loudness is the sensation of how strong a sound is; it depends on the intensity (square of the amplitude), e.g. a shout is louder than a whisper. Pitch is the sensation of how high or low a sound is; it depends on the frequency, e.g. a whistle has a higher pitch than a drum. Quality (timbre) is the characteristic that lets us tell two sounds of the same loudness and pitch apart, because of different harmonics, e.g. a sitar and a flute playing the same note.
q 5 | What is intensity level? Describe the decibels scale for the intensity of different sound levels.
sol Model answer ## Intensity is the sound energy passing per second through a unit area perpendicular to the direction of travel, $I = P/A$ in W/m². Intensity level $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ in decibels (dB), where $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m² is the threshold of hearing. Each increase of 10 dB means ten times the intensity. Examples: whisper 30 dB, normal conversation 60 dB, heavy traffic 80 dB, jet engine 120 dB, and about 120-130 dB is the threshold of pain.
q 6 | What is noise? Explain why noise is nuisance?
sol Model answer ## Noise is an unwanted, unpleasant sound with irregular vibrations (traffic, machines, loudspeakers). It is a nuisance because it disturbs sleep, concentration and conversation, causes stress and headaches and, if loud or prolonged (above about 85 dB), can damage hearing.
q 7 | How sound is reflected? Describe the difference between echo and reverberation.
sol Model answer ## Sound is reflected when it strikes a hard surface (wall, cliff) and obeys the laws of reflection. An echo is a reflected sound heard separately after the original sound; it needs a reflecting surface at least about 17 m away (the reflected sound arrives at least 0.1 s later). Reverberation is the persistence of sound in a room caused by many reflections that arrive less than 0.1 s apart and blend with the original sound, so there is no separate repetition.
q 8 | What is acoustic protection? Why is it important?
sol Model answer ## Acoustic protection is the control of sound so that unwanted noise is reduced and wanted sound is heard clearly: using sound-absorbing materials (carpets, curtains, foam, acoustic tiles), double walls and windows, ear plugs or ear muffs and sound barriers. It is important to protect hearing and health, to reduce noise pollution and to give good sound quality in halls and studios.
q 4 | NUMERICAL QUESTIONS
q 1 | The sound intensity 3 m from a jackhammer is 8.20 × 10⁻² W/m². What is the sound intensity level in decibels? (Use the usual reference level of I₀ = 1.00 × 10⁻¹² W/m².)
sol 109 dB ## $\beta = 10\log_{10}\!\dfrac{I}{I_0} = 10\log_{10}\!\dfrac{8.20\times10^{-2}}{1.00\times10^{-12}} = 10\log_{10}(8.20\times10^{10}) = 10(10.91) \approx 109$ dB.
q 2 | A ship is anchored where the depth of water is 120 m. An ultrasonic signal sent to the bottom of the lake returns in 0.16 s. What is the speed of sound in water?
sol 1500 m/s ## The signal travels to the bottom and back, a distance $2d = 2(120) = 240$ m in $0.16$ s, so $v = \dfrac{2d}{t} = \dfrac{240}{0.16} = 1500$ m/s.
q 3 | A gunshot from a 0.22 rimfire rifle has an intensity of about I = (2.5 × 10¹³) W/m². Do we need to wear ear protection? (Considering that prolonged exposure to sounds above 85 decibels can cause hearing damage or loss).
sol Yes, ear protection is needed ## $\beta = 10\log_{10}\!\dfrac{I}{I_0} = 10\log_{10}\!\dfrac{2.5\times10^{13}}{1.00\times10^{-12}} = 10\log_{10}(2.5\times10^{25}) \approx 254$ dB (as printed). This is far above 85 dB, so ear protection is certainly required. (The printed intensity looks unrealistically large for a rifle shot; a real shot is about 140 dB, still far above the 85 dB limit.)
q 4 | What sound intensity level in dB is produced by earphones that create an intensity of 4.00 × 10⁻² W/m²? (Use the usual reference level of I₀ = 1.00 × 10⁻¹² W/m².)
sol 106 dB ## $\beta = 10\log_{10}\!\dfrac{4.00\times10^{-2}}{1.00\times10^{-12}} = 10\log_{10}(4.00\times10^{10}) = 10(10.60) \approx 106$ dB.
