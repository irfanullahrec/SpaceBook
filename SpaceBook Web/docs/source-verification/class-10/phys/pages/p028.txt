sol Model answers ## 1: They have equal numbers of electrons and protons, so the negative and positive charges cancel and the net charge is zero. 2: Rubbing feet on the carpet transfers electrons, so the body (and hair) gets a net charge of one sign; the like-charged hairs repel each other and stand up. 3: The charged object induces opposite charge on the near side of the neutral object and like charge on the far side; the nearer opposite charge is attracted more strongly than the farther like charge is repelled, so the net force is always attraction. 4: On touching, some charge flows to the paper, giving it the same sign as the comb; like charges repel, so the paper flies away. 5: No. Bringing the charged body near the ball redistributes charge by induction: opposite charge gathers on the ball and like charge on the leaves, which repel and diverge. 6: The charged paint particles repel each other and spread evenly, and are attracted to the oppositely charged (earthed) object, giving uniform coverage, less paint use and less paint lost in the air. 7: Being highest, the rod is the nearest point to the cloud, so the strong field there makes the lightning strike the rod and its current is led safely to earth instead of through the building. 8: Charge could not spread over the insulating plates, so they could not hold charge +Q and −Q across their surfaces; the device would store almost no charge and would not work as a capacitor. 9: A capacitor stores electrical energy in the electric field between the plates (equal and opposite charges are separated). 10: In parallel, because the equivalent capacitance (Cₑ = C₁ + C₂ + …) is larger, so more charge, and hence more energy, is stored at the same voltage. || 1: ان میں الیکٹرانوں اور پروٹونوں کی تعداد برابر ہوتی ہے، اس
