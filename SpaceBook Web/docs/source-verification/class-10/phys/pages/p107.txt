q 3 | COMPREHENSIVE QUESTIONS. Give an extended response to the following questions
q 1 | What is meant by reflection of light? State and explain laws of reflection with diagrams.
sol Model answer ## Reflection is the bouncing back of light when it strikes a surface. Laws: (1) the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane; (2) the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, $\theta_i = \theta_r$. (Diagram: a ray AO hits the mirror at O; the normal ON is perpendicular to the surface; the reflected ray OB makes the angle $\theta_r = \theta_i$ with ON.)
q 2 | Derive spherical mirror formula.
sol Model answer ## For an object of height $h_o$ at distance $d_o$ from a concave mirror, the image has height $h_i$ at distance $d_i$. Two similar triangles (the ray striking the pole P and the ray through the centre of curvature C) give $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$ and, from the triangles at the focus F, $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{f}{d_o - f}$. Equating, $\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = \dfrac{f}{d_o - f}$, which gives $d_i(d_o - f) = f d_o$ and so $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i}$, the mirror equation. Magnification: $m = \dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$.
q 3 | What is meant by refraction of light? What is the index of refraction? State and explain laws of refraction with diagrams.
sol Model answer ## Refraction is the change of direction of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes. The index of refraction is $n = c/v$, the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium. Laws (Snell): (1) the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal lie in the same plane; (2) $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$, i.e. the ratio $\sin\theta_1/\sin\theta_2$ is constant for the two media. A ray entering a denser medium bends towards the normal; entering a rarer medium it bends away.
q 4 | What is total internal reflection? How we can calculate the critical angle for total internal reflection? What are the conditions for total internal reflection?
sol Model answer ## Total internal reflection (TIR) is the complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when it meets a boundary with a rarer medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle. The critical angle is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90°: $\sin\theta_c = n_2/n_1$ ($n_1 > n_2$). Conditions: (1) light must travel from a denser to a rarer medium; (2) the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.
q 5 | What are optical fibres? Give some applications of optical fibers.
sol Model answer ## An optical fibre is a thin, flexible strand of glass or plastic made of a core surrounded by a cladding of lower refractive index. Light entering one end is carried to the other by repeated total internal reflections at the core-cladding boundary with very little loss. Applications: telephone, television and internet communication cables, medical endoscopes to look inside the body, laser surgery, decorative lamps and sensors.
q 6 | Describe the behavior for a ray of light after passing through a prism.
sol Model answer ## When a ray of light passes through a glass prism it is refracted at both faces, bending towards the base each time, so the emergent ray is deviated from the incident direction. White light also splits into its seven colours (dispersion) because each colour has a different speed in glass and therefore a different refractive index: red is bent least and violet most, giving a spectrum.
q 7 | Derive thin lens equation.
sol Model answer ## For a thin lens, an object of height $h_o$ at distance $d_o$ forms an image of height $h_i$ at distance $d_i$. Using similar triangles formed by the ray through the optical centre and the ray through the focus, $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i}{d_o}$ and $\dfrac{h_i}{h_o} = \dfrac{d_i - f}{f}$. Equating, $\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = \dfrac{d_i - f}{f}$, so $f d_i = d_o d_i - f d_o$; dividing by $d_o d_i f$ gives $\dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, the thin-lens equation (with the sign convention, the magnification is $m = -d_i/d_o$).
q 8 | Describe power of lens and its resolving power. What are its units?
sol Model answer ## The power of a lens is its ability to bend light and is $P = 1/f$ with $f$ in metres; its unit is the dioptre (D = m⁻¹). A converging lens has positive power and a diverging lens negative power. The resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to show two close objects as separate; it is the reciprocal of the smallest angle $\theta$ that can be resolved, $\theta \approx 1.22\lambda/D$, so it increases with a larger aperture $D$ and shorter wavelength. Its unit is the reciprocal of radian (rad⁻¹).
q 9 | How the human eye works? How the defects in the eye like short sightedness and long sightedness be corrected by using lenses?
sol Model answer ## The eye is a lens system: light enters through the cornea and pupil (the iris controls its size), the eye lens focuses it, and a real, inverted image forms on the retina; the optic nerve carries the signal to the brain, which interprets it as upright. The ciliary muscles change the shape of the lens to focus near and far objects (accommodation). Short sightedness (myopia): the image forms in front of the retina, so distant objects are blurred; it is corrected with a concave (diverging) lens. Long sightedness (hypermetropia): the image forms behind the retina, so near objects are blurred; it is corrected with a convex (converging) lens.
q 10 | What is a simple microscope? Using a ray diagram explain its working, angular magnification and magnifying power.
sol Model answer ## A simple microscope is a single convex lens of short focal length used as a magnifying glass. The object is placed inside the focal length, so the lens forms an upright, enlarged, virtual image at the near point (25 cm). Angular magnification $M = \theta'/\theta$; for the image at the near point $M = 1 + \dfrac{N}{f}$ with $N = 25$ cm, and for the image at infinity $M = N/f$. (Ray diagram: the object lies between F and the lens; the rays diverge after the lens and appear to come from a larger image behind the object.)
q 11 | What is a compound microscope? Using a ray diagram for a compound microscope, mention its magnifying power.
sol Model answer ## A compound microscope uses two convex lenses: an objective of very short focal length that forms a real, inverted, magnified image of the object, and an eyepiece that acts as a magnifier of this image and gives a final virtual, inverted, highly magnified image. The total magnifying power is the product of the two magnifications, $M = m_o \times M_e = \left(-\dfrac{d_i}{d_o}\right)\left(1 + \dfrac{N}{f_e}\right)$; for a long tube it is about $M \approx -\dfrac{L N}{f_o f_e}$.
q 12 | What is a telescope? Using a ray diagram explain its working, angular magnification and magnifying power.
sol Model answer ## A telescope is an optical instrument that makes distant objects look nearer and bigger. A refracting (astronomical) telescope has an objective lens of long focal length $f_o$ that forms a real image of the distant object at its focal point, and an eyepiece of short focal length $f_e$ that magnifies this image; the lens separation is $L = f_o + f_e$ for the image at infinity. The angular magnification (magnifying power) is $M = \dfrac{\theta'}{\theta} = \dfrac{f_o}{f_e}$ (the final image is inverted).
q 4 | NUMERICAL QUESTIONS
q 1 | A 1.50 cm-high object is placed 20.0 cm from a concave mirror with radius of curvature 30.0 cm. Determine (a) the position of the image, and (b) its size, also draw the ray diagrams.
sol (a) 60 cm in front of the mirror (b) 4.5 cm, inverted ## $f = R/2 = 15.0$ cm. (a) $\dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{d_o} = \dfrac{1}{15.0} - \dfrac{1}{20.0} = \dfrac{1}{60}$, so $d_i = 60$ cm, a real image in front of the mirror. (b) $m = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = -\dfrac{60}{20} = -3$, so $h_i = m h_o = -3(1.50) = -4.5$ cm: the image is 4.5 cm high, real and inverted. (Ray diagram: parallel ray through F after reflection; ray through F parallel after reflection; ray to the pole; they meet at 60 cm.)
q 2 | A candle of height 8.0 cm is located at a distance of 300 mm from a convex mirror, its virtual image is formed behind the mirror at a distance of 3.0 cm from the pole (or vertex). Find the focal length of the mirror and height of the image formed.
sol $f = -3.3$ cm; $h_i = 0.8$ cm ## $d_o = 300$ mm $= 30$ cm and the virtual image is behind the mirror, $d_i = -3.0$ cm. $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{d_o} + \dfrac{1}{d_i} = \dfrac{1}{30} - \dfrac{1}{3.0} = \dfrac{1 - 10}{30} = -\dfrac{9}{30}$, so $f = -3.33$ cm. $m = -\dfrac{d_i}{d_o} = \dfrac{3.0}{30} = 0.10$, so $h_i = 0.10\times 8.0 = 0.8$ cm (upright and diminished).
q 3 | Calculate the speed of light in zircon with index of refraction n = 1.923, a material used in jewelry to replicate diamond.
sol $1.56\times10^{8}$ m/s ## $v = \dfrac{c}{n} = \dfrac{3.00\times10^{8}}{1.923} = 1.56\times10^{8}$ m/s.
