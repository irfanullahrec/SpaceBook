Model answer ## Sound is a form of energy produced by a vibrating body. Production: a vibrating source (vocal cords, string, tuning fork) disturbs the surrounding particles. Transmission: the disturbance travels as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions through a material medium (solid, liquid or gas); it cannot travel in a vacuum. Reception: the wave reaches the ear, makes the eardrum vibrate, and the vibrations are passed on to the inner ear and then as signals to the brain.

Model answer ## The speed of sound is the distance a sound wave travels in one second, $v = f\lambda$. In air at 0 °C it is about 331 m/s and increases by about 0.6 m/s for each °C: $v = 331 + 0.6\,T$. It is greater in liquids (about 1500 m/s in water) and greatest in solids (about 5000 m/s in steel).

Model answer ## Loudness is the sensation of how strong a sound is; it depends on the intensity (square of the amplitude), e.g. a shout is louder than a whisper. Pitch is the sensation of how high or low a sound is; it depends on the frequency, e.g. a whistle has a higher pitch than a drum. Quality (timbre) is the characteristic that lets us tell two sounds of the same loudness and pitch apart, because of different harmonics, e.g. a sitar and a flute playing the same note.
@@ p054:13
Model answer ## Intensity is the sound energy passing per second through a unit area perpendicular to the direction of travel, $I = P/A$ in W/m². Intensity level $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ in decibels (dB), where $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m² is the threshold of hearing. Each increase of 10 dB means ten times the intensity. Examples: whisper 30 dB, normal conversation 60 dB, heavy traffic 80 dB, jet engine 120 dB, and about 120-130 dB is the threshold of pain.

Model answer ## Sound is reflected when it strikes a hard surface (wall, cliff) and obeys the laws of reflection. An echo is a reflected sound heard separately after the original sound; it needs a reflecting surface at least about 17 m away (the reflected sound arrives at least 0.1 s later). Reverberation is the persistence of sound in a room caused by many reflections that arrive less than 0.1 s apart and blend with the original sound, so there is no separate repetition.
