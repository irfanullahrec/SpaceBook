q 2 | B. Write short answers to the following questions. || B. درج ذیل سوالات کے مختصر جوابات لکھیے۔ || B. لاندې پوښتنو ته لنډ ځوابونه ولیکئ.
q 1 | Why do plants not need a specialised respiratory system? || پودوں کو مخصوص نظامِ تنفس کی ضرورت کیوں نہیں ہوتی؟ || ولې بوټي ځانګړي تنفسي سیسټم ته اړتیا نه لري؟
sol Model answer ## Plants have a large surface area (thin, flat leaves with many stomata and lenticels in stems), their cells are close to the air, their demand for oxygen is low, and gases move in and out by simple diffusion. They do not need a system to transport oxygen, since each part exchanges gases directly.
q 2 | Differentiate between the terms “respiration” and “breathing”. || “تنفس” اور “سانس لینا” کی اصطلاحات میں فرق بیان کیجیے۔ || د “تنفس” او “ساه اخیستلو” اصطلاحاتو ترمنځ توپیر وکړئ.
sol Model answer ## Breathing is the physical process of taking air into the lungs (inhalation) and pushing it out (exhalation). Respiration is the chemical process that takes place inside the cells, by which glucose is oxidized with oxygen to release energy (ATP), with carbon dioxide and water as products.
q 3 | How are the surface area of leaves and lungs important for gaseous exchange? || پتوں اور پھیپھڑوں کا سطحی رقبہ گیسوں کے تبادلے کے لیے کیسے اہم ہے؟ || د پاڼو او سږو سطحي مساحت د ګازونو د تبادلې لپاره څنګه مهم دی؟
sol Model answer ## A large surface area gives more room for gases to diffuse across. Leaves are broad and flat and have many air spaces, so $CO_2$ and $O_2$ exchange quickly; lungs contain millions of tiny alveoli that together give an area of about 70 m², all with thin, moist walls and many capillaries, so exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide is fast and efficient.
q 4 | What is the function of the cartilage present in the walls of the trachea and bronchi? || ٹریکیا اور برونکائی کی دیواروں میں موجود غضروف کا کیا کام ہے؟ || د ټراکیا او برونکای په دېوالونو کې د موجود غضروف دنده څه ده؟
sol Model answer ## The C-shaped rings of cartilage keep the trachea and bronchi open all the time so that air can pass freely, and they stop the tubes from collapsing when air pressure falls during inhalation.
