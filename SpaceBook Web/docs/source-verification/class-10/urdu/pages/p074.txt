As the moonlit night of Muzdalifah ended, those moments of majestic solitude that the crowds of Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah had given him also departed; in the solitude within the crowd of Arafat there had been nothing but peace. Returning to Makkah after the Hajj, he was seized by the longing to reach Madinah, but the mu'allim Abdul Razzaq Mahboob coldly said that the date for leaving for Madinah would be fixed by the Saudi government; until then he should be patient and not keep asking for his passport. Disappointed, he headed for the Kaaba when he saw a Pakistan Embassy car coming; an embassy official who knew him stopped and asked whether he would go to Madinah. Flustered, he said, "Yes, certainly — but how?" The official said a caravan of Pakistani pilgrims who had come overland was leaving Jeddah for Madinah that evening; if he wanted to join, he should come with them to Jeddah now. He ran to do the farewell tawaf and picked up his bundle; the embassy staff collected his passport from the mu'allim, and within three and a half hours he was in the caravan's bus heading for Madinah.
