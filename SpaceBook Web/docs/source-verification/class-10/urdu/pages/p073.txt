After Umrah and the dawn prayer the author leaned against a pillar to recite the Quran but grew drowsy, and with difficulty made his way back to his lodging. Near him by the stream a family from Bahawalpur had camped: an old couple and their young daughter-in-law. The mother-in-law and daughter-in-law quarrelled endlessly over every little thing; losing, the daughter-in-law would cry, "All right, do all the cruelty you want; I'll go and do tawaf now and take my complaint to Allah." Hearing this, the mother-in-law would anxiously seize her hem and beg her not to make such a mistake, for whatever is said during tawaf comes true. The author says the faith of these simple Muslims was so firm that on doing tawaf they reached, as it were, the summit of Mount Sinai and, conversing intimately with the True Lord, found peace of soul; they possessed certainty of faith and informally presented their requests before the Lord of the Kaaba and had them stamped as accepted. Leaving the city, a flood of people in ihram flowed towards Mina; lines of buses, trucks and cars crept along, and the air echoed with the talbiya.
