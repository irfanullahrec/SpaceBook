I compared my condition with his and was myself surprised to find that in most matters he was better off than I. It is true that I work while he lives on free food, and that I am educated while he is ignorant; I am well dressed and he wears torn clothes. Only that far am I better than he. Beyond that his condition is far better than mine. I ought to envy his health. I spend day and night in worry, while he lives in such contentment that, despite his whimpering and woeful face, cheerfulness showed on his face. For a long time I wondered what the reason for his enviable state was, and at last I reached this seemingly strange conclusion: what he thinks his misfortune is in fact a blessing for him. He says with regret, "I have no friend." I say with regret, "I have so many friends!"
