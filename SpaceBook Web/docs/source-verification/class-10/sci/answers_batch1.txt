@@ p085:8
Model answer ## The molecular basis of heredity is DNA: it is a double helix of two strands of nucleotides held by bases (A pairs with T and G with C). The sequence of bases forms the genetic code in triplets (codons) for amino acids; DNA is copied exactly in replication and passed on, and it is used to make proteins through RNA, so parents' traits are passed to children.
@@ p085:9
Model answer ## DNA replication is semi-conservative: the double helix unwinds and unzips by the enzyme helicase; each old strand acts as a template and new nucleotides pair with it (A-T, G-C) by DNA polymerase, so two identical DNA molecules form, each having one old and one new strand.
@@ p085:10
Model answer ## Mitosis gives two identical daughter cells with the same number of chromosomes (growth and repair). Prophase: chromosomes condense, the nuclear envelope breaks down, spindle forms. Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the equator. Anaphase: the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles. Telophase: nuclear envelopes form again and chromosomes uncoil. Cytokinesis then divides the cytoplasm.
@@ p085:11
Model answer ## Meiosis forms four haploid gametes in two divisions. Meiosis I: homologous chromosomes pair (prophase I with crossing over), line up, and separate (anaphase I), giving two cells with half the number of chromosomes. Meiosis II: the chromatids separate as in mitosis, giving four haploid cells. It halves the chromosome number and gives variation.
