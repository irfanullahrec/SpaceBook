sol Model answers ## 1: Both are C₆H₁₂O₆ hexoses; glucose is a pentahydroxy aldehyde (–CHO, an aldose), fructose a pentahydroxy ketone (C=O at carbon 2, a ketose); fructose is sweeter. 2: Long-term energy store; thermal insulation under the skin; protective cushion round organs; solvent for vitamins A, D, E (also cooking, soaps, cell membranes). 3: Simple lipids (fats, oils, waxes) give only fatty acids and alcohols on hydrolysis; complex lipids (phospholipids, glycolipids, sulpholipids) give fatty acids, alcohols and other substances. 4: Loss of a protein's folded shape when its hydrogen bonds and disulphide bridges are broken by heat (or acid, alcohol), e.g. egg white solidifying on boiling. 5: Fat soluble (A, D, E, K) and water soluble (B-complex, C). 6: The body cannot make them, yet they are needed in small amounts for normal metabolism, growth, immunity and to prevent deficiency diseases. 7: Glucose and fructose. 8: H₂N–CH(R)–COOH: amino group –NH₂, carboxylic group –COOH, both on the alpha carbon, which also carries H and the side chain R. 9: Sources: milk, butter, fish oils, eggs, green vegetables, fruits; deficiency: night blindness, dry skin, burning of the eyes.
