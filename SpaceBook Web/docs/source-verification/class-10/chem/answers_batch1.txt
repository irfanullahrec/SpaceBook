Model answer ## a. Refining of petroleum is the separation of crude oil into useful fractions by fractional distillation. Crude petroleum is a mixture of many hydrocarbons (alkanes, cycloalkanes and aromatics) with small amounts of compounds of sulphur, nitrogen and oxygen. b. The organic (biogenic) theory: it formed from the remains of marine plants and animals buried under sediments under heat and pressure over millions of years. The inorganic (abiogenic) theory: it formed from metal carbides and water deep in the earth. c. Crude oil is heated to about 400 °C and enters a fractionating tower, hotter at the bottom and cooler at the top. Fractions rise until they condense at levels where the temperature matches their boiling points: petroleum gas and LPG at the top, then petrol, naphtha, kerosene, diesel, lubricating oil and fuel oil, and bitumen (residue) at the bottom.
