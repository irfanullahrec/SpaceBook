Surface of the Earth has been covered with large landmasses and vast water bodies. Landmasses are known as continents and vast water bodies are identified as oceans and seas. 
Water covers about 71 % of total surface area of the Earth. Position of the different continents has subdivided the water covered area into five oceans. Oceans are huge bodies of saltwater surrounding the continents. Seas are water bodies smaller than oceans. 
Seas are partially enclosed margins of the oceans; a large landlocked lake can also be termed as a sea. Almost all the oceans have a number of seas along their coast lines, yet there are large water bodies found in the interiors of the continents that are termed as seas. 
1.1 Origin of Oceans and Seas 
Earth processes that formed the ocean beds stretch over long period of time. These processes involved the movement of tectonic plates. A brief history of formation of the ocean beds has been explained here. 
A supercontinent, known as Rodina existed about 600 million years ago. It split apart and oceans filled the basins. Tectonic plates collided, thrusting up mountain ranges. A new polar supercontinent, Pannotia, formed. 
A breakaway portion of Pannotia drifted north about 500 million years ago, splitting into three masses-Laurentia (North America), Baltica (Northern Europe), and Siberia. In shallow waters, the first multicellular animals with exoskeletons appeared, and the Cambrian explosion of life took off. 300 million years ago Laurentia collided with Baltica and later with Avalonia (Britain and New England). The Appalachian Mountains arose along the edge of the supercontinent, Pangea. Dinosaurs roamed Pangea about 200 million years ago, that stretched nearly from pole to pole and almost encircled Tethys, the oceanic ancestor of the Mediterranean Sea. The immense Panthalassic Ocean surrounded the supercontinent. 
About 100 million years ago Pangea broke apart. The Atlantic poured in between Africa and the Americas. India split away from Africa, and Antarctica and Australia were stranded near the South Pole.

1.6 Movements of Oceans 
Ocean water remains under different types of motions due to the influence of different types of forces. These movements can be classified as waves, tides or currents. 
i) Waves 
Waves are series of crests and troughs that are developed on the surface of water by dragging effects of winds. Waves do not displace water from one place to another, rather they are the means of moving energy through water. 
There are several types of waves such as ripples, chops, swells and tsunamis. Ripples are smallest waves and chops are a collection of small waves that make the ocean surface rough. Swells are deep water waves, that travel great distances whereas tsunami is a set of waves created by an earthquake.

Periodical rise and fall of the sea level due to the force of attraction of the Moon and the Sun is called tides. Sea level rises and falls twice in a day. This rise of sea level is termed as high tide and fall in sea level is termed as low tide. Shores of continents and islands facing the Moon and those which are opposite to it, experience high tides. Shores of continents and islands that are neither facing the Moon nor are opposite to it experience low tides. 
There are two types of tides with reference to relative positions of the Sun, Moon and Earth. 
a. Spring tides 
When the Sun, Moon and Earth are in a straight line, high tides rise to higher levels and low tides fall to lower level than normal. These higher high tides and lower low tides are called spring tides and they occur on the occasions of Full and New Moon. 
b. Neap tides 
When the Sun and Moon are at right angles with respect to the Earth, high tides do not rise so high and low tides do not fall so low. Such moderate tides are known as neap tides and they occur on 7th and 21st day of the Moon.

iii) Currents 
Ocean currents are continuous, directed movement of ocean water caused by a number of factors. Ocean currents have specific names, directions, and speed. Ocean Currents can be grouped into surface and deep currents. Surface Currents are caused by winds and their direction is modified by effects of rotation of the Earth and continental margins. Whereas deep currents are caused by salinity and temperature differences of the water. 
Surface currents are of two types, i.e., cold currents and warm currents. Cold currents flow from polar to equatorial areas. These currents move along the western coasts of the continents for example the California and Peru currents. 
Warm Currents move from equatorial to the polar areas. These currents move along the eastern margins of the continents for example the Gulf Stream and Brazil current. 
Since the Pacific and Atlantic oceans are the largest oceans of the world, their major currents are briefly described below. 
a. Currents of the Pacific Ocean 
These loops 
There are two circular loops of ocean currents in Pacific Ocean. These loops are called gyres. In Northern Pacific, currents move clockwise and in Southern Pacific, currents move anti-clockwise. The great circular loop of the Northern Pacific is formed by four currents. These are North Equatorial Current, Kuroshio or Japan Current, North Pacific Current and Californian Current. Californian Current and Oyashio Currents are also cold currents that moves towards this loop from the north. 
In South Pacific circular pattern of currents is completed by South Equatorial Current, East Australia Current, West Wind Drift and the Peruvian Current. West Wind Drift and the Peruvian current are cold currents of the South Pacific.

b. Currents of the Atlantic Ocean 
Like the Pacific Ocean, the Atlantic Ocean also has two large loops of currents. Loop of the Northern Atlantic consists of North Equatorial Current, Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift, and Canary Current. Canary Current is a cold current that brings cold polar water to the equatorial region.

2.1 Extreme Weather on the Earth 
Deviation from regular weather patterns leads to extreme weather or extreme climate. These events include unexpected, unusual, severe, or unseasonal weather. Extreme weather is a deviation of weather in terms of intensity from the range that has been seen in the past. 
Hurricanes 
Hurricanes are a type of extreme weather. Hurricanes are giant, spiraling tropical cyclones. They include strong winds of over 252 kilometers per hour. They produce heavy rains. They are caused by intense heating of sea surfaces, strong convective uplift of air and high moisture content in air.

Tornado 
Tornadoes are vertical funnels of rapidly spinning air. They are formed due to extreme low air pressure, strong convective uplift of air aided by jet streams in upper troposphere. Tip of the tornado always reaches the surface of the Earth, hence it attains its peculiar funnel shape.

Importance of Ozone Layer 
Ozone layer is present in the upper region of our atmosphere, between roughly 15 and 35 km above Earths surface and it contains relatively high concentrations of ozone molecules (0,). Its presence is essential because it protects the Earth from harmful ultraviolet radiation coming from the Sun. Ultraviolet radiations can cause the risk of skin cancer, headaches, eye diseases and other health problems. It can also damage plant life and aquatic ecosystems. In short, human existence would be difficult without the Ozone layer. Harmful compounds such as Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) from air conditioners and refrigerators and oxides of nitrogen and sulphur cause depletion of Ozone layer. This process is known as Ozone depletion. Severe depletion leads to a hole in the Ozone layer that is called ozone hole.

6.3 Economic Activities 
Economic activities determine the level of development of a country. Economic activity refers to the human activities or efforts for the purpose of livelihood and income generation. Geographers categorize human economic activities into five groups: 
1. Primary Economic Activities: 
These activities are directly related to the nature or extraction of resources of the Earth. Agriculture, livestock farming, forestry, fishing, mining are some of the important primary 
activities. 
2. Secondary Economic Activities: 
These activities use raw materials produced by primary activities and change their form into more valuable finished goods. Secondary economic activities include industry, manufacturing and construction. 
3. Tertiary Economic Activities: 
These activities provide a wide range of services to primary and secondary activities. Important tertiary activities include clerical and financial services, trade (retail and wholesale), transport and communication. 
4. Quaternary Economic Activities: 
These activities are related to special type of services and often closely linked with tertiary activities. Examples of quaternary activities include Educational Services, Health Services, Recreational Services and Information Technology. 
5. Quinary Economic Activities: 
These activities are relatively new and associated with high intellectuals. Scientists, researchers, policy makers, and top executives (highest level decision-makers) are some of the important professionals engaged in quinary activities.

6.4 Role of Inflation and Unemployment in the Development 
Cost of living increases when the inflation rate is high, which also leads to low level of development. Inflation can sometimes cause unemployment when the uncertainty of inflation leads to low investment and low economic growth in the long term. Customer purchases few goods during high unemployment, which sometimes reduces inflation. 
High unemployment rate leads to negative impact on development because unemployed people waste resources without contributing to the economy. Unemployment can cause illness, conflicts and social unrest in the country. In developed countries unemployed people put extra burden on the economy by taking benefits of joblessness. Unemployment is low in many developing countries, because jobless benefits are uncommon. Poor quality of education is one of the causes of unemployment.

A good healthcare system plays an indirect role in the development of a country in many ways. It improves productivity and increases the quality of life. It also prepares people for future. Improved health conditions can promote development. Healthier children grow up to have healthier minds with higher earning capacities.
