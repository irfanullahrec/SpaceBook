Battle of Plassey (1757) and the British Rise to Power in Bengal Battle of Plassey 1757 is an important milestone in the history of the Subcontinent. In this battle, East India Company defeated the Nawab of Bengal, Siraj- Ud-Daulah thus strengthened their position in Bengal. 
The British wanted to protect their business in India but Siraj-ud-Daulah was inclined towards the French. In this way he was a big hurdle for the British interests. This made the British think to get rid of him. The Commander in Chief of the British Army in India, Robert Clive, was aware of the superior military strength of the Nawab. For this reason, they bribed Nawab's General Mir Jaffar whose treachery helped the British to win the war against Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah.

They were not only able to take a lead against the French but were also encouraged to capture other parts of India. The historians believe that victory in the battle of Plassey paved the way of colonization for the British in the Subcontinent. 
Battle of Buxar 
During the battle of Plassey, military commander of the Siraj-Ud-Daulah's forces was Mir Jaffar who helped the British East India Company. After the defeat, Siraj-Ud-Daulah was dethroned as the Nawab of Bengal and Mir Jaffar was made the Nawab of Bengal. He was only a puppet in the hands of the British, but his rule could not last long. Soon, Mir Jaffar's son-in-law Mir Qasim was throned as the new Nawab.

Haider Ali and Tipu Sultan; Symbols of Resistance 
The real name of Tipu Sultan was Fateh Ali. He was the son of Haider Ali, the Raja of Mysore who was one of the most powerful rivals the British ever faced during their colonial presence in India. 
Like his brave father, Tipu Sultan was also trained in the art of warfare. He accompanied his father in different military campaigns. At the age of 15, Tipu Sultan took part in the First Anglo Mysore War under his father's command in 1766. 
Tipu Sultan, the Tiger of Mysore After the death of his father, Tipu Sultan became the ruler of Mysore on December 22, 1782. He was one of the few rulers who challenged British India. On resuming power, he immediately started working on devising military strategies against the British. He played a major role in keeping the British forces away. The Muslim forces won the First and Second Anglo-Mysore Wars under Haider Ali which enabled him to dictate his terms to the British in the Treaties of Madras and Mangalore. 
His determination and command of resources started shaking the foundations of the British dominion in India. While on one hand, Tipu Sultan was seeking alliance with the French and the leaders of Afghanistan and Turkey, on the other hand, East India Company was making local alliances with Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad. 
Tipu Sultan defeated the British time and again thus became a big threat for the British rule. The fourth Anglo-Mysore War proved to be decisive when British forces attacked capital Seringapatam. Tipu Sultan could not get any help from his French allies and after showing great acts of bravery, he was martyred on May 4, 1799. 
His legacy is still alive today as the life of Tipu Sultan gives message to everyone under illegal dominance living a life like a tiger and not to live like a coward.

Structure of Administration in British India 
Efforts to Co-opt Indians in the British Setup 
Though the British introduced much efficient administration, there were large gaps between the British and the Indians were treated as second-class citizens. Muslims of the Subcontinent faced more maltreatment by the hands of the British as they were considered to be the main cause of the War of Independence of 1857. In such an environment of distrust, it became necessary to include locals in the administrative setup. 
The British were aware of the fact that in the face of absolute monarchy over many centuries, the people of the Subcontinent could not have their say in the affairs of the government. Through several reforms and laws, it was ensured that representative institutions of the people are developed which could run the affairs in a better way. Developing democratic values was an important aim of those developments. Queen Victoria Proclamation 1858, Indian Councils Act 1892, Statistical Survey of India 1871, Minto-Morley Reforms, Local government setup of Lord Rippon and many other such steps were taken to ensure participation of the locals in British government of India.

British Education Policy 
Before the British, India had its own educational system. Education in the Subcontinent had always been of a classical and spiritual rather of a practical nature. Languages of instruction were Arabic, Persian and Sanskrit, which were sacred classical languages of the Muslims and the Hindus. However, under the British more emphasis was put on modern education. 
Under the British rule, educational policies in India were shaped by the colonial interests. However, during the first hundred years of the East India Company, no attention was given to education. The Charter Act of 1813 was turning point in the history of education in 
Lord Macaulay the Subcontinent. With this Act education became one of the duties of the Company. Section 43 of this Act defined the objectives of the educational policy which included 'the revival and improvement of literature', 'the encouragement of learned natives of India' and 'the introduction and promotion of a knowledge of sciences among the inhabitants of the British territories in India'. 
In 1823, General Committee of Public Instruction was formed which was to guide the East India Company. There was a clear difference of opinion among the members in deciding whether eastern education is to be preferred or the western one. This controversy continued till 1836 when Lord Macaulay presented his famous minute. Lord Macaulay's Minute (1836-37) 
On February 2, 1836 Macaulay presented his famous minute. He declared more funding for English and scientific education. He declared English as the key to success and declared Arabic, Persian and Sanskrit as inferior languages. Following words present true depiction of British education policy in India: 
&quot;We must at present do our best to form a class of persons, Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals and in intellect&quot;. 
Macaulay further expressed his preference for higher education for the upper class at the cost of ignoring elementary classes. He believed that the education from the upper class could be transmitted to lower class, but this could never happen. The result of Macaulay's minute was systematic wiping out of traditional Indian education system and sciences.

Wood's Despatch (1854) 
In order to review the education policies followed by East India Company, a special parliamentary committee was appointed to suggest educational policy for India. After a thorough evaluation, the Committee presented a policy document on education. As Charles Wood was the President of the Board of Control for India, this document was called &quot;Wood's Despatch&quot;. 
The objective of this Despatch was raising the moral character and supply of servants, in addition to producing a higher degree of intellectual fitness. The function of education was to spread European arts, philosophy, science and literature through English and other languages of India. Wood stressed on the importance of useful education for the public. 
Education under the East India Company ended with the Wood's Despatch of 1854 as the Company was abolished in 1858 and the Government of India came directly under the British government. In 1882, Hunter Commission was formed to evaluate the achievements of Wood Despatch of 1854. This commission underlined the state's role in the improvement of elementary and secondary education. It also highlighted the transfer of control to district boards and recommended two divisions of secondary education up to university and vocational for commercial career.

Muslims after the Decline of the Mughal Empire 
After the death of Aurangzeb Alamgir in 1707 AD, the Mughal Empire was facing decline in both power and authority. There were numerous causes behind the lost glory of the Mughals such as economic bankruptcy, loss of nobility, weak rulers, rebellions and invasions by the foreign rulers. Moreover, the Mughal army lost its capacity to fight because of obsolete weapons and constant infighting. The Indians were becoming increasingly concerned about preserving their faiths, culture, traditions, and society. 
Muslim Scholars in Subcontinent 
The efforts for the Muslim awakening and impact of influential Muslim scholars started to appear at the horizon of Subcontinent in the 17th century during the reign of Mughal emperor Akbar. Sheikh Ahmad Sarhindi-Mujaddid Alf Sani, Shah Wali Ullah, Syed Ahmad Shaheed Bareilvi and Haji Shariat Ullah were recognized for their prolific services for the regeneration of Islamic society in the history of the Muslims of Subcontinent. Sheikh Ahmad Sarhindi - Mujaddid Alf Sani (1564-1624) 
Introduction 
Sheikh Ahmad Sarhindi al-Farooqi an-Naqshbandi was born on 26 June 1564. His father Sheikh Abdul Ahad was a prominent Muslim Sufi scholar of his time. Sheikh Ahmed obtained his basic education in Quran, Hadith, Tafseer, Philosophy and theology from Sarhind. During this time, Islamic teachings were in decline because Muslim scholars and Ulama neglected the necessity of the actual teachings of Quran and Sunnah. He wrote many books and described the vitality of Quran and Prophethood for the Muslims and the humanity. 
Main Contributions 
Sheikh Ahmed Sarhindi quickly rose to the pinnacle of experience and vision and made extraordinary contributions to revival of Islam. Some of his important services are outlined as follows; 
i. Fight Against Deen-e-llahi 
Mughal Emperor Akbar presented Deen-e-llahi that was combination of the teachings of Hindu and Muslim beliefs and doctrines of some local religions. Sheikh Ahmed Sarhindi described it as an attack on Islam and vowed to eradicate the ambiguous religious teachings from daily lives of Muslims. His efforts became successful when Emperor Jahangir canceled all those orders of his father Akbar which were against the teachings of Islam and hence, the infamous Deen-e-llahi came to an end.

Introduction 
lab.com 
Shah Wali Ullah was born on 21 February 1703. His real name was Qutub-ud-din but later on he was known as Shah Wali Ullah due to his piety. His father, Shah Abdul Rahim, a well-known religious scholar, established Madrasah Rahimiya in Delhi. Shah Wali Ullah also taught in Madrasah Rahimiya and became a role model for his students. In 1724, during his visit to Hejaz (Saudi Arabia), Shah Wali Ullah received further education from Sheikh Abu Tahir bin Ibrahim. 
After the death of Aurangzeb Alamgir, Mughal Empire was declining. The Muslims were disunited and their religion was vulnerable to attacks. According to Shah Wali Ullah, these issues were partly the result of incomplete knowledge of Quran and Hadith. He also realized that the Muslim community needed strong leadership particularly in Delhi. He wanted Muslims to follow Islamic morals and spiritual teachings in their social, economic and political lives. 
Main Contributions 
i. Translation of the Holy Quran 
Shah Wali Ullah desired that Muslims should understand the teachings of Islam. Hence he translated Holy Quran in Persian language because there were very less people who could speak Arabic. Later on, his two sons Shah Abdul Qadir and Shah Rafi translated the Holy Quran in Urdu langauge.

ii. Scholarly Works 
Shah Wali Ullah promoted his message through his writings and teachings. He wrote fifty-one books in Persian and Arabic. Among his writings, Hujjat Allah al- Baligha and Izalat al-Khafa'an Khilafat al-Khulfa were important contributions. 
Facts to Know

iii. Opposition to Marathas and Restoration of Mughal Empire 
ก 
One of the greatest contributions of Shah Wali Ullah was his organization of opposition against the growing power of Marathas. They were threatening to suppress the Mughal Empire. Shah Wali Ullah bridged the gap between various Muslim sects through his writings and speeches. This book was highly acclaimed as the main source of political thought of Shah Wali Ullah. 
Shah Wali Ullah's ideas motivated the Muslims in times of despair. The influence of his teachings lasted for long even after his death. His followers kept promoting his ideas and teachings including his sons Shah Abdul Aziz, Shah Abdul Qadir and Shah Rafiuddin. Many supporters of the Pakistan movement believed that they were building on the idea of &quot;two nations&quot; posited in Shah Wali Ullah's works.

Constitutional Amendments 
Constitution of Pakistan provides solution to almost all the issues related to governance. However, in later years, different constitutional amendments were made in the constitution. Through these amendments different articles of the constitutions were amended or new articles were introduced. 
Through eighth amendment in 1985 the President of Pakistan was given authority to dissolve the assemblies. This amendment was introduced in 1985 by the assembly elected through non-party elections. This power of dissolution of assemblies by the President and the provincial governors was taken back through thirteenth amendment in 1997. This power of dissolution of assemblies by the President was restored again through seventeenth amendment in 2003. It empowered the president once again. Eighteenth Constitutional amendment in 2010 was aimed at bringing back the parliamentary supremacy. It gave more powers to the provinces to utilize their resources. In addition this amendment ensured judicial autonomy, removed discretionary powers of the President of Pakistan and improved the situation of women empowerment through introduction of 5% quota for women in election tickets by the political parties.

The Parliament 
The Parliament of Pakistan is the law-making body of Pakistan. It consists of two houses: Senate as the upper house and National Assembly as the lower house. The President of Pakistan is also a part of the Parliament.

National Assembly consists of 342 members. 272 are elected by direct voting, 60 seats are reserved for women while 10 seats are for religious minorities. The members of the National Assembly are elected for a five-year term. These members make laws for federal government. Different issues are discussed through debates in the parliament, the bills are passed which are then sent to the Senate for approval. If the bill is passed from the other house too, it is sent to the President for approval in order to make it a law. 
Members of the parliament raise different questions of public interest and ensure that the government functions according to the constitution. 
Senate of Pakistan has the equal representation from all federating units of Pakistan. It consists of total 100 members. On general seats, from each province 14 members, from Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) 4 members, and from Islamabad 2 members are elected. The members of the Senate are elected for a term of 6 years. Since the membership of the National Assembly was based on the population of each province, equal representation to all the federating units is very important for developing harmony among all the provinces.

South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) is a regional organization of eight countries. It was established on 8th December 1985 for cooperation and friendship among the developing member countries. SAARC is the largest regional organization covering more than 2 billion population. Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka are the founding members of the organization while Afghanistan joined this organization as eighth member in 2007. 
Pakistan has been playing an important role in SAARC. Pakistan hosted 4th Summit of SAARC in 1988 and 12th summit in 2004 in Islamabad. Pakistan is the second largest contributor towards all SAARC related activities and budget. Pakistan believes in peace and stability in the region. Pakistan has also expressed its support for a concerted SAARC strategy on the issue of drug abuse. 
Pakistan and Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) 
Shanghai Cooperation organization was founded on 15 June 2001 in Shanghai, China by the leaders of China, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Russia, Tajikistan, and Uzbekistan. Pakistan and India joined SCO as full members on 9 June 2017 at a summit in Astana, Kazakhstan. Important activities of this organization include cooperation on security, economic collaboration and cultural cooperation. In the last few years, the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) has emerged as an important actor on the international platform. Pakistan's inclusion in this organization in 2017 provided it an opportunity to improve its relations with the regional countries by facilitating trade through the Gwadar port. It also provided a chance for a regional solution to the Afghanistan issue and addressing the energy crisis of the country.
