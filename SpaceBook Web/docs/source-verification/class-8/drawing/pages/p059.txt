q 1 | Fill in the blanks.
qp i | Arrangement of art elements is called ______________.
sol composition ## Composition is the arrangement of art elements such as line, shape, volume, colour, value and texture according to the principles of art.
qp ii | In art balance means ______________ balance.
sol visual ## Balance in art means visual balance: arranging the art elements so that the picture gives a sense of balance.
qp iii | Arrangement of different objects which give the impression of oneness is called ______________.
sol harmony ## Harmony is the combined effect of similar or related art elements that gives visual satisfaction; each landscape has its own strong harmony or oneness.
qp iv | The use of complementary colours in an artwork is called ______________.
sol contrast ## Contrast is often created by using complementary colours (such as red and green) or extremely light and dark tones.
qp v | Red, yellow and blue are ______________ colours.
sol primary ## The three basic colours red, yellow and blue are called primary colours; all other colours are made by mixing them.
q 2 | Answer the following questions.
qp i | Explain composition.
sol Composition is the arrangement of art elements such as line, shape, volume, colour, value and texture according to the principles of art (movement, harmony, variety, balance, rhythm, emphasis and contrast). A good composition makes the artwork interesting and pleasing. ## Definition from the book.
qp ii | What do you mean by the terms light, shade and shadow?
sol Light creates different tones and tints on an object. The area directly hit by the light glows almost white and is called the highlight; the back side of the object looks darker and is called the body shadow (shade); between them is the body tone. The shadow that an object casts on a surface or another object is called the cast shadow. Correct placing of these tones gives the illusion of volume. ## From the study of the sphere in the book.
qp iii | Define texture. What are the main types of texture?
sol Texture is the tactile quality of a surface: its smoothness or roughness (for example tree bark, rock, a wall or glass). The main types are: 1. real or tactile texture, which is felt by the sense of touch, and 2. visual texture, which is only seen and is made to look like texture with lines, tones and colours in a drawing or painting. ## Types of texture.
