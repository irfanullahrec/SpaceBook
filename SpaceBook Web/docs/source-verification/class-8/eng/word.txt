419: Hazrat Muhammad Rasoolullah
420: abided by Allah's) commands, even before he was entrusted with the duty of nabuwwat, justice was instilled in him as a result of divine training. From the time of his childhood, Hazrat Muhammad Rasoolullah remained outside the moral degeneration of the societies in the Age of Ignorance. Het opposed oppression and injustice in his youth, and he joined the pact of social justice, Hillul-Fudul an association, formed for fair commercial dealing in pre- Islamic Makkah. "Hulf means oath whereas Fudul" means people with virtue. Before Islam, justice system was not based on a sound foundation among the Arabs. There was no written law to be taken as a basis for implementation, therefore, resolving conflicts was left to the discretion of judges. However, the decision was not absolute, because the powerful might not conform to judgments against them. For this reason, the weak were usually found guilty. Hazrat Muhammad Rasoolullah based the Justice system on the principle of giving a person his/her due; no matter whatever his/her stature may be; dominant or oppressed. During Hazrat Muhammad Rasoolullah time, a cottage was built on a piece of land belonging to two brothers. The heirs disputed the ownership of the cottage. They went to the Rasool to resolve the issue.The beloved Rasool assigned Hazrat Huzaifa Bin Yaman () to handle the case. Hazrat Huzaifa (0752 ) went to the site of the cottage for investigation. He

1256: Deforestation
1257: Deforestation is the destruction of native forests and woods on a long-term basis. It is a very severe problem with several consequences for the ecosystem. It happens when humans cut trees for their own needs. Even if it is to meet human requirements, it cannot be justified because it poses a significant threat to the environment's biodiversity. Biodiversity is critical to the ecosystem's overall health. Forests are necessary for maintaining and sustaining global ecosystems, so we must safeguard them. Many living species rely on forests for their survival. They provide humans with a wide range of gifts that improve our quality of life. They take CO, from the atmosphere and generate oxygen, which is required by living creatures. Natural phenomena also account for deforestation. Volcanic eruptions, for example, can burn away the woodlands surrounding the volcano. Some other natural phenomena of deforestation are storms, floods, attack of parasites on plants, and lightening etc.
1258: Agriculture and cattle grazing are two of the primary causes of deforestation. To meet the ever increasing demand for food, forest area is regularly destroyed and converted to agricultural land (which can, in turn, be linked to overpopulation). Palm oil, lumber, soy, and cattle are the four key commodities responsible for tropical deforestation. Another major driver of deforestation is the creation of grazing land for livestock. Forests act as carbon sinks by absorbing atmospheric CO, during photosynthesis. Because carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas, deforestation contributes directly to the greenhouse effect and (as a result) global warming. Tree roots tend to attach to the soil bedrock, strengthening the soil. Additionally, the plant Itter produced by trees protects the soil's surface. When trees are removed (due to deforestation), the soil becomes prone to erosion. Landslides are frequently associated with sloped terrain deforestation, which can be explained by the loss of soil adhesion caused by the absence of trees. Natural disasters, such as floods, amplify the extent of erosion (note that the plant Ifter found on forest surfaces helps reduce the amount of soil washed away).
1259: Forests are home to a diverse range of species. Tropical rainforests are thought to have the most diverse ecosystems in the world. Deforestation is a significant danger to this biodiversity. Locally, forest area removal might lead to a reduction in the number of certain species. However, on a global basis, deforestation can lead to the extinction of some desirable species.
