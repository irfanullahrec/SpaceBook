She went on telling, I went on writing. Many things came to her lips but she could not say them out of modesty. At last I could not restrain myself and I explained to her: God has sent down your livelihood in a foreign land, and the foreign land not for a month or two but for the whole life. Why then do you not learn to write yourself? Then she said with great longing: How can it be that my age is for learning to write? In the bustle of the children fifteen fifteen days pass that the turn to wash the head does not come. I read the Quran in childhood; thanks be, by the blessing of the teacher I did not forget it, but if I leave it for even a month the whole Quran becomes blank. Hearing this I said: when you have the Quran memorised, learning to write is no great matter. Surely you read it every day. She said: yes, somewhat just stammering and often words are left out; but printed I can make out fairly well. I said: then you do not even need a teacher. By copying you will come to write. In her heart the lady accepted my words but said: I feel a sort of shame. Then I took her roundly to task that, while carrying your need to others, flattering others, laying out your circumstances before others imploring, you feel no shame, and feel shame at learning to write. Is writing some fault or sin?
