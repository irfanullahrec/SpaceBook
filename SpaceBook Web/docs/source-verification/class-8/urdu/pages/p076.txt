Preparing gradually wastes time needlessly and worry is stretched for free. Our rule was that on arriving we would say at once that this year at least we could not pass. The parents often did not believe it. On such occasions the mind is greatly perplexed. I know well what I have come away having written in the paper. I know well that unless the examiners look at the papers in a state of intoxication my passing is quite impossible. I want all my well-wishers also to become sure of this so that they are not shocked in time. But the well-wishers take all my explanations as mere modesty. In the last years my father would believe at once because experience had proved to him that my estimate was not wrong, but the people around, with phrases such as "Oh no, sir", "Oh, what are you saying?", "Oh, is that any sort of thing?" would drive me up the wall. Anyhow this time too, as soon as we reached home we, as usual, made the prediction of our failing.
