Importance of Freedom of Speech 
According to human rights, every human being has the right to freedom of expression. The article 19 of Universal Declaration of Human Rights 1948 declares, "Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression. This right includes freedom to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive and impart information and ideas through any media and regardless of frontiers". 
The theory of freedom of expression was first presented by a British Liberal thinker Jhon Stuart Mill in 19th Century. He said that the only way to social development is to let human beings express themselves freely. Freedom of expression plays an important role in the formation of a diverse society. It creates harmony and tolerance in society. Besides, it promotes democratic system in a country. It is helpful in eradicating social evils. No social evil can be eradicated until people speak against it. In freedom of expression, it is necessary not to hurt feelings of others.

Nationalism 
Nation is derived from a Latin word 'Natio' which means birth. It means that nation is related to the birth or race of the individual. Nationalism is actually the name of such a feeling and spirit which is created among individuals based on race, colour, religion, region, language and customs. Because of this spirit, people consider themselves a nation. We are Pakistani nation and we are proud to be Paksitanis. As Pakistanis, we live in love, peace and brotherhood. To promote the spirit of nationalism, we must take care of each other's economic and social rights.

Federal and provincial governments are formed after general elections, which are held after every five years. According to the constitution of Pakistan Federal and Provincial governments have delegated powers. All federal developmental programmes are finalized according to the policies of parliament. Federal and provincial governments work together with coordination.

Provincial governments work for the welfare of their respective provinces whereas local government controls matters at district level. Provincial governments are autonomous in some matters but some matters are settled by both the government for example distribution of national resources for national and provincial developmental works are decided by both the governments.
