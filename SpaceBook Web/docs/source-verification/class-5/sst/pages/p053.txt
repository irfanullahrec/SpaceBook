p A meeting of the provincial Muslim League of Sindh was held in Karachi in 1938, presided over by Quaid-e-Azam. A resolution was passed in this meeting for the establishment of an independent state for Muslims; Sindh was the first province which passed such a resolution. This resolution was presented by Sheikh Abdul Majeed Sindhi. Prominent leaders of Sindh were Quaid-e-Azam, Fatima Jinnah, Begum Abdullah Haroon, Sir Abdullah Haroon, Begum Rana Liaquat Ali Khan, G. Allana and Muhammad Ayub Khoro. Students, religious leaders and traders also played an important role, along with political leaders, in creating awareness among the people of Sindh for the creation of an independent Muslim state. Students of the Sindh Muslim Student Federation and Sindh Madrassatul Islam actively participated in the Pakistan Movement. The people of Sindh supported the Pakistan Resolution. p The province of Punjab has remained a centre of political activities. Sir Mian Muhammad Shafi established the Punjab Muslim League in 1906 to unite the Muslims of Punjab. In the politics of Punjab, Dr. Allama Muhammad Iqbal, Sir Mian Muhammad Shafi and Malik Barkat Ali played an important role. p In 1930, Allama Iqbal proposed the division of the subcontinent on the basis of the Two Nation Theory in his address at Allahabad. Quaid-e-Azam came back from England at the request of Muslim leaders and took the responsibility of leading them. The annual session of the All India Muslim League was held at Minto Park, Lahore, on 23rd March 1940. A resolution was presented in this meeting for the establishment of a separate state for the Muslims; it was titled the "Pakistan Resolution". The students of Punjab did exemplary work for the creation of Pakistan; the Punjab Muslim Students Federation spread the message of Quaid-e-Azam to the cities and towns of Punjab.
