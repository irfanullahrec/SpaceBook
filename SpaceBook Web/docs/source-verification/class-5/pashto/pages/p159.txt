But the other languages of India strongly influenced it, and original, classical Sanskrit was gradually being forgotten; yet the language held a great store of learning — many books on religion, literature and other subjects. So some scholars worked to make learning it easier and to explain it correctly; among them, a scholar from the village of Lahor in Swabi, Panini, came forward and compiled, for the first time, a grammar of Sanskrit — its morphology and syntax — called the "Ashtadhyayi".
