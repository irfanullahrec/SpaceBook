The British felt their prestige was at stake and blamed the Afridi tribes for the theft. They called a jirga of tribal elders and demanded that the culprits be found and handed over. Tribesmen do not hide secrets at such times — every tribe and clan admits what its people have done — but on this occasion no clan accepted responsibility, calling it the private act of a few men. In those days Ajab Khan of the Basti Khel tribe and his brother Shahzada Khan were famous, so the British blamed them for the theft too.
